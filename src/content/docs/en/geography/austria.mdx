Austria is a landlocked country in Central Europe with an area of about **83,880 km²** and about **9.2 million** inhabitants. Its natural regions range from the high mountains of the Alps to the Pannonian Plain at Lake Neusiedl. This diversity in a small space shapes settlement, transport, agriculture and tourism. The political system (federal state, constitution, parliament) is covered in the section [History and Civic Education](/en/history-and-politics/austrian-political-system/).

## Location and borders

- Austria extends about 575 km from west to east (from Lake Constance to the March), but only about 295 km from north to south. The narrowest part is in Tyrol.
- It borders **eight states**: Germany, Czechia, Slovakia, Hungary, Slovenia, Italy, Switzerland and Liechtenstein.
- The highest point is the **Grossglockner** (3798 m), the lowest point the Hedwighof near Apetlon in the Seewinkel (about 114 m).
- Population density is about 110 inhabitants per km². Because a large part of the area is mountainous, however, only about a third of the area is available as **permanent settlement area** (settlement, transport, agriculture), in Tyrol only about an eighth. The actual density there is correspondingly higher.

## Major landscape regions

| Landscape region | Location | Characteristics and use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Eastern Alps** | almost two thirds of the national territory, from Vorarlberg to the Vienna Woods | Northern Limestone Alps, Central Alps, Southern Limestone Alps (see [mountain building](/en/geography/earth-structure-and-plate-tectonics/#mountain-building-the-alps)); longitudinal valleys (Inn, Salzach, Enns, Mur, Drava) as settlement and transport axes; grassland, forestry, tourism, hydropower |
| **Alpine and Carpathian foreland** | north of the Alps in Upper and Lower Austria, continuing in the Weinviertel | hill country of deposits; fertile soils, arable farming, dense settlement, industry (Linz, Wels, St. Pölten), main transport axis of the Western Railway and West Motorway |
| **Granite and gneiss plateau** (Bohemian Massif) | Mühlviertel and Waldviertel north of the Danube | Austria's oldest mountains, an eroded plateau of granite and gneiss; harsh climate, poor soils, forestry, out-migration |
| **Vienna Basin** | between the eastern edge of the Alps and the Carpathians | subsidence basin, oil and gas deposits; Vienna, Industrieviertel, Thermenlinie with viticulture |
| **Pannonian lowlands and hills** | Burgenland, eastern Lower Austria, eastern Styria | continental Pannonian climate (hot, dry summers); viticulture, vegetables, Lake Neusiedl (steppe lake with a reed belt, UNESCO World Heritage site together with Hungary) |

The most important rivers are the Danube with its tributaries Inn, Traun, Enns, Ybbs, Kamp and March; the Mur and Drava only flow into the Danube outside Austria (via Slovenia and Croatia). Austria has numerous lakes, most of them of glacial origin. The largest is Lake Neusiedl, whose southern part belongs to Hungary; the largest lake lying entirely in Austria is the Attersee.

Austria is one of the wealthiest states in the EU. Typical features are a strong base of small and medium-sized enterprises with many family businesses, including numerous specialised world market leaders in niches ("hidden champions"), a high export ratio, the social partnership between employers' and employees' organisations and a large tourism sector. Economic strength is unevenly distributed in space: Vienna, Salzburg, Upper Austria and Vorarlberg are above average, Burgenland and Carinthia below.

## Infrastructure

### Transport

Austria is a **transit country** between northern and southern Europe (Brenner, Tauern, Pyhrn) and between western and eastern Europe (Danube axis). The Alpine region forces traffic into a few valleys and passes, which increases noise and air pollution there.

- **Road**: the high-level network of motorways and expressways is built and operated by the state-owned **ASFINAG**, financed by the vignette and tolls.
- **Rail**: **ÖBB** operates the main network. Major projects are the **Koralm Railway** (in operation since December 2025, Graz to Klagenfurt in about 41 minutes), the **Semmering Base Tunnel** and the **Brenner Base Tunnel**, which together with the upgrading of the Western Railway are parts of European corridors (TEN-T).
- **Waterway**: the **Danube** is connected to the Rhine and the North Sea via the Main-Danube Canal (1992); the most important port is Linz.
- **Air transport**: **Vienna-Schwechat** airport is the country's hub, plus regional airports in Graz, Linz, Salzburg, Innsbruck and Klagenfurt.

### Energy and communication

- **Electricity**: run-of-river and storage power stations on the Danube, the Inn and in the Alps, increasingly wind (Burgenland, Lower Austria) and photovoltaics. Pumped-storage power stations in the Alps (for example Kaprun, Malta) store energy and balance fluctuations in the European grid.
- **Natural gas**: **Baumgarten an der March** is an important gas hub in Central Europe. Since deliveries from Russia via Ukraine ended at the start of 2025, Austria has been obtaining natural gas via other routes, mainly via Germany and Italy.
- **Oil**: the Transalpine Pipeline (TAL) from Trieste with its branch, the Adriatic-Vienna Pipeline, supplies the Schwechat refinery.
- **Broadband**: the federal **Broadband Strategy 2030** aims to provide gigabit-capable connections throughout Austria by 2030. Rural regions still lag behind in fibre roll-out, which is a locational disadvantage for businesses and working from home.

## Austria in Europe

- **EU member since 1995**, part of the single market, the **Schengen area** and since 1999 (cash since 2002) the **euro area**.
- Since 1955 Austria has been **permanently neutral**; it is not a member of NATO but takes part in the EU's Common Security and Defence Policy.
- **Vienna** is one of the four headquarters of the United Nations (Vienna International Centre with the IAEA, UNIDO and UNODC) and seat of the OSCE and OPEC.
- EU eastern enlargement in 2004 moved Austria from a peripheral location on the border of the former Eastern bloc to a **central location**. Austrian banks, insurers, retailers and industrial companies are among the largest investors in Central and Eastern Europe.
- Austria is a member of the **EU Strategy for the Danube Region** and the **EU Strategy for the Alpine Region** and of the **Alpine Convention** (convention on the protection of the Alps, in force since 1995).
- In EU regional policy, Burgenland was the only Austrian region to be an Objective 1 area from 1995 to 2006, that is a region lagging behind in development, and received particularly high funding (see [Europe](/en/geography/europe/#eu-regional-policy)).
