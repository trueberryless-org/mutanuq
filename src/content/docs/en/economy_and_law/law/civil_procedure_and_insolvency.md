## Civil proceedings

If a dispute between private persons or companies cannot be settled out of court, a court decides in a **civil lawsuit**. The person who sues is the plaintiff, the person being sued is the defendant.

### Jurisdiction and court hierarchy

| Instance   | Court                                   | responsible for                                                  |
| ---------- | --------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1st instance | District court                    | amounts in dispute up to €15,000 and certain matters regardless of the amount (e.g. tenancy law, family law) |
| 1st instance | Regional court                    | amounts in dispute over €15,000                                  |
| 2nd instance | regional court or higher regional court | appeals against judgements of the first instance           |
| 3rd instance | Supreme Court                     | appeals on points of law of considerable importance              |

Employment disputes are heard by the **labour and social courts**. In Vienna, there is a separate commercial court for business matters.

From a certain amount in dispute (at district courts from €5,000) and at the higher courts, parties must be **represented by a lawyer**.

### Course of a civil lawsuit

1. **Statement of claim:** The plaintiff files a lawsuit with the competent court.
2. **Defence:** The defendant can respond within a deadline.
3. **Hearing:** The court first tries to reach a settlement. If that fails, evidence is taken (documents, witnesses, experts, questioning of the parties).
4. **Judgement:** The court decides. The losing party generally has to pay the legal costs of both sides.
5. **Appeal:** An appeal against the judgement can be lodged within four weeks.

A final judgement is an **enforceable title**.

### Payment order procedure

For **monetary claims up to €75,000**, the courts automatically carry out a simplified payment order procedure:

1. The creditor files a lawsuit (also electronically).
2. Without a hearing, the court issues a payment order and serves it on the debtor.
3. If the debtor does not lodge an objection within four weeks, the payment order becomes final and enforceable.
4. If they object, normal proceedings take place.

This allows undisputed claims to be enforced quickly and at low cost.

## Enforcement

If the debtor still does not pay despite a final enforceable title, the creditor can apply to the district court for **enforcement**. Possible measures are

- seizure of movable property by the bailiff,
- **wage garnishment:** part of the salary is paid directly by the employer to the creditor, while the debtor keeps a subsistence minimum,
- garnishment of bank accounts and other claims,
- compulsory auction or compulsory administration of real estate.

## Insolvency proceedings

If a debtor can no longer pay their debts, **insolvency proceedings** are opened. They aim to satisfy the creditors equally and, if possible, to save the company. The rules are laid down in the Insolvency Code.

### Grounds for insolvency

- **Inability to pay:** The debtor can no longer pay their debts that are due, and not just temporarily. This ground applies to all debtors.
- **Over-indebtedness:** The assets no longer cover the debts, and there is no positive going-concern forecast. This ground only applies to corporations and other legal persons.

If a ground for insolvency occurs, the managing director of a limited company must file for insolvency **without culpable delay**, at the latest within 60 days. Otherwise, they are personally liable and may be committing a criminal offence. Creditors can also file an application.

### Types of insolvency proceedings

| Proceedings                      | Aim                                                         |
| -------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Restructuring with self-administration | continuing the business; the debtor manages it under the supervision of a restructuring administrator; creditors receive at least 30 % of their claims within 2 years |
| Restructuring without self-administration | continuing the business under an insolvency administrator; minimum quota 20 % |
| Bankruptcy proceedings       | selling the assets and distributing the proceeds to the creditors, usually closing the business |
| Debt settlement proceedings (personal insolvency) | for private persons; agreement with the creditors on a payment plan or a skimming procedure |

In **bankruptcy proceedings**, an insolvency administrator appointed by the court manages the assets. The creditors register their claims and receive a quota at the end, often only a small percentage.

### Personal insolvency

In **debt settlement proceedings**, the debtor first offers their creditors a payment plan. If the majority agrees, the debtor is released from the remaining debts once the plan has been fulfilled. Otherwise, the court can initiate a skimming procedure: for several years, the debtor has to hand over the garnishable part of their income and is then released from the remaining debts (discharge of residual debt).

:::tip[Tip]
If you are over-indebted, state-recognised **debt counselling services** help free of charge.
:::
