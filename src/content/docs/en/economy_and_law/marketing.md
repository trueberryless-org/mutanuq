Marketing presents products and services as attractively as possible to the customer. But marketing is not just an advertising strategy; rather, it represents the **mindset** of the company and puts the customer at the centre of all business activities. With marketing activities, companies above all want to better understand the needs of customers and fulfil them as well as possible. This is intended to achieve customer satisfaction and customer loyalty.

## Marketing concept

- defining the targeted goals
- choosing strategies for achieving them
- selecting adequate marketing instruments

To be able to use marketing better, **market research** is carried out.

## Marketing goals

There are two different kinds of marketing goals: market-economic (also primary) and market-psychological goals.

### Market-economic goals

- turnover
- market share
  - absolute: own turnover in relation to the turnover of the entire market
  - relative: own market share in relation to the market share of the strongest competitor
- contribution margin
  - revenue - variable costs
  - in the long run, also covering fixed costs
- advertising return (additional profit from an advertising measure in relation to its costs)

### Market-psychological goals

- level of awareness
- image (of the company / brand)
- customer satisfaction
- brand loyalty of customers
- ...

## Marketing strategies

Marketing strategies define the measures necessary in the long term to ensure that all instruments are used purposefully.

### Segmentation

When analysing the market, you cannot consider all target groups at once, since different groups of people or other companies buy different products and have different interests. For this reason, you should find out which target group is the buyer of a particular product. Even the best and most creative marketing concepts cannot be successful if they are not tailored to a target group.

#### Analysis process

First, the overall market is divided into different sub-markets (segments) based on certain criteria. Then it is decided whether one or several of these segments should be targeted, and finally the customer benefit is addressed specifically for this segment / these segments so that customers are convinced of the company's performance.

#### Possible segmentation criteria

- geographic (federal states, cities, domestic, ...)
- demographic (age, gender, marital status, income, ...)
- psychographic (lifestyle, personality, interests, ...)
- behavioural (occasion of purchase, frequency of use, ...)

#### Type of marketing

##### Concentrated marketing

Marketing is focused on one or a few specific segments. This variant is often used, especially by smaller companies, since marketing can otherwise become quite expensive.

##### Differentiated marketing

Here, each individual target group gets its own marketing.

##### Undifferentiated marketing

The company runs one marketing approach for all customers.

#### Advantage

By dividing the market into groups of buyers with similar characteristics, marketing instruments can be adapted to the specific customer wishes and expectations.

#### Market positioning

A significant customer benefit is addressed in order to convince customers of the offering. The company wants to set itself apart from the competition **positively** in the long term in order to obtain a unique selling proposition (`USP`).

## Instruments

Marketing instruments are specific measures and means for shaping activities on the market in a targeted way. Classical marketing theory distinguishes four different instruments (`4Ps`). They can be used best when they are used together. The combination of the individual marketing instruments is called the **marketing mix**.

Other marketing theories describe a total of `7Ps`, which is why all of them are listed here.

### Product and range policy (product)

- What can the product do?
- Can a product bundle (e.g. phone + case) be marketed?
- What do packaging, brand, warranty and service look like?
- How broad (number of product groups) and how deep (variants per group) should the range be?

### Pricing policy / price and conditions (price)

- How much does the product cost?
- Which payment terms (cash discount, rebate, ...) can we offer?
- Which pricing strategy is pursued?
  - **Premium pricing**: high quality and exclusive image, e.g. luxury brands
  - **Low-price strategy**: quantity over quality, e.g. discount stores
  - **Penetration pricing**: low introductory price to gain market share quickly, followed by a price increase
  - **Skimming**: high introductory price that is gradually lowered, e.g. for new smartphones

### Distribution policy (place)

- How do the goods get from me to the customer?
- **Direct sales**: without intermediaries, e.g. own online shop or own stores
- **Indirect sales**: via wholesale and retail
- Where should warehouses and points of sale be located, and how is delivery organised (logistics)?

### Communication policy (promotion)

- How is information exchanged between the company and customers?
- **Advertising**: paid messages in media such as TV, radio, posters or social media
- **Sales promotion**: short-term purchase incentives such as vouchers, competitions or samples
- **Public relations (PR)**: building a positive image, e.g. through press releases or sponsoring
- **Personal selling**: direct sales conversation with the customer

### Personnel policy (people)

- services are judged by the staff
- employee satisfaction affects customer satisfaction

### Process management (process)

- making services tangible, designing processes in a customer-oriented way
- only satisfied customers speak positively about the offering and recommend the service to others.

### Physical environment policy (physical evidence)

- optimising the physical environment of the point of sale
- the furnishing, type, location, available space and quality of the sales room influence customer satisfaction

### **Marketing mix**

> A targeted combination of all marketing instruments, which must be coordinated with each other. A luxury product, for example, does not fit a discount price and distribution via a discount store.

## Market research

Market research is intended to help get to know the market better and to use the instruments better.

Market research is the systematic

- collection
- preparation
- analysis
- interpretation

of information about markets using suitable **methods and techniques.**

### Tasks

- market position of the company
- general market characteristics and developments
- customer segments
- customer behaviour and needs
- customer satisfaction and loyalty
- competitors

---

The results of market research provide information about current events (market diagnosis) and future developments of the market (market forecast).

### Methods

#### Secondary market research

Here, already existing data is evaluated.

- **Internal sources of information**
  - field sales reports
  - customer statistics
  - accounting documents
  - sales statistics
  - previous market research studies
- **External sources of information**
  - internet
  - official statistics
  - external databases
  - publications
  - publications by other companies or institutions

If the problem cannot be solved this way, primary market research must be carried out.

#### Primary market research

When data is collected on the market for the first time for a specific purpose, this is called **primary market research**.

##### Data collection methods

- Survey: asking targeted questions (in person / by phone / in writing / computer-assisted)

- Observation: systematically recording behaviour; in person (people); by devices (cameras); online (social networks)

- Experiment: defined, controlled conditions in which participants are observed and/or interviewed afterwards

  - Product test: adapting the product to the needs
  - Store test: testing the sales figures of different versions of a product
  - Market test: first selling the new product together with its marketing mix only in a limited test area

  ***

  - Field experiment: natural conditions
  - Laboratory experiment: artificial conditions

### Customer- and market-oriented product design

Products should meet or even exceed the needs and expectations of the customer in order to create enthusiasm.

#### Benefits of a product

- Core benefit
  - what the customer basically expects from the product
- Additional benefit
  - everything that exceeds the core benefit -\> e.g. additional functions
- Additional services
  - services offered in addition to a product

### Product analysis

For companies, it is important not only to make a profit at the moment, but also to be able to make a steady profit in the long term. Due to the [product life cycle](#product-life-cycle), however, products are not always profitable in the long term. That is why companies are constantly looking for new innovative products that could be successful later.

**Product life cycle analysis** and **portfolio analysis** are two of many very helpful methods that make product analysis easier.

#### Product life cycle

The product life cycle assumes that products, like living beings, are basically subject to a life cycle. The lifespan of products is limited by:

- technical progress
- changes in consumer behaviour
- short-lived trends

The life cycle can refer to individual products, product groups or even entire industries.

![Product life cycle](/images/economy_and_law/product_lifecycle.png)

The following phases are distinguished:

- **Introduction phase** / **relaunch**  
  The product is newly launched on the market and still generates little revenue, usually even losses, since high costs for development and advertising are incurred. If the company invests in the product and turns potential customers into customers through marketing, the product can bring the company a lot of profit.

- **Growth phase**  
  Turnover rises continuously and the product is well received by buyers. Advertising measures support the growth phase of the product.

- **Maturity phase**  
  The growth in turnover slows down, but people still keep buying the product steadily and bring the company a lot of profit. At this point, the company has to spend less capital on marketing, since customers already know the product and buy it anyway.

- **Saturation phase**  
  The market is saturated, i.e. many people already own the product or no longer need it as much. An increase in turnover is no longer possible and turnover declines.

- **Decline phase**  
  Turnover keeps falling; a relaunch can be attempted or the product can be taken off the market through elimination.

- **Elimination**  
  Nobody needs the product anymore. The product is no longer manufactured.

#### Portfolio analysis

The portfolio analysis (also `BCG portfolio`) classifies the products in a 4-field matrix with the axes `market growth` and `relative market share` (relative to the largest competitor). In this context, a `relative market share` of 1 means that the company is on a par with the strongest competitor.

![Portfolio analysis](/images/economy_and_law/portfolioanalyse.png)

Each of the four quadrants has a different meaning for the product, and the company should act accordingly.

- **Question marks**  
  These are up-and-coming products in the introduction phase that currently still have a low market share in a fast-growing market. If the company promotes these products and monitors their growth, the product can grow strongly on the market and become a `star`. If the company misses the opportunity to promote it or no customers are interested in the product, the product could turn directly into a `poor dog`, which the company wants to avoid.

- **Stars**  
  Once the product has reached the `stars` sector, the company must use marketing to ensure that many customers choose the company and not the competition. Due to the high market growth, there are still many customers who do not yet own a product of this kind, and these customers are now choosing between the products of the competing companies.

  `Stars` bring the company high profits, and turnover and market share can be increased further. To achieve this increase, however, a lot of capital must also be invested in marketing.

- **Cash cows**  
  As soon as the annual growth rate falls, the `star` becomes a `cash cow`. A `cash cow` hardly requires any investment, but contributes a lot of profit to the company's coffers. Companies try to keep products _as `cash cows` for as long as possible_ in order to skim off the highest possible profits.

- **Poor dogs**  
  Products in this sector should be taken off the market due to poor future prospects. However, you have to consider how much of a `poor dog` the product is. If the product has only just turned from a `cash cow` into a `poor dog`, the relative market share will still be around 1 and you can also consider a relaunch.
