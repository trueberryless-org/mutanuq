The human body consists of trillions of cells that combine into **tissues** (epithelial, connective, muscle and nervous tissue), **organs** and **organ systems**. The systems work together to keep the internal environment constant (**homeostasis**): body temperature about $37\,\mathrm{°C}$, blood pH 7.35 to 7.45, blood sugar within narrow limits. Many of these processes work like **control loops** in engineering, with a sensor, set point, controller and actuator.

| Organ system             | Main organs                                    | Task                                                |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| nervous system           | brain, spinal cord, nerves, sense organs       | detect and process stimuli, fast control            |
| endocrine system         | pituitary gland, thyroid, pancreas, adrenal glands, gonads | slow, long-lasting control             |
| cardiovascular system    | heart, blood vessels, blood                    | transport of oxygen, nutrients, heat, hormones      |
| respiratory system       | airways, lungs                                 | gas exchange                                        |
| digestive system         | mouth, stomach, intestine, liver, pancreas     | break down and absorb food                          |
| excretory system         | kidneys, urinary tract                         | remove waste products, water and salt balance       |
| immune system            | lymph nodes, spleen, bone marrow, white blood cells | defence against pathogens                      |
| musculoskeletal system   | bones, joints, muscles, tendons                | support, protection, movement                       |
| reproductive system      | testes, ovaries, uterus                        | reproduction                                        |
| skin                     | skin, hair, glands                             | protection, temperature regulation, sense organ     |

## Nervous system

The **central nervous system** (CNS) consists of the brain and spinal cord, the **peripheral nervous system** of the nerves that run through the body. Functionally, the **somatic** nervous system (voluntary movement, conscious perception) is distinguished from the **autonomic** nervous system, which controls the organs unconsciously: the **sympathetic** system activates the body under exertion and stress (pulse and blood pressure rise, digestion is inhibited), the **parasympathetic** system ensures rest and recovery.

### Nerve cell

A **nerve cell** (neuron) consists of a cell body, branched **dendrites** that receive signals, and a long **axon** that conducts signals onwards. Many axons are wrapped in an insulating **myelin sheath**.

- At rest the inside of the cell is negatively charged relative to the outside (**resting potential** about $-70\,\mathrm{mV}$). The sodium-potassium pump maintains this ion gradient.
- If a threshold is exceeded, sodium channels open and the voltage briefly jumps to about $+30\,\mathrm{mV}$ (**action potential**). It follows the **all-or-nothing principle**: the strength of a stimulus is encoded not by the height but by the **frequency** of the action potentials, similar to a digital signal.
- In myelinated axons the excitation jumps from node to node (**saltatory conduction**) and reaches up to about $120\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
- At the **synapse** the electrical signal is transmitted chemically: the nerve ending releases **neurotransmitters** (such as acetylcholine, dopamine) that bind to receptors on the next cell. Many medicines and drugs act at synapses.

The brain contains about 86 billion nerve cells. It makes up about 2 % of body mass but uses about 20 % of the energy. In a **reflex** (knee-jerk reflex, pulling the hand away from a hot plate) the signal is switched in the spinal cord, without going via the cerebrum.

### Sense organs

| Sense         | Organ, receptors                                 | Stimulus                                  |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------- |
| sight         | eye, retina with **rods** (light and dark, about 120 million) and **cones** (colour, three types for red, green, blue, about 6 million) | light of about $380$ to $780\,\mathrm{nm}$ |
| hearing       | inner ear (cochlea) with hair cells              | sound of about $20\,\mathrm{Hz}$ to $20\,\mathrm{kHz}$ |
| balance       | semicircular canals and vestibule in the inner ear | rotation, acceleration, position         |
| smell         | olfactory epithelium of the nose                 | odorants                                   |
| taste         | taste buds of the tongue                         | sweet, sour, salty, bitter, umami          |
| touch, temperature, pain | receptors in the skin                 | pressure, vibration, warmth, cold, tissue damage |

In the **eye**, the cornea and lens focus light onto the retina. The lens changes its curvature to produce sharp images of near and distant objects (**accommodation**). If the eyeball is too long, the image forms in front of the retina (**short-sightedness**, corrected with a diverging lens); if it is too short, behind it (**long-sightedness**, converging lens). From about 45 years of age the elasticity of the lens declines (**presbyopia**). That screens mix colours from red, green and blue (RGB) is based on the three types of cone.

In the **ear** the eardrum passes sound via the three ossicles of the middle ear (hammer, anvil, stirrup) into the fluid-filled inner ear. There the hair cells in the cochlea are stimulated at different places depending on the frequency. Destroyed hair cells do not grow back, so noise damage is permanent.

## Endocrine system

**Hormones** are messenger substances released by glands into the blood that act on target cells with matching receptors. The overall control centre is the **hypothalamus** in the brain, which controls other glands via the **pituitary gland**.

| Gland                        | Hormone                    | Effect                                                      |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| thyroid                      | thyroxine (contains iodine) | stimulates metabolism and growth                           |
| pancreas                     | **insulin**, glucagon      | lowers or raises blood sugar                                 |
| adrenal medulla              | adrenaline                 | stress response: pulse, blood pressure and blood sugar rise |
| adrenal cortex               | cortisol                   | long-term stress, inhibits inflammation                      |
| testes, ovaries              | testosterone, oestrogen, progesterone | sexual characteristics, puberty, menstrual cycle  |
| pineal gland                 | melatonin                  | sleep-wake rhythm; inhibited by blue light, for example from screens |

**Blood sugar regulation** is a control loop with **negative feedback**: when blood sugar rises after a meal, the pancreas releases insulin, the cells take up glucose and the value falls. In **type 1 diabetes mellitus** the immune system destroys the insulin-producing cells; patients must inject insulin. In **type 2** (about 90 % of cases) the cells respond poorly to insulin; the main risk factors are overweight and lack of exercise.

## Heart and circulation

The **heart** is a hollow muscle with four chambers: two atria and two ventricles. Heart valves act as valves and ensure the direction of flow. Humans have a **double circulation**:

- **Pulmonary circulation**: right ventricle → pulmonary artery → lungs (uptake of $\mathrm{O_2}$, release of $\mathrm{CO_2}$) → pulmonary veins → left atrium.
- **Systemic circulation**: left ventricle → aorta → arteries → **capillaries** in all tissues (exchange of substances) → veins → right atrium.

**Arteries** carry blood away from the heart, **veins** towards it. An adult's heart beats about 60 to 80 times a minute at rest, pumping about $5\,\mathrm{L}$ of blood per minute. Normal blood pressure is about $120/80\,\mathrm{mmHg}$ (systolic/diastolic).

An adult has about $5$ to $6\,\mathrm{L}$ of blood:

| Component                         | Function                                                       |
| --------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Red blood cells** (erythrocytes) | oxygen transport with the iron-containing **haemoglobin**     |
| **White blood cells** (leukocytes) | immune defence                                                |
| **Platelets** (thrombocytes)      | blood clotting, closing wounds                                 |
| **Blood plasma**                  | water with nutrients, salts, hormones, proteins; transport of $\mathrm{CO_2}$ (as hydrogen carbonate) and heat |

Cardiovascular diseases (heart attack, stroke) are the most common cause of death in Austria. Risk factors are smoking, high blood pressure, lack of exercise, overweight, unhealthy diet and diabetes.

## Respiration

Air passes through the nose, pharynx, larynx, **trachea** and **bronchi** into the lungs, where the bronchi branch down to about 300 million **alveoli**. Their total surface area is very large, about $80$ to $100\,\mathrm{m^2}$. Oxygen diffuses through the thin wall into the blood and carbon dioxide into the air, each down its concentration gradient.

On breathing in, the **diaphragm** contracts and the intercostal muscles raise the rib cage; lung volume increases and air flows in. At rest an adult breathes about 12 to 16 times a minute, taking in about $0.5\,\mathrm{L}$ each time. Breathing rate is controlled by the $\mathrm{CO_2}$ content of the blood. Exhaled air contains about $16\,\%$ oxygen and $4\,\%$ carbon dioxide. In poorly ventilated classrooms the $\mathrm{CO_2}$ content therefore rises quickly, and concentration and performance fall. A guide value for good indoor air is below $1000\,\mathrm{ppm}$ $\mathrm{CO_2}$; a $\mathrm{CO_2}$ sensor shows when to ventilate.

Cellular respiration, which ultimately uses the oxygen, is covered on the page [Biomolecules and Metabolism](/en/natural-sciences/biology-biomolecules-and-metabolism/#cellular-respiration).

## Digestion

| Section           | Process                                                                     |
| ----------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| mouth             | chewing; saliva with **amylase** starts the digestion of starch             |
| stomach           | hydrochloric acid (pH 1 to 2) kills germs and denatures proteins, **pepsin** breaks down proteins |
| small intestine   | enzymes from the pancreas break down carbohydrates, proteins and fats; **bile** from the liver emulsifies fats. **Villi** increase the surface area; the building blocks are absorbed into the blood or lymph |
| large intestine   | recovery of water and salts; gut bacteria break down dietary fibre          |
| liver             | processes absorbed nutrients, stores glycogen, detoxifies (e.g. alcohol)    |

A balanced diet provides energy from carbohydrates, fats and proteins as well as vitamins, minerals, dietary fibre and enough water. The energy requirement of young people and adults lies, depending on age, sex and activity, roughly between $8$ and $12\,\mathrm{MJ}$ ($1900$ to $2900\,\mathrm{kcal}$) per day. The **Austrian food pyramid** of the Ministry of Health gives recommendations for the composition.

## Immune system

The **immune system** protects against pathogens (bacteria, viruses, fungi, parasites) and degenerate cells.

**Non-specific** (innate) defence: skin and mucous membranes as a barrier, stomach acid, enzymes in tears and saliva, **phagocytes** (macrophages, granulocytes) that engulf and digest pathogens, inflammation and fever.

**Specific** (acquired) defence by **lymphocytes**, which recognise particular features of a pathogen, the **antigens**:

- **B lymphocytes** become plasma cells and produce **antibodies** that fit exactly one antigen (lock-and-key principle), clump pathogens together and mark them for phagocytes (**humoral** response).
- **T helper cells** coordinate the defence, **cytotoxic T cells** (killer cells) kill infected body cells (**cell-mediated** response).
- **Memory cells** remain for years after an infection. On renewed contact with the same pathogen the immune system reacts so quickly that you usually do not fall ill: **immunity**.

### Vaccination

| Type                          | Principle                                                         | Effect                           |
| ----------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| **Active immunisation**       | weakened or killed pathogens, parts of pathogens or their blueprint (mRNA vaccine) are given; the body itself forms antibodies and memory cells | long-lasting protection, takes a few days to weeks to work; preventive |
| **Passive immunisation**      | ready-made antibodies are injected                                | immediate but short protection (weeks); e.g. after a snakebite or suspected tetanus |

If enough people are immune, a pathogen can hardly spread, and people who cannot be vaccinated are protected too (**herd protection**). For measles this requires a vaccination rate of about 95 %. Which vaccinations are recommended in Austria is laid down in the annually updated **Austrian Vaccination Plan**; many vaccinations for children and adolescents are free of charge. **Antibiotics** work only against bacteria, not against viruses.

In an **allergy** the immune system overreacts to harmless substances such as pollen; in **autoimmune diseases** it attacks the body's own cells.

## Musculoskeletal system

An adult's skeleton consists of about 206 bones, connected by **joints** (ball-and-socket, hinge, saddle joints). Over 600 skeletal muscles move them via **tendons**. Muscles can only pull, so they work in pairs as **antagonists** (flexors and extensors, such as biceps and triceps). Besides skeletal muscle there is smooth muscle (intestine, blood vessels) and heart muscle.

The **spine** consists of 7 cervical, 12 thoracic and 5 lumbar vertebrae, plus the sacrum and coccyx. Its **double S-curve** absorbs shocks. Between the vertebrae lie **intervertebral discs**, which are supplied with nutrients only through loading and unloading. Long, rigid sitting and weak core muscles are major causes of back pain.

## Health

In its constitution (1946) the World Health Organization defines: "Health is a state of complete physical, mental and social well-being and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity." Important influences are physical activity (for adolescents the WHO recommends an average of at least 60 minutes of moderate to vigorous activity a day), diet, sleep (adolescents need about 8 to 10 hours), coping with stress, social contacts and avoiding smoking and excessive alcohol consumption.

## Screen work and ergonomics

**Ergonomics** adapts work and the workplace to people. In Austria screen work is governed by the Employee Protection Act (ASchG) and the **Screen Work Ordinance** (BS-V, Federal Law Gazette II No. 124/1998). An ergonomic workstation has the following features:

| Area            | Recommendation                                                                 |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| screen          | top edge at or slightly below eye level, distance about an arm's length ($50$ to $70\,\mathrm{cm}$), directly in front of the body, tilted slightly back |
| light           | screen at right angles to the window, no reflections or glare; lighting about $500\,\mathrm{lx}$ at the workstation |
| chair           | height-adjustable, backrest supports the lumbar spine, feet flat on the floor, thighs horizontal, knee angle at least $90°$ |
| desk, keyboard  | forearms horizontal, shoulders relaxed, wrists straight; space in front of the keyboard to rest the heels of the hands |
| behaviour       | change sitting position often (dynamic sitting), stand up, regularly look into the distance, blink consciously |

Among other things, the BS-V requires:

- After every 50 minutes of uninterrupted screen work there must be a **break or a change of activity** of at least 10 minutes (§ 10). This does not apply if no more than two hours of uninterrupted screen work are done per day. The breaks count as working time.
- The employer must offer an **examination of the eyes** and eyesight: before starting the work, then regularly at intervals of no more than three years, and when vision problems occur (§ 11).
- If **special screen glasses** are needed, the employer must provide them and bear the costs, unless social insurance covers them (§ 12).

Typical complaints after long screen work are dry, tired eyes (blinking less often), tension in the neck and shoulders, back pain and overuse of the hands and forearms from mouse and keyboard.

## Noise and health

The **sound level** is given in decibels, A-weighted to match human hearing as **dB(A)**. The scale is logarithmic: $+10\,\mathrm{dB}$ means ten times the sound intensity and is perceived as roughly twice as loud; even $+3\,\mathrm{dB}$ is double the sound energy.

| Sound source (typical values)    | Level in dB(A) |
| -------------------------------- | -------------- |
| threshold of hearing             | 0              |
| quiet room                       | 30             |
| conversation                     | 60             |
| server room, busy road           | 70 to 85       |
| headphones at high volume, disco | 100 and more   |
| threshold of pain                | about 130      |

Continuous exposure from about $85\,\mathrm{dB(A)}$ (over an eight-hour working day) damages the hair cells in the inner ear. Because $+3\,\mathrm{dB}$ means double the energy, the acceptable exposure time halves for every $3\,\mathrm{dB}$ more: at $100\,\mathrm{dB(A)}$ the daily dose is reached after only about 15 minutes. The consequences are permanent **noise-induced hearing loss** and **tinnitus** (ringing in the ears). Noise also has effects below these levels: it disturbs concentration and sleep and raises stress hormones and blood pressure.

At work in Austria the Noise and Vibration Ordinance (VOLV) applies: from a daily exposure level of $80\,\mathrm{dB(A)}$ the employer must provide hearing protection, and the exposure limit of $85\,\mathrm{dB(A)}$, taking hearing protection into account, must not be exceeded. For headphones the 60/60 rule of thumb applies: at most 60 % of maximum volume for at most 60 minutes at a time.
