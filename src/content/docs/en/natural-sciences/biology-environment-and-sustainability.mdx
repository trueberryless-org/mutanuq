Humans now change the Earth so strongly that climate, matter cycles and biodiversity are measurably affected. This page links the scientific basics from [Environmental Chemistry](/en/natural-sciences/chemistry-environmental-chemistry/) and [Ecology](/en/natural-sciences/biology-ecology/) with the question of how society and technology, IT included, can become more sustainable.

## Climate change

**Weather** is the state of the atmosphere at one place at one time; **climate** is the statistics of the weather over at least 30 years. The climate has always changed in Earth's history, for example between ice ages and warm periods, but mostly over thousands of years. Today's warming is much faster and caused by humans.

### Causes

The **anthropogenic greenhouse effect** results from additional [greenhouse gases](/en/natural-sciences/chemistry-environmental-chemistry/#greenhouse-gases): above all $\mathrm{CO_2}$ from burning coal, oil and natural gas, from cement production and deforestation, plus methane and nitrous oxide from agriculture and landfills, and fluorinated gases. Warming is amplified by **feedbacks**: melting ice exposes darker surfaces that absorb more sunlight (ice-albedo feedback); warmer air holds more water vapour, itself a greenhouse gas; thawing permafrost releases methane and $\mathrm{CO_2}$.

### IPCC findings

The **Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change** (IPCC) summarises the state of research in assessment reports. The sixth report (AR6, Synthesis Report 2023) states, among other things:

| Finding                                                               | Value                                                      |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| cause                                                                 | humans have "unequivocally" caused the warming, principally through greenhouse gas emissions |
| global surface temperature 2011 to 2020 compared with 1850 to 1900    | $+1.1\,\mathrm{°C}$ (over land $+1.6\,\mathrm{°C}$, over the ocean $+0.9\,\mathrm{°C}$) |
| $\mathrm{CO_2}$ concentration 2019                                     | $410\,\mathrm{ppm}$, higher than at any time in at least 2 million years |
| methane and nitrous oxide 2019                                        | $1866\,\mathrm{ppb}$ and $332\,\mathrm{ppb}$, higher than in at least 800,000 years |
| rise in global mean sea level 1901 to 2018                            | about $0.20\,\mathrm{m}$, recently at an accelerating rate ($3.7\,\mathrm{mm}$ per year 2006 to 2018) |
| remaining $\mathrm{CO_2}$ budget from 2020 for $1.5\,\mathrm{°C}$ (50 % probability) | about $500\,\mathrm{Gt}$ $\mathrm{CO_2}$ |
| reduction needed for $1.5\,\mathrm{°C}$                                | greenhouse gas emissions down by about 43 % by 2030 and 60 % by 2035 compared with 2019, net zero $\mathrm{CO_2}$ in the early 2050s |
| warming by 2100 with policies implemented by 2020                     | about $3.2\,\mathrm{°C}$ (range $2.2$ to $3.5\,\mathrm{°C}$) |

Every additional tenth of a degree increases the risks. According to the World Meteorological Organization (WMO), 2024, at about $1.55\,\mathrm{°C}$ above the pre-industrial level, was the warmest year on record and probably the first calendar year above $1.5\,\mathrm{°C}$. The Paris target, however, refers to the average over decades.

**Austria**, as a landlocked mountainous country, is warming considerably faster than the global average: according to an analysis by GeoSphere Austria, the temperature here is already about $2.9\,\mathrm{°C}$ above the 1851 to 1900 mean (as of 2023).

### Consequences

| Area                      | Consequences                                                               |
| ------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| weather extremes          | more frequent and intense heatwaves, heavy rainfall, droughts; in Austria more hot days and debris flows |
| ice and sea               | retreat of glaciers (Austria's glaciers could largely disappear by the middle of the century), sea level rise, ocean acidification, decline in Arctic sea ice |
| ecosystems                | habitats shift northwards and uphill, species extinction, coral bleaching, bark beetle infestation of weakened spruce forests |
| health                    | heat deaths, spread of disease vectors (tiger mosquito), longer pollen season |
| agriculture, economy      | crop failures, water shortages, damage to infrastructure, decline in winter tourism |
| society                   | food insecurity, migration, conflicts over resources                       |

**Tipping points** are thresholds beyond which a part of the climate system changes abruptly and often irreversibly, for example the melting of the Greenland ice sheet or the death of coral reefs.

A distinction is made between **mitigation** (reducing emissions, preserving carbon sinks) and **adaptation** (heat protection in cities, flood protection, climate-resilient mixed forests). In the **Paris Agreement** (2015) states committed to holding warming well below $2\,\mathrm{°C}$ and pursuing efforts to limit it to $1.5\,\mathrm{°C}$.

## Sustainability

The term comes from forestry: in 1713 Hans Carl von Carlowitz demanded that no more wood be felled than grows back. The definition commonly used today comes from the United Nations' **Brundtland Report** (1987): sustainable development meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.

The **three-pillar model** requires three dimensions to be considered at the same time:

| Pillar        | Goal                                                    | Example                                               |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| **environmental** | preserve nature and resources                       | reduce emissions, circular economy                    |
| **economic**  | economic activity that is viable in the long term       | durable products, fair prices that include environmental costs |
| **social**    | fair distribution, decent work, education               | fair supply chains, no child labour in mining         |

Because the economy and society depend on functioning ecosystems, newer models (nested or priority model) treat the environmental dimension as the foundation of the other two.

### Sustainable Development Goals

With the **2030 Agenda**, all UN member states adopted the 17 **Sustainable Development Goals** (SDGs) in 2015, to be achieved by 2030. They include: no poverty (1), zero hunger (2), good health (3), quality education (4), gender equality (5), clean water (6), affordable and clean energy (7), decent work (8), industry, innovation and infrastructure (9), sustainable cities (11), responsible consumption and production (12), climate action (13), life below water (14) and life on land (15).

## Resources

**Renewable resources** (wood, water, fish, solar and wind energy) regenerate as long as they are not overused. **Non-renewable resources** (fossil fuels, metals, phosphate rock) are finite. Metals are not lost, but they become dispersed in products and are then hard to recover.

The **circular economy** replaces the linear model of "make, use, throw away". The order of measures is: avoid, reduce, reuse, repair, refurbish, recycle; only then energy recovery and finally landfill. This is also the waste hierarchy laid down in Austria's Waste Management Act.

### Ecological footprint

The **ecological footprint** states how much biologically productive area (in global hectares) is needed to cover the resource use of a person, country or humanity and to absorb its $\mathrm{CO_2}$ emissions. In industrialised countries the carbon footprint is the largest share. **Biocapacity** is the area actually available.

According to the Global Footprint Network, humanity currently consumes as much as if it had 1.8 Earths. **Earth Overshoot Day**, from which the resources of a year are used up by calculation, fell on 24 July in 2025. If everyone lived like the population of Austria, about four Earths would be needed; Austria's own overshoot day fell as early as 2 April in 2026.

The **carbon footprint** measures only greenhouse gas emissions (in $\mathrm{CO_2}$ equivalents). For individuals the main contributors are mobility (flights, car), heating and housing, diet (especially meat and dairy) and consumption.

## Renewable energy

| Energy source      | Principle                                                  | Advantages and disadvantages                                          |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Hydropower**     | potential and kinetic energy of water drives turbines      | Austria's most important source of electricity; controllable (storage power stations), but an intervention in river ecosystems |
| **Wind power**     | rotor drives a generator                                   | cheap, fluctuating (variable); land use and visual impact          |
| **Photovoltaics**  | semiconductor solar cells convert light directly into electricity ([pn junction](/en/natural-sciences/chemistry-materials-and-plastics/#doping)) | can be used on roofs, strong growth; generation during the day and in summer |
| **Solar thermal**  | collectors heat water                                      | simple, for hot water and heating                                     |
| **Biomass**        | burning or fermenting wood, residues, biogas               | storable and controllable; limited land, particulate matter           |
| **Geothermal energy**, ambient heat | heat from the Earth, heat pumps           | available around the clock; heat pumps need electricity              |

Because wind and solar fluctuate, a renewable energy system needs **storage** (pumped storage power stations, batteries, hydrogen), well-developed **power grids** and **flexible demand** (charging electric cars when plenty of electricity is available). Under the **Renewable Energy Expansion Act** (Erneuerbaren-Ausbau-Gesetz, EAG, 2021), Austria is to cover 100 % of its electricity consumption from renewable sources on a national annual balance from 2030. In 2024 the share was about 94 % according to the regulator E-Control; it varies with the water flow of the rivers. In total energy consumption, including transport and heating, the fossil share is still considerably higher.

## Green IT

**Green IT** refers to measures that reduce the resource and energy consumption of information technology over its whole life cycle, and to the use of IT to save emissions elsewhere.

### Energy use of data centres

According to the International Energy Agency (IEA), data centres used about $415\,\mathrm{TWh}$ of electricity in 2024, about 1.5 % of global electricity consumption. Mainly because of artificial intelligence, demand could more than double to about $945\,\mathrm{TWh}$ by 2030. Networks and end devices come on top of this.

The efficiency of the infrastructure is measured by **Power Usage Effectiveness**:

$$
\mathrm{PUE} = \frac{\text{total energy of the data centre}}{\text{energy of the IT equipment}}
$$

A PUE of $1.0$ would be ideal (no energy for cooling, lighting, losses). Modern large data centres reach about $1.1$ to $1.2$; older ones are considerably higher. Measures include efficient cooling (free cooling with outside air, liquid cooling), using waste heat for district heating, virtualisation and high server utilisation, electricity from renewable sources and efficient software. Under the EU Energy Efficiency Directive (2023), data centres from $500\,\mathrm{kW}$ must report their energy performance indicators.

### Life cycle of devices

For smartphones, laptops and many other end devices, most greenhouse gas emissions arise during **manufacturing** (mining of raw materials, chip production), not during use. The most effective measure is therefore to **use devices for longer**: repair them, replace batteries, buy refurbished devices, provide software updates for a long time. At the end, devices belong in [e-waste collection](/en/natural-sciences/chemistry-materials-and-plastics/#e-waste).

The EU reinforces this with rules: since December 2024 **USB-C** has been mandatory as the common charging port for smartphones, tablets and many other devices. Since June 2025 **ecodesign requirements** have applied to smartphones and tablets (spare parts for seven years, longer-lasting batteries, energy label with a repairability class). The **Right to Repair Directive** (2024) obliges manufacturers to repair certain products even after the legal guarantee has expired. In Austria the repair of electrical devices is subsidised by the state (2022 to 2025 with the **Reparaturbonus**, since January 2026 with the **Geräteretterprämie**).

:::tip[What you can contribute as a developer]
Efficient algorithms and data structures, lean websites (compress images, avoid unnecessary scripts), storing data only as long as necessary, moving compute-intensive tasks to times with plenty of renewable electricity, not leaving servers and containers idling, and building software that also runs on older hardware.
:::

## Environmental law in Austria and the EU

Principles of environmental law are the **precautionary principle** (avoid damage even if not all connections have been proven yet), the **polluter pays principle** (whoever causes environmental damage bears the costs) and the **cooperation principle**.

| Level      | Regulation                                                     | Content                                                    |
| ---------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Austria    | **Federal Constitutional Act on Sustainability** (BVG Nachhaltigkeit, 2013) | commitment of the Republic to sustainability and comprehensive environmental protection in the constitution |
| Austria    | **Environmental Impact Assessment Act** (UVP-G 2000)           | large projects (power stations, roads, data centres above certain sizes) are assessed for environmental impact before approval, with public participation |
| Austria    | **Waste Management Act** (AWG 2002), WEEE Ordinance            | waste hierarchy, separate collection, producer responsibility |
| Austria    | **Water Rights Act**, **Air Pollution Control Act** (IG-L)     | protection of waters, limit values for air pollutants      |
| Austria    | **Climate Protection Act** (2011), Renewable Energy Expansion Act (2021), national $\mathrm{CO_2}$ pricing (since 2022) | emission ceilings up to 2020; expansion of renewable energy; a price on emissions from transport and heating |
| EU         | **European Green Deal** (2019), **European Climate Law** (2021) | climate neutrality by 2050, net emissions cut by at least 55 % by 2030 compared with 1990 |
| EU         | **Emissions Trading System** (EU ETS, since 2005)              | cap and tradable allowances for power stations, industry, aviation |
| EU         | REACH, RoHS, WEEE, Ecodesign Regulation                        | chemicals, hazardous substances in electronics, e-waste, durable products |

The reduction targets of the Austrian Climate Protection Act expired in 2020. A new climate law with the goal of climate neutrality for Austria by 2040 was sent out for consultation in 2026; the current status can be found in the Legal Information System (RIS).
