Energy is the central conserved quantity of physics. Many problems that are tedious with the equations of motion can be solved in a few lines with an energy balance, because you do not need to know the exact course of the motion.

## Work

If a body is displaced along the path $\vec s$ by a force $\vec F$, the force does **mechanical work**:

$$
W = \vec F \cdot \vec s = F \cdot s \cdot \cos\varphi, \qquad [W] = \mathrm{N\,m} = \mathrm{J} \;(\text{joule})
$$

$\varphi$ is the angle between force and path. Only the force component in the direction of motion does work:

- $\varphi = 0^\circ$: $W = F s$ (maximum)
- $\varphi = 90^\circ$: $W = 0$. Carrying a suitcase horizontally does no work on the suitcase in the physical sense; neither does the centripetal force in circular motion.
- $\varphi = 180^\circ$: $W = -F s$. Friction does negative work; it removes energy from the body.

If the force is not constant, you integrate: $W = \int_{s_1}^{s_2} F(s)\,\mathrm{d}s$. In the $F$-$s$ graph, the work is the area under the curve.

| Form of work | Formula | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| work against gravity (lifting) | $W = m g h$ | lifting a box onto a shelf |
| work of acceleration | $W = \frac{1}{2} m (v_2^2 - v_1^2)$ | accelerating a car |
| work of stretching | $W = \frac{1}{2} D s^2$ | stretching a spring (triangle under $F = Ds$) |
| work against friction | $W = \mu F_\mathrm{N} s$ | pulling a sledge |

:::tip[Example: lifting]
A $20\,\mathrm{kg}$ box is lifted $1.5\,\mathrm{m}$: $W = 20 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 1.5 = 294\,\mathrm{J}$. If instead you push it up a frictionless ramp to the same height, the force is smaller, the distance longer and the work the same (**golden rule of mechanics**: what you save in force, you must add in distance).
:::

## Energy

**Energy** is the capacity to do work (or to give off heat, emit light). Work is energy transferred by a force. Both have the unit joule.

### Kinetic energy

A moving body has **kinetic energy**:

$$
E_\mathrm{kin} = \frac{1}{2} m v^2
$$

It grows with the square of the speed. This explains why accidents at higher speed are so much more serious.

:::tip[Example: car]
A $1200\,\mathrm{kg}$ car at $50\,\mathrm{km/h}$ ($13.9\,\mathrm{m/s}$) has a kinetic energy of $\frac{1}{2} \cdot 1200 \cdot 13.9^2 = 116\,\mathrm{kJ}$; at $100\,\mathrm{km/h}$ four times as much, $463\,\mathrm{kJ}$. The brakes have to convert this energy into heat.
:::

The **work-energy theorem** states: the work done by the resultant force equals the change in kinetic energy, $W_\mathrm{res} = \Delta E_\mathrm{kin}$.

### Potential energy

A body at height $h$ above a chosen reference level has **potential energy** (gravitational potential energy):

$$
E_\mathrm{pot} = m g h
$$

The reference level can be chosen freely; only differences are physically meaningful. The formula holds near the Earth's surface, where $g$ is constant; for the general case see [Gravitation](/en/natural-sciences/gravitation/#potential-energy-in-the-gravitational-field).

### Elastic energy

A spring stretched or compressed by $s$ stores **elastic potential energy**:

$$
E_\mathrm{sp} = \frac{1}{2} D s^2
$$

A spring with $D = 500\,\mathrm{N/m}$, compressed by $10\,\mathrm{cm}$, stores $\frac{1}{2} \cdot 500 \cdot 0.1^2 = 2.5\,\mathrm{J}$.

### Other forms of energy

Besides mechanical energy, there is internal (thermal) energy, chemical energy, electrical energy, radiant energy and nuclear energy. Energy can be converted between these forms, for example in a power station or an electric motor (see [Energy and Climate](/en/natural-sciences/energy-and-climate-physics/)).

## Conservation of energy

> In a closed system, the total energy remains constant. Energy can be neither created nor destroyed, only converted.

If only **conservative forces** act (weight, spring force), **mechanical energy** is conserved:

$$
E_\mathrm{kin} + E_\mathrm{pot} + E_\mathrm{sp} = \text{constant}
$$

A force is called conservative if its work depends only on the start and end points, not on the path. Friction is **not** conservative: it converts mechanical energy into internal energy (heat). The total energy is still conserved, only the mechanical energy is not.

:::tip[Example: roller coaster]
A car starts from rest at a height of $30\,\mathrm{m}$. Without friction, $m g h = \frac{1}{2} m v^2$, so at the lowest point

$$
v = \sqrt{2 g h} = \sqrt{2 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 30} = 24.3\,\mathrm{m/s} \approx 87\,\mathrm{km/h}
$$

The mass cancels, and the shape of the track does not matter. With the equations of motion, you would have to calculate each section separately.
:::

:::tip[Example: spring gun]
A spring ($D = 500\,\mathrm{N/m}$, compressed by $10\,\mathrm{cm}$) fires a ball with $m = 10\,\mathrm{g}$ vertically upwards. From $\frac{1}{2} D s^2 = m g h$ it follows that $h = \frac{2.5}{0.01 \cdot 9.81} = 25.5\,\mathrm{m}$ (without air resistance).
:::

## Power

**Power** states how fast work is done or energy is converted:

$$
P = \frac{W}{t} = \frac{\Delta E}{\Delta t}, \qquad [P] = \mathrm{J/s} = \mathrm{W} \;(\text{watt})
$$

For a constant force in the direction of motion, $W = F s$ and $v = s/t$ give:

$$
P = F \cdot v
$$

That is why a car needs a lot of power at high speed: air resistance grows with $v^2$, so the power needed to overcome it grows with $v^3$.

:::tip[Example: crane]
A crane lifts $500\,\mathrm{kg}$ by $12\,\mathrm{m}$ in $20\,\mathrm{s}$. Work $W = 500 \cdot 9.81 \cdot 12 = 58.9\,\mathrm{kJ}$, power $P = 58\,860/20 = 2.94\,\mathrm{kW}$.
:::

The obsolete unit metric horsepower ($1\,\mathrm{PS} = 735.5\,\mathrm{W}$) is still found for cars; the legal unit is the kilowatt.

## Efficiency

No machine converts the supplied energy entirely into the desired form; part of it becomes heat. The **efficiency** $\eta$ (eta) is the ratio of useful output to input:

$$
\eta = \frac{P_\mathrm{out}}{P_\mathrm{in}} = \frac{E_\mathrm{useful}}{E_\mathrm{in}} < 1
$$

For converters connected in series, the efficiencies multiply: $\eta_\mathrm{total} = \eta_1 \cdot \eta_2 \cdot \eta_3 \cdots$

:::tip[Example]
The crane motor draws $3.5\,\mathrm{kW}$ of electrical power. Its efficiency is $\eta = 2.94/3.5 = 0.84 = 84\,\%$. If it drives through a gearbox with $\eta = 0.95$, the overall efficiency is $0.84 \cdot 0.95 = 0.80$.
:::

| Converter (typical values) | Efficiency |
| --- | --- |
| large electric motor, transformer | 95 to 99 % |
| hydroelectric power station | 85 to 90 % |
| combined-cycle gas power station | about 60 % |
| coal-fired power station | 40 to 45 % |
| petrol engine in a car | 25 to 35 % |
| photovoltaic module | 18 to 23 % |
| incandescent lamp (light) | about 3 % |

## Energy units

| Unit | Conversion | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| joule ($\mathrm{J}$) | $1\,\mathrm{J} = 1\,\mathrm{N\,m} = 1\,\mathrm{W\,s}$ | SI unit |
| kilowatt hour ($\mathrm{kWh}$) | $1\,\mathrm{kWh} = 1000\,\mathrm{W} \cdot 3600\,\mathrm{s} = 3.6\,\mathrm{MJ}$ | electricity bill |
| electronvolt ($\mathrm{eV}$) | $1\,\mathrm{eV} = 1.602\,176\,634 \times 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{J}$ | atomic and nuclear physics |
| calorie ($\mathrm{cal}$, obsolete) | $1\,\mathrm{cal} = 4.184\,\mathrm{J}$ | food energy ("kcal") |

One electronvolt is the energy an electron gains when it passes through a voltage of $1\,\mathrm{V}$ ($W = e \cdot U$). An Austrian household uses roughly $3000$ to $4000\,\mathrm{kWh}$ of electricity per year, which is about $11$ to $14\,\mathrm{GJ}$.
