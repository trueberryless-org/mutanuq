**Evolution** is the change in the heritable traits of populations over many generations. It explains the diversity and the relatedness of all living things. The Earth is about $4.6$ billion years old; the oldest traces of life (bacteria) are about $3.5$ billion years old. The biologist Theodosius Dobzhansky wrote in 1973: "Nothing in biology makes sense except in the light of evolution."

## Evidence for evolution

| Area of evidence          | Examples                                                                    |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Fossils**               | sequence in rock layers from simple to complex forms; **transitional forms** such as _Archaeopteryx_ with features of reptiles (teeth, tail vertebrae) and birds (feathers); evolution of horses and whales |
| **Homologous organs**     | same basic structure with different functions, indicating common descent: human arm, bat wing, whale flipper, mole's digging limb |
| **Analogous organs**      | similar function with different structure, arising from similar environmental conditions (convergence): wings of birds and insects, body shape of shark and dolphin |
| **Vestigial organs**      | reduced organs without (their original) function: coccyx and wisdom teeth in humans, pelvic bones in whales |
| **Embryology**            | embryos of all vertebrates initially have pharyngeal arches and a tail      |
| **Molecular biology**     | universal genetic code; the more similar the DNA sequences, the closer the relationship (humans and chimpanzees match in about 98.8 % of bases) |
| **Biogeography**          | island species such as Darwin's finches on the Galápagos Islands resemble species on the nearest mainland |
| **Observed evolution**    | antibiotic resistance in bacteria, insecticide resistance in pests, peppered moths in industrial areas |

## Theories of evolution

### Lamarck

Jean-Baptiste de Lamarck published the first comprehensive theory of evolution in 1809. He assumed that organs are strengthened by use and weakened by disuse and that these **acquired characteristics are inherited**: giraffes would have got ever longer necks by stretching for leaves. This mechanism has been disproved, because changes to the body do not reach the germ cells.

### Darwin

Charles Darwin (after his voyage round the world on the _Beagle_ from 1831 to 1836) and, independently, Alfred Russel Wallace developed the theory of **natural selection**. Darwin's book _On the Origin of Species_ appeared in 1859. The argument:

1. Living things produce more offspring than can survive (**overproduction**).
2. Individuals of a species differ (**variation**), and many differences are heritable.
3. Resources are limited, which leads to **competition** ("struggle for existence").
4. Individuals better adapted to the environment survive more often and have more offspring (**survival of the fittest**, where _fit_ means "well suited", not "strong").
5. Over many generations advantageous traits accumulate in the population.

For the giraffe this means: animals that happened to have slightly longer necks reached more food in times of drought and had more offspring.

### Modern synthesis

Darwin knew neither genes nor mutations. The **modern synthesis** (from about 1940) combines his theory of selection with genetics and population biology. Evolution is then the change in **allele frequencies** in the **gene pool** of a population.

## Factors of evolution

| Factor                    | Effect                                                                  |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Mutation**              | creates new alleles; undirected and random                              |
| **Recombination**         | reshuffles existing alleles in meiosis and fertilisation               |
| **Selection**             | environmental factors (predators, climate, food, parasites) favour particular phenotypes; directed |
| **Genetic drift**         | random change in allele frequencies, especially strong in small populations |
| **Gene flow** (migration) | immigration and emigration exchange alleles between populations         |
| **Isolation**             | interrupts gene flow, a precondition for speciation                     |

### Selection

| Form                          | Effect on the distribution of a trait                    | Example                                               |
| ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| **stabilising** selection     | extremes are at a disadvantage, the average is favoured  | birth weight in humans                                |
| **directional** selection     | one extreme is favoured, the mean shifts                 | antibiotic resistance, dark peppered moths on soot-blackened trees |
| **disruptive** selection      | both extremes are favoured                               | beak sizes in finches with two types of seed          |
| **sexual** selection          | traits that give advantages in choosing a mate           | peacock's tail, antlers of deer                       |

:::note[Antibiotic resistance]
Among millions of bacteria, mutation produces a few that survive an antibiotic. When the antibiotic is used, the others die and the resistant ones multiply. Unnecessary use and stopping treatment early speed up this selection. Resistant germs are a major problem in hospitals.
:::

### Genetic drift

In small populations chance helps decide which alleles are passed on. In the **bottleneck effect** only a small part of a population survives a catastrophe, and genetic diversity remains low afterwards (for example in cheetahs). In the **founder effect** a few individuals colonise a new area, and their random set of alleles shapes the new population.

## Speciation

According to the **biological species concept**, individuals belong to the same **species** if they interbreed under natural conditions and produce fertile offspring. Horse and donkey are different species: their offspring, the mule, is usually infertile.

New species arise when gene flow between subpopulations is interrupted and they develop separately:

- **Allopatric speciation**: a geographical barrier (mountain range, sea, river, ice-age glacier) separates the populations. Mutation, selection and drift change them differently until they can no longer interbreed.
- **Sympatric speciation**: species arise in the same area, for example through genome mutations (polyploidy in plants) or specialisation on different food.

**Isolating mechanisms** prevent interbreeding: **prezygotic** (different mating seasons, courtship behaviour, habitats, incompatible reproductive organs) or **postzygotic** (hybrids die or are infertile).

If a species colonises many new ecological niches and splits into numerous adapted species, this is called **adaptive radiation**. The classic example is Darwin's finches: from one ancestral species, species with beaks for seeds, insects, cacti or flowers arose on the Galápagos Islands.

## Human evolution

Humans (_Homo sapiens_) belong to the great apes. Their closest living relatives are the chimpanzee and the bonobo; the lineages split about 6 to 7 million years ago in Africa.

| Time (before present)         | Group                        | Features                                                     |
| ----------------------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| from about 4 million years    | _Australopithecus_ (e.g. "Lucy", _A. afarensis_, about 3.2 million years) | upright walking, small brain, Africa |
| from about 2.5 million years  | early _Homo_ forms (_Homo habilis_) | larger brain, simple stone tools                      |
| from about 1.9 million years  | _Homo erectus_               | first to leave Africa, use of fire                           |
| about 400,000 to 40,000 years | Neanderthals (_Homo neanderthalensis_) | Europe and western Asia, adapted to cold, burials  |
| from about 300,000 years      | _Homo sapiens_               | arose in Africa, spread worldwide from about 60,000 years ago ("Out of Africa") |

_Homo sapiens_ and Neanderthals had offspring together: people outside Africa carry about 1 to 2 % Neanderthal DNA. Svante Pääbo received the 2022 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for decoding the Neanderthal genome. One of the best-known works of art of the Palaeolithic comes from Austria: the roughly 30,000-year-old **Venus of Willendorf** from the Wachau valley.

The biological evolution of humans is now strongly overlaid by **cultural evolution**: knowledge is passed on much faster through language, writing and technology than through genes.

:::tip[Evolution in computer science]
**Genetic algorithms** apply the principle to optimisation problems: a population of candidate solutions is evaluated (fitness function), the best are selected (selection), combined (crossover) and randomly changed (mutation). They are used to optimise, for example, antenna shapes, timetables or routes.
:::
