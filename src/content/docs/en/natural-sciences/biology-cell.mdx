The **cell** is the smallest unit that shows all the characteristics of life. Robert Hooke was the first to describe "cells" in cork in 1665. The **cell theory** of Schleiden, Schwann and Virchow (19th century) states: all living things consist of cells, and every cell arises from another cell ("omnis cellula e cellula").

## Characteristics of life

Living things differ from non-living matter in showing all of the following characteristics:

| Characteristic                | Meaning                                                           |
| ----------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **cellular structure**        | consist of one or many cells                                      |
| **metabolism**                | take in substances, transform them and release waste products     |
| **energy conversion**         | obtain energy from light or food                                  |
| **growth and development**    | increase in size and change according to a programme              |
| **reproduction**              | produce offspring and pass on genetic information                 |
| **responsiveness**            | detect stimuli from the environment and respond to them           |
| **movement**                  | active movement, also within the cell                             |
| **homeostasis**               | keep their internal environment (temperature, pH, water) constant |

**Viruses** show only some of these characteristics: they consist of genetic material (DNA or RNA) in a protein coat, have no metabolism of their own and reproduce only in host cells. They are therefore usually not regarded as living things.

## Prokaryotes and eukaryotes

| Feature                | **Prokaryotes** (bacteria, archaea)        | **Eukaryotes** (animals, plants, fungi, protists) |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------- |
| nucleus                | none; circular DNA free in the cytoplasm (**nucleoid**), plus **plasmids** | nucleus enclosed by a double membrane, linear chromosomes |
| size                   | about $1$ to $10\,\mathrm{\mu m}$          | about $10$ to $100\,\mathrm{\mu m}$               |
| membrane-bound organelles | none                                    | mitochondria, ER, Golgi apparatus, in plants chloroplasts |
| ribosomes              | 70S (smaller)                              | 80S in the cytoplasm, 70S in mitochondria and chloroplasts |
| cell wall              | usually present (murein in bacteria)       | plants: cellulose, fungi: chitin, animals: none   |
| locomotion             | flagellum made of flagellin                | flagella and cilia with microtubules              |

The **endosymbiotic theory** explains why mitochondria and chloroplasts have their own circular DNA, 70S ribosomes and a double membrane: they descend from bacteria that were engulfed by an ancestral cell more than a billion years ago and not digested.

## Cell organelles

| Organelle                             | Structure                                      | Function                                                        |
| ------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Nucleus**                           | double membrane with nuclear pores, chromatin, nucleolus | contains the DNA, controls the cell, site of transcription |
| **Mitochondrion**                     | double membrane, inner membrane folded (cristae) | **cellular respiration**, production of ATP ("powerhouse of the cell") |
| **Chloroplast** (plants, algae only)  | double membrane, thylakoids with chlorophyll    | **photosynthesis**                                              |
| **Ribosomes**                         | made of rRNA and proteins, no membrane          | **protein synthesis** (translation)                             |
| **Rough endoplasmic reticulum**       | membrane system with ribosomes                  | synthesis of membrane and export proteins                       |
| **Smooth ER**                         | membrane system without ribosomes               | lipid synthesis, detoxification, calcium store                 |
| **Golgi apparatus**                   | stack of flat membrane sacs                     | modifies, sorts and packages proteins into vesicles            |
| **Lysosomes** (animals)               | vesicles with digestive enzymes                 | breakdown of foreign substances and old cell components        |
| **Vacuole** (plants)                  | large membrane-enclosed space                   | storage, cell pressure (turgor)                                 |
| **Cell membrane**                     | lipid bilayer with proteins                     | boundary, controlled exchange of substances, signal reception  |
| **Cell wall** (plants, fungi, bacteria) | cellulose, chitin or murein                   | stability, shape                                                |
| **Cytoskeleton**                      | microtubules, actin filaments                   | shape, movement, transport, distribution of chromosomes         |

Plant cells differ from animal cells in having a **cell wall**, **chloroplasts** and a large **central vacuole**.

## Biomembranes and transport

According to the **fluid mosaic model** (Singer and Nicolson, 1972), biomembranes consist of a **lipid bilayer** of phospholipids with embedded proteins. The phospholipids have a water-loving (hydrophilic) head and two water-repelling (hydrophobic) fatty acid tails. In water they arrange themselves with the heads facing outwards and the tails inwards. The membrane is **selectively permeable**: small non-polar molecules ($\mathrm{O_2}$, $\mathrm{CO_2}$) pass through easily, ions and large polar molecules only via transport proteins.

| Type of transport                    | Energy needed | Direction                        | Example                                           |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------- | -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| **Diffusion**                        | no            | down the concentration gradient  | $\mathrm{O_2}$ and $\mathrm{CO_2}$ in the lungs   |
| **Osmosis**                          | no            | water towards the higher concentration of dissolved particles | water uptake by roots |
| **Facilitated diffusion**            | no            | down the gradient, via channel or carrier proteins | glucose, ion channels in nerve cells |
| **Active transport**                 | yes (ATP)     | against the concentration gradient | **sodium-potassium pump**: 3 $\mathrm{Na^+}$ out, 2 $\mathrm{K^+}$ in |
| **Endocytosis, exocytosis**          | yes           | uptake or release in vesicles    | phagocytes engulf bacteria, release of hormones   |

**Osmosis** is the diffusion of water through a **semi-permeable** membrane. If red blood cells are placed in pure water (a **hypotonic** solution), water flows in and they burst. In concentrated salt solution (**hypertonic**) they lose water and shrink. Infusions are therefore **isotonic** (physiological saline with $0.9\,\%$ NaCl). Plant cells do not burst in water, because the cell wall withstands the pressure.

## Cell division

### Cell cycle

The **cell cycle** consists of **interphase** and the actual division:

- **G1 phase**: the cell grows and forms organelles.
- **S phase**: **replication** of the DNA. Each chromosome then consists of two identical **sister chromatids** joined at the **centromere**.
- **G2 phase**: preparation for division.
- **M phase**: mitosis and division of the cell (cytokinesis).

Checkpoints check whether the DNA is undamaged and completely copied. If these controls fail, a cell can divide uncontrollably: a tumour develops (**cancer**).

Humans have **46 chromosomes** in every body cell, i.e. two sets of 23 (**diploid**, $2n$). One set comes from the mother and one from the father; chromosomes that look alike are called **homologous chromosomes**. The two sex chromosomes are XX in women and XY in men. Gametes (egg cell, sperm cell) have only one set (**haploid**, $n = 23$).

### Mitosis

**Mitosis** produces two genetically identical daughter cells with a complete set of chromosomes from one cell. It serves growth, tissue renewal and wound healing.

| Phase           | Process                                                                       |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Prophase**    | chromosomes condense and become visible, the nuclear envelope breaks down, the spindle forms |
| **Metaphase**   | chromosomes line up in the equatorial plane                                   |
| **Anaphase**    | sister chromatids are separated and pulled to the poles                       |
| **Telophase**   | nuclear envelopes re-form, chromosomes decondense; then the cytoplasm divides |

### Meiosis

**Meiosis** (reduction division) produces four haploid gametes from one diploid cell. Only in this way does the number of chromosomes stay constant at fertilisation ($n + n = 2n$).

- **Meiosis I** (reduction division): homologous chromosomes pair up and exchange segments (**crossing over**). The homologous chromosomes are then distributed randomly to the daughter cells. The number of chromosomes is halved.
- **Meiosis II** (equational division): like a mitosis, the sister chromatids are separated.

Meiosis creates **genetic diversity**: the random distribution of the 23 chromosome pairs alone gives $2^{23} \approx 8.4$ million combinations, and crossing over many more. That is why siblings (except identical twins) are genetically different. Errors in distribution lead to abnormal chromosome numbers, for example three copies of chromosome 21 in **Down syndrome** (trisomy 21).

| Feature                 | Mitosis                      | Meiosis                                  |
| ----------------------- | ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| number of divisions     | 1                            | 2                                        |
| daughter cells          | 2, diploid                   | 4, haploid                               |
| genetic information     | identical to the parent cell | recombined                               |
| purpose                 | growth, regeneration         | formation of gametes                     |
