The formal letter is a text type of the SRDP English exam at BHS. In terms of content, the same rules apply as for the [formal email](/en/languages/text_types/email/#formal-email): a polite, factual tone, no short forms and a clear division into paragraphs. The difference lies in the **layout**: a letter has addresses, a date in letter form and a signature. The most important subtypes are the **letter of complaint**, the **letter of enquiry**, the **letter of application** (see [CV and Application](/en/languages/english/cv_and_application/#cover-letter)) and the **letter to the editor**.

## Characteristics

-   formal, polite
-   `I` and `you` are allowed
-   no short forms (`I am` instead of `I'm`) and no colloquial expressions
-   short paragraphs, each with one clear content
-   salutation and closing must match (`Yours sincerely` or `Yours faithfully`)

## Layout

### Letter format

In British English the **block format** is common today: all parts are aligned to the left, with a blank line between the parts.

> Max Muster  
> Waldstraße 12  
> 3500 Krems  
> Austria
>
> 20 January 2024
>
> Customer Service  
> TechShop Ltd  
> 15 High Street  
> London W1 2AB  
> United Kingdom

The order is: your own address (with or without your name, depending on whether your name appears under the signature), the date, the recipient's address. In the traditional British form your own address is at the top right with the date below it; both forms are correct.

| Date        | British English | American English |
| ----------- | --------------- | ---------------- |
| written out | 20 January 2024 | January 20, 2024 |
| short form  | 20/01/2024      | 01/20/2024       |

:::caution
The short form is ambiguous between British and American English. In a letter, therefore, always write the date with the month spelt out.
:::

### Subject line

A subject line is optional but makes the letter easier to read. It goes after or before the salutation and is often printed in bold or introduced with `Re:`.

> Re: Order No. 48213, faulty laptop

### Salutation

| Recipient          | Salutation                      | Closing (BrE)     |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------- | ----------------- |
| name known         | Dear Mr Smith, / Dear Ms Smith, | Yours sincerely,  |
| name unknown       | Dear Sir or Madam,              | Yours faithfully, |
| position known     | Dear Hiring Manager,            | Yours faithfully, |
| newspaper editor   | Dear Editor, / Sir,             | Yours faithfully, |

In British English there is no full stop after `Mr`, `Mrs`, `Ms` and `Dr`; in American English there is (`Mr.`). Use `Ms` for women when you do not know their marital status; it is the usual form today. In American business letters the salutation is followed by a colon (`Dear Mr. Smith:`) and the closing is usually `Sincerely,` or `Sincerely yours,`.

### Introduction

State the reason for the letter in the very first sentence.

> I am writing to complain about a laptop which I ordered from your online shop on 5 January.

### Main part

Divide the main part into two to three paragraphs. For a complaint: what happened, what the consequences were, what you expect. For an enquiry: what information you need and what you need it for.

### Conclusion and closing

Say what you expect from the recipient and thank them. Then follow the closing, your signature and your name in print.

> I look forward to hearing from you.
>
> Yours sincerely,
>
> _Max Muster_
>
> Max Muster

:::tip[Remember]
**S**incerely goes with a known **S**urname. If you know the name, write `Yours sincerely`, otherwise `Yours faithfully`.
:::

## Letter of complaint

The letter of complaint follows the same sequence as the [email of complaint](/en/languages/text_types/email/#email-of-complaint): reason for the complaint, an exact description of the problem with facts (date, order number, price), the effects on you and a concrete demand. Stay polite and factual even if you are very annoyed; a factual tone is more convincing than an emotional one.

| Phrases                                                                    |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| I am writing to complain about ...                                         |
| I am writing to express my dissatisfaction with ...                        |
| On ... I ordered ... (order number ...).                                   |
| Unfortunately, when the parcel arrived, I found that ...                   |
| This has caused me considerable inconvenience, as ...                      |
| I would therefore like you to _replace the item / refund the full amount_. |
| If I do not hear from you within 14 days, I will have to take further steps. |

## Letter of enquiry

In a letter of enquiry you ask for information, for example about a language course, an internship or a product. It is short, polite and asks concrete questions.

| Phrases                                                    |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| I am writing to enquire about ...                          |
| I saw your advertisement in ... and would like to know ... |
| Could you please send me further information about ...?    |
| I would be grateful if you could tell me ...               |
| I would also like to know whether ...                      |
| Thank you in advance for your help.                        |

:::note
Indirect questions such as `I would like to know whether the course includes accommodation.` have the word order of a statement and no question mark. See [Sentence Structure](/en/languages/english/sentence_structure/#indirect-questions) for more.
:::

## Letter to the editor

A letter to the editor responds to an article in a newspaper or magazine. It is addressed to the editor but written for the readers of the paper that publishes it. Its structure is similar to the [German letter to the editor](/en/languages/german_text_types/letter_to_the_editor/).

### Characteristics

-   formal, but opinionated and persuasive
-   reference to the article with its title and date of publication
-   a clear position of your own, supported by arguments and examples
-   short and to the point, since newspapers shorten long letters
-   signed with name and town (`Max Muster, Krems`)

### Layout

1.  **Salutation:** `Dear Editor,` (traditionally also just `Sir,` in British newspapers)
2.  **Introduction:** reference to the article and your position
3.  **Main part:** two to three arguments, possibly a refuted counter-argument
4.  **Conclusion:** demand, suggestion or appeal to the readers
5.  **Closing:** `Yours faithfully,` and your name with your town

| Phrases                                                                |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| I am writing in response to your article "..." published on ...      |
| I read your article on ... with great interest.                        |
| I strongly _agree / disagree_ with the author's view that ...         |
| The article fails to mention that ...                                  |
| As a pupil at a technical college, I can confirm that ...              |
| I would urge _readers / the authorities_ to ...                        |
| I hope that my letter will encourage others to ...                     |
