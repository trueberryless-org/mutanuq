Every word can be assigned to a **word class** (Wortart). If you can identify word classes reliably, you will find the rules of capitalisation, punctuation and syntax much easier, because almost all of these rules are linked to word classes. German schools usually distinguish ten word classes.

## Overview

The most important division is by whether a word **changes its form** (inflection, Flexion):

| Group                         | Word classes                                       | Change                                       |
| ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| conjugable                    | verb                                               | by person, number, tense, mood, voice        |
| declinable                    | noun, article, pronoun, adjective, (numeral)       | by case, number, gender                      |
| comparable                    | adjective, some adverbs                            | comparison                                   |
| invariable (particles in the wider sense) | adverb, preposition, conjunction, interjection | none                                  |

:::note
Some grammars (such as the Duden grammar) group words like `sehr` (very), `nur` (only), `ja`, `doch` into a separate word class called **particle** (Partikel: degree, focus and modal particles). At school, they are usually counted as adverbs.
:::

## Noun (Nomen, Substantiv)

Nouns denote living beings, things, concepts and processes: `Lehrerin` (female teacher), `Laptop`, `Freiheit` (freedom), `das Laufen` (running). In German they are always **capitalised** and can take an article.

### Gender

Every noun has a fixed grammatical gender: **masculine** (der Server), **feminine** (die Datei, the file), **neuter** (das Netzwerk, the network). Gender is not always logical (`das Mädchen`, the girl, is neuter), but some endings help:

| Gender    | typical endings                                          | Examples                          |
| --------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| masculine | -er (people/devices), -ling, -ismus, -or                 | der Drucker (printer), der Lehrling (apprentice), der Motor |
| feminine  | -ung, -heit, -keit, -schaft, -ion, -tät, -ik, -ei        | die Lösung (solution), die Einheit (unit), die Informatik |
| neuter    | -chen, -lein, -ment, -um, nominalised infinitives        | das Häuschen (little house), das Dokument, das Speichern (saving) |

In Austria, some words have a different gender: `das Cola` (alongside `die Cola`), `das E-Mail` (alongside `die E-Mail`), `der Polster` (das Kissen, the cushion), `der Akt` (die Akte, the file).

### Number

Nouns are in the **singular** or **plural**. The plural is formed in five ways, sometimes with an umlaut:

| Plural ending | Example                   |
| ------------- | ------------------------- |
| -e            | der Tag, die Tage (day); die Hand, die Hände (hand) |
| -er           | das Kind, die Kinder (child); das Buch, die Bücher (book) |
| -(e)n         | die Frau, die Frauen (woman); der Bote, die Boten (messenger) |
| -s            | das Auto, die Autos (car) |
| no ending     | der Lehrer, die Lehrer (teacher); der Vater, die Väter (father) |

Some nouns exist only in the singular (singulare tantum: `die Milch`, milk; `das Obst`, fruit) or only in the plural (plurale tantum: `die Leute`, people; `die Ferien`, holidays).

### Case

German has four cases. You identify them with the matching question:

| Case        | Question        | Example                                  |
| ----------- | --------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| nominative  | Wer oder was? (who or what?) | **Der Techniker** repariert den Server. (The technician repairs the server.) |
| genitive    | Wessen? (whose?) | Das Kabel **des Routers** ist defekt. (The router's cable is faulty.) |
| dative      | Wem? (to whom?) | Sie hilft **dem Schüler**. (She helps the pupil.) |
| accusative  | Wen oder was? (whom or what?) | Er kauft **einen Laptop**. (He buys a laptop.) |

### Declension

**Declension** (Deklination) is the change of a noun (with its article) by case and number. There are three classes:

| Case | strong (masc./neut.) | weak (n-declension) | feminine |
| ---- | -------------------- | ------------------- | -------- |
| nom. sg. | der Tisch        | der Mensch          | die Frau |
| gen. sg. | des Tisch**es**  | des Mensch**en**    | der Frau |
| dat. sg. | dem Tisch        | dem Mensch**en**    | der Frau |
| acc. sg. | den Tisch        | den Mensch**en**    | die Frau |
| nom. pl. | die Tische       | die Menschen        | die Frauen |
| dat. pl. | den Tisch**en**  | den Menschen        | den Frauen |

-   **Strong:** genitive singular in -(e)s, otherwise no ending in the singular.
-   **Weak (n-declension):** only masculine nouns such as `der Mensch` (human), `der Student`, `der Kollege` (colleague), `der Präsident`, `der Kunde` (customer). All forms except the nominative singular end in -(e)n. A common mistake is `*dem Präsident` instead of `dem Präsidenten`.
-   **Mixed:** a few words such as `der Name` (des Namens, dem Namen) and `das Herz` (heart: des Herzens, dem Herzen).
-   **Feminine** nouns do not change in the singular.
-   In the **dative plural**, every noun ends in -n unless the plural ends in -s: `mit den Kindern` (with the children), but `mit den Autos`.

## Article (Artikel)

The article accompanies the noun and shows gender, number and case.

-   **definite article:** der, die, das (for something known or unambiguous)
-   **indefinite article:** ein, eine, ein (for something new or unspecified; no plural)
-   **zero article:** no article, for example with mass nouns and indefinite plurals (`Wir brauchen Strom.`, we need electricity; `Er kauft Äpfel.`, he buys apples)

| Case        | masculine   | feminine   | neuter    | plural |
| ----------- | ----------- | ---------- | --------- | ------ |
| nominative  | der / ein   | die / eine | das / ein | die    |
| genitive    | des / eines | der / einer | des / eines | der  |
| dative      | dem / einem | der / einer | dem / einem | den  |
| accusative  | den / einen | die / eine | das / ein | die    |

`kein` (no, not a) is declined like `ein` but also has plural forms (keine, keiner, keinen, keine).

## Pronoun (Pronomen)

Pronouns stand for a noun (replacing it) or accompany it (as a determiner).

| Type                   | Examples                                           | Use                                                       |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| personal pronoun       | ich, du, er, sie, es, wir, ihr, sie, Sie           | replace people and things                                 |
| reflexive pronoun      | mich, dich, sich, uns, euch                        | refer back to the subject: Sie freut **sich**. (She is pleased.) |
| possessive pronoun     | mein, dein, sein, ihr, unser, euer, Ihr            | show possession or belonging                              |
| demonstrative pronoun  | dieser, jener, der, derselbe, derjenige, solcher   | point to something                                        |
| relative pronoun       | der, die, das, welcher, welche, welches; wer, was  | introduce relative clauses: das Programm, **das** abstürzt (the program that crashes) |
| interrogative pronoun  | wer, was, welcher, was für ein                     | introduce questions                                       |
| indefinite pronoun     | man, jemand, niemand, etwas, nichts, alle, jeder, einige, manche | refer to something unspecified              |
| reciprocal pronoun     | einander, sich (gegenseitig)                       | mutual relationship: Sie helfen **einander**. (They help each other.) |

The personal pronouns are declined as follows:

| Case | ich  | du   | er  | sie (sg.) | es  | wir | ihr  | sie / Sie |
| ---- | ---- | ---- | --- | --------- | --- | --- | ---- | --------- |
| nom. | ich  | du   | er  | sie       | es  | wir | ihr  | sie / Sie |
| gen. | meiner | deiner | seiner | ihrer | seiner | unser | euer | ihrer / Ihrer |
| dat. | mir  | dir  | ihm | ihr       | ihm | uns | euch | ihnen / Ihnen |
| acc. | mich | dich | ihn | sie       | es  | uns | euch | sie / Sie |

## Adjective (Adjektiv)

Adjectives describe properties: `schnell` (fast), `neu` (new), `digital`. They can be used **attributively** (before the noun, declined: der schnelle Rechner), **predicatively** (with sein, werden, bleiben, not declined: Der Rechner ist schnell) or **adverbially** (with the verb, not declined: Der Rechner arbeitet schnell).

### Declension

Which ending an attributive adjective takes depends on what precedes it. The rule behind it: some word in the noun phrase must clearly show case and gender. If the article already shows it, a weak ending on the adjective is enough.

**Strong declension** (without an article, after numbers such as zwei, drei, after etwas, viel, wenig):

| Case | masculine    | feminine     | neuter       | plural        |
| ---- | ------------ | ------------ | ------------ | ------------- |
| nom. | gut**er** Wein | frisch**e** Milch | kalt**es** Wasser | neu**e** Geräte |
| gen. | gut**en** Weines | frisch**er** Milch | kalt**en** Wassers | neu**er** Geräte |
| dat. | gut**em** Wein | frisch**er** Milch | kalt**em** Wasser | neu**en** Geräten |
| acc. | gut**en** Wein | frisch**e** Milch | kalt**es** Wasser | neu**e** Geräte |

**Weak declension** (after the definite article and after dieser, jener, jeder, welcher, derselbe, alle):

| Case | masculine        | feminine         | neuter           | plural            |
| ---- | ---------------- | ---------------- | ---------------- | ----------------- |
| nom. | der gut**e** Wein | die frisch**e** Milch | das kalt**e** Wasser | die neu**en** Geräte |
| gen. | des gut**en** Weines | der frisch**en** Milch | des kalt**en** Wassers | der neu**en** Geräte |
| dat. | dem gut**en** Wein | der frisch**en** Milch | dem kalt**en** Wasser | den neu**en** Geräten |
| acc. | den gut**en** Wein | die frisch**e** Milch | das kalt**e** Wasser | die neu**en** Geräte |

**Mixed declension** (after ein, kein and the possessives mein, dein, unser ...):

| Case | masculine          | feminine            | neuter             | plural               |
| ---- | ------------------ | ------------------- | ------------------ | -------------------- |
| nom. | ein gut**er** Wein | eine frisch**e** Milch | ein kalt**es** Wasser | keine neu**en** Geräte |
| gen. | eines gut**en** Weines | einer frisch**en** Milch | eines kalt**en** Wassers | keiner neu**en** Geräte |
| dat. | einem gut**en** Wein | einer frisch**en** Milch | einem kalt**en** Wasser | keinen neu**en** Geräten |
| acc. | einen gut**en** Wein | eine frisch**e** Milch | ein kalt**es** Wasser | keine neu**en** Geräte |

The mixed declension is strong where `ein` has no ending (nom. masc., nom./acc. neut.) and weak everywhere else.

### Comparison (Komparation)

| Degree       | Formation           | Example                        |
| ------------ | ------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| positive     | base form           | schnell, so schnell **wie** (as fast as) |
| comparative  | -er                 | schneller, schneller **als** (faster than) |
| superlative  | am -(e)sten / der -(e)ste | am schnellsten, der schnellste (fastest) |

Many one-syllable adjectives take an umlaut (alt, älter, am ältesten; groß, größer, am größten). Irregular forms are `gut, besser, am besten` (good), `viel, mehr, am meisten` (much), `gern, lieber, am liebsten` (gladly), `hoch, höher, am höchsten` (high), `nah, näher, am nächsten` (near).

:::caution[Common mistake]
In comparisons, `wie` follows the positive and `als` follows the comparative: „Das Handy ist so teuer **wie** ein Laptop“ (as expensive as), but „Das Handy ist teurer **als** ein Laptop“ (more expensive than). Forms like `*teurer wie` are considered wrong in standard German. Some adjectives cannot meaningfully be compared (`tot`, dead; `schwanger`, pregnant; `optimal`; `einzig`, only), so `*optimalste` is wrong.
:::

## Verb

Verbs denote actions, processes and states: `programmieren` (to program), `wachsen` (to grow), `liegen` (to lie). In the sentence they form the predicate. Changing the form of a verb is called **conjugation** (Konjugation).

### Conjugation

A conjugated (finite) verb is marked for **person** (1st, 2nd, 3rd), **number** (singular, plural), **tense**, **mood** (indicative, subjunctive, imperative) and **voice** (active, passive). Forms without a personal ending are called **non-finite forms**: the infinitive (lernen), present participle (Partizip I: lernend) and past participle (Partizip II: gelernt).

| Person | Singular       | Plural          |
| ------ | -------------- | --------------- |
| 1st    | ich lern**e**  | wir lern**en**  |
| 2nd    | du lern**st**  | ihr lern**t**   |
| 3rd    | er/sie/es lern**t** | sie/Sie lern**en** |

Tenses, the passive and moods are covered in detail on the page [Verb forms](/en/languages/german/verb_forms/).

### Strong, weak and mixed verbs

| Type      | Feature                                                | Example (infinitive, preterite, past participle) |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------ |
| weak (regular) | preterite with -te, participle in -t              | machen, machte, gemacht (to make)                |
| strong (irregular) | vowel change (ablaut), participle usually in -en | singen, sang, gesungen (to sing); fahren, fuhr, gefahren (to drive) |
| mixed     | vowel change and -te / -t                              | bringen, brachte, gebracht (to bring); denken, dachte, gedacht (to think); kennen, kannte, gekannt (to know) |

You have to learn strong verbs by heart because their forms cannot be derived from the infinitive. Other mixed verbs are `nennen` (to name), `rennen` (to run), `brennen` (to burn), `senden` (to send), `wenden` (to turn) and `wissen` (to know).

### Auxiliary and modal verbs

**Auxiliary verbs** (`sein`, `haben`, `werden`) form compound tenses and the passive: Ich **habe** gelernt (I have learnt). Sie **ist** gekommen (she has come). Die Datei **wird** gelöscht (the file is being deleted). They can also be used as full verbs (Ich **bin** müde, I am tired).

Whether the perfect is formed with `sein` or `haben` depends on the verb: verbs of change of place and change of state form it with `sein` (gehen, fahren, aufwachen, sterben), most others with `haben`. In Austria and southern Germany, `sitzen`, `liegen` and `stehen` form the perfect with `sein` (Ich **bin** gesessen, I sat), in northern Germany with `haben`; both are standard.

**Modal verbs** change the meaning of another verb, which is in the infinitive without `zu`: `dürfen` (permission), `können` (ability, possibility), `mögen` (wish, preference), `müssen` (necessity), `sollen` (instruction, duty), `wollen` (intention). In the present singular they have no ending in the 1st and 3rd person (ich kann, er kann) and often a vowel change (dürfen, ich darf).

In the perfect, a modal verb used with another verb takes the substitute infinitive: Sie hat das Programm nicht installieren **können** (she was not able to install the program), not `*gekonnt`.

## Adverb

Adverbs are invariable and describe the circumstances of an action more closely. Important groups:

| Group              | Question           | Examples                                    |
| ------------------ | ------------------ | ------------------------------------------- |
| adverb of place    | Wo? Wohin? Woher? (where?) | hier, dort, oben, draußen, hinein   |
| adverb of time     | Wann? Wie lange? (when?) | heute, jetzt, bald, oft, immer, damals |
| adverb of manner   | Wie? (how?)        | gern, sehr, kaum, vielleicht, leider        |
| causal adverb      | Warum? (why?)      | deshalb, darum, trotzdem, folglich          |

Adverbs such as `deshalb` (therefore), `trotzdem` (nevertheless), `außerdem` (moreover), `dann` (then) are also called **conjunctional adverbs**: they link sentences but are themselves part of the sentence (usually in the first position) and therefore trigger verb-second order: „Es regnet, **deshalb bleibe** ich zu Hause.“ (It is raining, so I am staying at home.)

A few adverbs can be compared: `oft, öfter, am öftesten` (often), `gern, lieber, am liebsten` (gladly), `bald, eher, am ehesten` (soon).

## Preposition (Präposition)

Prepositions establish relationships between words (spatial, temporal, causal, modal). They determine the **case** of the following word (government, Rektion).

| Case                  | Prepositions                                                                  |
| --------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| accusative            | durch, für, gegen, ohne, um, bis, entlang (postposed: den Fluss entlang)      |
| dative                | aus, bei, mit, nach, seit, von, zu, gegenüber, ab, außer                      |
| genitive              | wegen, trotz, während, statt (anstatt), aufgrund, innerhalb, außerhalb, oberhalb, unterhalb, infolge, mithilfe, laut |
| two-way prepositions  | an, auf, hinter, in, neben, über, unter, vor, zwischen                        |

With **two-way prepositions** (Wechselpräpositionen): the question `Wohin?` (where to, direction) requires the accusative, the question `Wo?` (where, location) the dative: „Ich lege das Handy **auf den** Tisch“ (I put the phone on the table), „Das Handy liegt **auf dem** Tisch“ (the phone is lying on the table).

In standard German, `wegen`, `trotz` and `während` take the genitive (wegen **des** Regens, because of the rain). The dative (`wegen dem Regen`) is colloquial and should be avoided in the Matura. Preposition and article often merge: an dem to `am`, in dem to `im`, zu der to `zur`, von dem to `vom`.

## Conjunction (Konjunktion)

Conjunctions link words, phrases and clauses.

| Type                                     | Examples                                                | Effect on the sentence                                    |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| coordinating                             | und, oder, aber, denn, sondern, doch; sowohl ... als auch, weder ... noch, entweder ... oder | link elements of equal rank; a main clause stays a main clause, no effect on word order |
| subordinating (Subjunktion)              | dass, weil, da, obwohl, wenn, falls, als, nachdem, bevor, während, damit, sodass, ob, indem | introduce a subordinate clause; the finite verb goes to the end |

Compare: „Ich bleibe zu Hause, **denn** ich **bin** krank“ (main clause, verb in second position) and „Ich bleibe zu Hause, **weil** ich krank **bin**“ (subordinate clause, verb at the end). Both mean "I am staying at home because I am ill".

How subordinating conjunctions are classified by meaning (temporal, causal, concessive ...) is explained in [Sentence structure](/en/languages/german/sentence_structure/).

## Numeral (Numerale)

Numerals indicate quantities and order. Many grammars count them as adjectives because they behave similarly.

| Type                         | Examples                                       |
| ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| cardinal number              | eins, zwei, hundert, dreitausend               |
| ordinal number               | der erste, der zweite, der 3. (with a full stop) |
| fraction                     | ein Drittel (a third), drei Viertel, halb      |
| multiplicative               | doppelt (double), dreifach (threefold)         |
| iterative                    | einmal (once), dreimal (three times)           |
| indefinite numeral           | viele, wenige, einige, mehrere, alle           |

Numbers below one million are written **as one word** when spelt out (`dreihundertvierundzwanzig`, 324); millions and billions are nouns (`zwei Millionen`). In running text, numbers up to twelve are usually written out and larger numbers in digits.

## Interjection (Interjektion)

Interjections express feelings or reactions or imitate sounds: `ach`, `au`, `hurra`, `oh`, `pfui`, `hallo`, `psst`, `peng`. They usually stand outside the sentence structure and are separated by a comma or exclamation mark: „**Ach**, das habe ich vergessen.“ (Oh, I forgot that.)
