**Syntax** describes how words are put together to form sentences. It is the basis of punctuation: almost all German comma rules depend on whether you are dealing with a main or subordinate clause, an infinitive group or an insertion. It also helps you describe the sentence structure of a text in a text analysis (paratactic, hypotactic, ellipses).

## Clause elements (Satzglieder)

A **clause element** is a word or group of words that can only be moved as a unit within the sentence. The word class says what a word _is_; the clause element says what _job_ it does in the sentence.

### Movement test (Umstellprobe)

The **movement test** shows you the boundaries of clause elements: everything that can be moved together to the start of the sentence (before the finite verb) is one clause element.

> Die neue Lehrerin | erklärt | uns | morgen | den Algorithmus.
> Morgen | erklärt | die neue Lehrerin | uns | den Algorithmus.
> Den Algorithmus | erklärt | uns | die neue Lehrerin | morgen.
> (The new teacher will explain the algorithm to us tomorrow.)

`die neue Lehrerin` always stays together, so it is one clause element. The finite verb stays in second position.

### Question test (Frageprobe)

With the **question test**, you identify _which_ clause element you are looking at:

| Clause element            | Question                       | Example                                                   |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| subject                   | Wer oder was? (who or what?)   | **Der Router** blinkt. (The router is flashing.)          |
| predicate                 | Was tut / geschieht? (what happens?) | Der Router **blinkt**. Er **hat** lange **geblinkt**. |
| accusative object         | Wen oder was? (whom or what?)  | Sie repariert **den Router**. (She repairs the router.)   |
| dative object             | Wem? (to whom?)                | Er hilft **seinem Kollegen**. (He helps his colleague.)   |
| genitive object           | Wessen? (of whom?)             | Wir gedenken **der Opfer**. (We remember the victims.)    |
| prepositional object      | preposition + question word (Worauf? Woran? An wen?) | Sie wartet **auf das Update**. (She is waiting for the update.) |
| adverbial                 | Wann? Wo? Warum? Wie? (when, where, why, how?) | Sie arbeitet **seit Montag** **im Labor**. (She has been working in the lab since Monday.) |
| predicative complement    | Wer oder was ist jemand? (what is someone?) | Sie ist **Netzwerktechnikerin**. Er wird **müde**. |

### Subject and predicate

-   The **subject** is in the nominative. The finite verb agrees with it in person and number: Die Daten **werden** gesichert (the data is backed up), not `*wird`.
-   The **predicate** consists of the finite verb and any other verb parts (participle, infinitive, separated prefix). In a declarative sentence, it forms a **sentence bracket** (Satzklammer): Er **ruft** seine Mutter **an** (he phones his mother). Sie **hat** das Programm **getestet** (she has tested the program).
-   A **predicative complement** (predicate nominative or predicative adjective) completes the verbs sein, werden, bleiben, heißen: Sie **ist Informatikerin** (she is a computer scientist). Das bleibt **schwierig** (that remains difficult).

### Objects

Objects are complements required by the verb (valency). The verb determines which case is needed: `helfen` (help) requires the dative, `unterstützen` (support) the accusative, `warten` (wait) a prepositional object with `auf`.

-   **Accusative object** (most frequent): Ich lese **ein Buch**.
-   **Dative object**: Das Buch gehört **mir**. Often together with an accusative object: Sie schenkt **ihm** (dat.) **einen USB-Stick** (acc.). (She gives him a USB stick.)
-   **Genitive object**: rare and formal, with verbs such as gedenken, bedürfen, sich erinnern, anklagen: Das bedarf **einer Erklärung**. (That requires an explanation.)
-   **Prepositional object**: the preposition is fixed by the verb and has lost its literal meaning: Ich denke **an dich** (I am thinking of you). Sie interessiert sich **für Robotik** (she is interested in robotics).

:::caution[Prepositional object or adverbial?]
„Er wartet **auf den Bus**“ (he is waiting for the bus) is a prepositional object (question: Worauf wartet er? The preposition `auf` is fixed by `warten`). „Er wartet **auf dem Bahnsteig**“ (he is waiting on the platform) is an adverbial of place (question: Wo wartet er? The preposition can be replaced: am, neben dem Bahnsteig).
:::

### Adverbials (adverbiale Bestimmungen)

Adverbials state the circumstances. They can usually be left out without making the sentence ungrammatical.

| Type                      | Question                     | Example                                      |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| temporal (time)           | Wann? Wie lange? Seit wann?  | **Am Freitag** ist Schularbeit. (There is a test on Friday.) |
| local (place)             | Wo? Wohin? Woher?            | Wir treffen uns **im EDV-Saal**. (We meet in the computer room.) |
| causal (reason)           | Warum? Weshalb?              | **Wegen des Stromausfalls** fiel der Server aus. (The server failed because of the power cut.) |
| modal (manner)            | Wie? Womit?                  | Sie löst die Aufgabe **sehr elegant**.       |
| final (purpose)           | Wozu?                        | **Zur Sicherheit** speichert er doppelt. (To be safe, he saves twice.) |
| conditional (condition)   | Unter welcher Bedingung?     | **Bei Regen** fällt das Fest aus. (In case of rain, the party is cancelled.) |
| concessive (concession)   | Trotz welchen Umstands?      | **Trotz der Kälte** fuhr sie mit dem Rad. (Despite the cold, she cycled.) |
| consecutive (result)      | Mit welcher Folge?           | Er arbeitete **bis zur Erschöpfung**. (He worked to the point of exhaustion.) |
| instrumental (means)      | Womit?                       | Sie öffnet die Datei **mit einem Editor**.   |

### Attributes

An **attribute** is not a clause element but part of one: it describes a noun in more detail and moves with it in the movement test.

| Attribute                 | Example                                               |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| adjective attribute       | der **schnelle** Prozessor (the fast processor)       |
| genitive attribute        | das Handbuch **des Druckers** (the printer's manual)  |
| prepositional attribute   | der Laptop **mit dem Sprung** (the laptop with the crack) |
| apposition                | Wien, **die Hauptstadt Österreichs**, ... (Vienna, the capital of Austria, ...) |
| adverb attribute          | das Haus **dort** (the house over there)              |
| attributive clause (relative clause) | das Programm, **das ich geschrieben habe** (the program that I wrote) |

## Sentence types

| Sentence type          | Verb position | Punctuation | Example                               |
| ---------------------- | ------------- | ----------- | ------------------------------------- |
| declarative            | V2            | full stop   | Die Prüfung beginnt um acht. (The exam starts at eight.) |
| yes/no question        | V1            | question mark | Beginnt die Prüfung um acht?        |
| wh-question            | V2            | question mark | Wann beginnt die Prüfung?           |
| imperative             | V1            | exclamation mark or full stop | Komm pünktlich! Bitte schließen Sie die Tür. |
| exclamative            | V1, V2 or VL  | exclamation mark | Ist das schön! Wie schön das ist! (How beautiful that is!) |
| optative (wish)        | V1 or VL (Konj. II) | exclamation mark | Hätte ich doch gelernt! Wenn ich doch gelernt hätte! (If only I had studied!) |

A **rhetorical question** has the form of a question but expects no answer because the answer is already clear (see [stylistic devices](/en/languages/german_text_types/stylistic_devices/)).

## Main and subordinate clauses

-   A **main clause** (Hauptsatz) can stand alone. The finite verb is in second position (statement, wh-question) or first position (yes/no question, imperative).
-   A **subordinate clause** (Nebensatz) cannot stand alone; it depends on a superordinate clause. It is usually introduced by a subordinating conjunction, a relative pronoun or a question word, and the finite verb goes **at the end**.

> Main clause: Ich **bleibe** zu Hause. (I am staying at home.)
> Subordinate clause: ..., weil ich krank **bin**. (... because I am ill.)

Depending on its position, a subordinate clause is called a **preceding clause** (Vordersatz: Weil ich krank bin, bleibe ich zu Hause.), an **embedded clause** (Zwischensatz: Ich bleibe, weil ich krank bin, zu Hause.) or a **following clause** (Nachsatz).

### Compound and complex sentences

| German term             | Structure                                | Technical term (text analysis) | Example                                |
| ----------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ | -------------------------------------- |
| einfacher Satz (simple sentence) | one main clause                 |                                | Der Akku ist leer. (The battery is flat.) |
| Satzreihe (compound sentence) | main clause + main clause          | parataxis (paratactic)         | Der Akku ist leer, das Handy geht nicht. |
| Satzgefüge (complex sentence) | main clause + at least one subordinate clause | hypotaxis (hypotactic) | Das Handy geht nicht, weil der Akku leer ist. |

A complex sentence can have several levels: a first-degree subordinate clause depends on the main clause, a second-degree subordinate clause on the first-degree clause. Many nested subordinate clauses make texts hard to read; in non-fiction texts, two levels are usually enough.

## Types of subordinate clause

Subordinate clauses can be classified by their **form** (how are they introduced?) and by their **function** (which clause element do they replace?).

### By form

| Type                      | Introduced by                                   | Example                                                |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| relative clause           | relative pronoun der, die, das, welcher; wer, was, wo | Das ist der Server, **der** ständig abstürzt. (That is the server that keeps crashing.) |
| conjunctional clause      | subordinating conjunction (dass, weil, obwohl, wenn ...) | Ich hoffe, **dass** das Update funktioniert. |
| indirect question         | ob (whether) or a question word                 | Sie fragt, **ob** er kommt. Ich weiß nicht, **wann** er kommt. |
| infinitive group          | zu + infinitive (often with um, ohne, statt)    | Sie ging, **ohne sich zu verabschieden**. (She left without saying goodbye.) |
| participle group          | present or past participle with extension       | **Vom Lärm geweckt**, stand er auf. (Woken by the noise, he got up.) |
| clause without introducer | no introducing word, verb in first position     | **Hätte ich Zeit**, käme ich mit. (= Wenn ich Zeit hätte; if I had time, I would come.) |

Infinitive and participle groups have no finite verb and are therefore not complete clauses but **clause-like groups**. For punctuation, they are treated similarly (see [Punctuation](/en/languages/german/punctuation/)).

### Conjunctional clauses by meaning

| Type         | Conjunctions                                   | Example                                                   |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| temporal     | als, wenn, während, nachdem, bevor, seit, sobald, bis | **Nachdem** sie gespeichert hatte, schloss sie das Programm. (After she had saved, she closed the program.) |
| causal       | weil, da                                       | Er fehlt, **weil** er krank ist.                          |
| conditional  | wenn, falls, sofern                            | **Falls** es regnet, bleiben wir drinnen.                 |
| concessive   | obwohl, obgleich, wenngleich                   | **Obwohl** er gelernt hat, ist er nervös. (Although he has studied, he is nervous.) |
| final        | damit, um ... zu                               | Sie spart, **damit** sie sich einen Laptop kaufen kann.   |
| consecutive  | sodass (so dass), so ..., dass                 | Es war so laut, **dass** niemand etwas verstand.          |
| modal / instrumental | indem, dadurch dass, ohne dass         | Er löste das Problem, **indem** er neu startete. (He solved the problem by restarting.) |
| adversative  | während, wohingegen                            | Sie liebt Mathe, **während** er Sprachen bevorzugt.       |
| comparative  | als, wie, als ob                               | Er tut so, **als ob** er nichts wüsste. (He acts as if he knew nothing.) |

`als` is used for single events in the past (**Als** ich zehn war, ..., when I was ten), `wenn` for repeated or future events (**Immer wenn** ich ihn sehe, ..., whenever I see him).

### By function

| Function            | replaces             | Example                                                   |
| ------------------- | -------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| subject clause      | the subject          | **Wer lernt**, besteht die Prüfung. (Whoever studies passes the exam.) |
| object clause       | an object            | Ich weiß, **dass du recht hast**. (I know that you are right.) |
| adverbial clause    | an adverbial         | **Weil es regnet**, bleiben wir hier.                     |
| attributive clause  | an attribute         | Das Buch, **das du mir geliehen hast**, ist spannend.     |

## Verb position

The position of the finite verb is the most important ordering principle in German. There are three basic positions:

| Position              | Where is the finite verb? | Sentence types                                                    | Example                               |
| --------------------- | ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| verb-first (V1)       | first position            | yes/no question, imperative, conditional clause without introducer | **Hast** du Zeit? **Komm** her!      |
| verb-second (V2)      | second position           | declarative sentence, wh-question                                 | Morgen **habe** ich Zeit.             |
| verb-final (VL)       | at the end                | introduced subordinate clauses                                    | ..., weil ich morgen Zeit **habe**.   |

### The topological field model

In a declarative sentence, the finite verb and the other predicate parts form a **sentence bracket**. This results in five fields:

| Prefield (Vorfeld) | left bracket | middle field (Mittelfeld)       | right bracket | postfield (Nachfeld)     |
| ------------------ | ------------ | ------------------------------- | ------------- | ------------------------ |
| Gestern            | hat          | sie das Programm endlich        | getestet,     | weil die Frist ablief.   |
|                    | Hat          | sie das Programm                | getestet?     |                          |
| Er                 | ruft         | seine Mutter jeden Sonntag      | an.           |                          |

-   The **prefield** contains exactly one clause element (any one). This explains why the verb follows immediately after `deshalb` or `trotzdem`: the adverb occupies the prefield.
-   In a subordinate clause, the introducing word is in the left bracket and all verb parts are in the right one: ..., **weil** sie das Programm getestet **hat**.
-   Mainly subordinate clauses and comparisons move into the **postfield** (extraposition). A middle field that is too long makes sentences hard to understand because the reader has to wait a long time for the second part of the bracket.
