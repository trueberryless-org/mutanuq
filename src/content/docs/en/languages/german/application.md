At the latest for the **compulsory internship** (Pflichtpraktikum), you need a complete application at the HTL. An application is advertising for yourself: it should show that you are a good fit for the position and get you an interview. HR staff often read dozens of applications and decide quickly which ones to look at more closely. This is why a clear structure, a concrete link to the position and error-free language count.

## Parts of an application

| Part                       | Content                                                            |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| cover letter (Bewerbungsschreiben, Anschreiben) | why this position, why you, why this company; one page at most |
| CV (Lebenslauf)            | tabular overview of education, experience and skills               |
| attachments (Anlagen)      | school reports (the most recent annual reports), internship confirmations, certificates (e.g. Cisco, language certificates) |
| optional                   | cover page, letter of motivation, link to projects (GitHub, portfolio) |

## Cover letter

The **cover letter** is a formal business letter. In Austria, **ÖNORM A 1080** governs the layout of written documents; in Germany it is DIN 5008. For school purposes, it is enough if all the elements are present and clearly arranged.

### Structure

| Element              | Notes                                                                       |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| sender               | name, address, phone number, a professional e-mail address                  |
| recipient            | company, contact person, address                                            |
| place and date       | Linz, 3. Februar 2026                                                       |
| subject line         | without the word „Betreff“, in bold: **Bewerbung um ein Pflichtpraktikum als Softwareentwickler, Juli und August 2026** (application for a compulsory internship as a software developer, July and August 2026) |
| salutation           | with a name if possible: Sehr geehrte Frau Mag. Berger,                     |
| introduction         | reference to the position and the occasion, a strong first sentence         |
| main part            | qualifications with evidence, link to the requirements, motivation for this company |
| conclusion           | availability, wish for a personal interview                                 |
| closing              | Mit freundlichen Grüßen (Kind regards)                                      |
| signature            | handwritten or scanned, with your name below                                |
| attachments          | CV, school reports                                                          |

### Content

-   **Introduction:** avoid set phrases such as „Hiermit bewerbe ich mich ...“ (I hereby apply ...). Instead, say where you found out about the position or what appeals to you about the company: „Ihre Anzeige auf karriere.at hat mich sofort angesprochen, weil ich in meinem Diplomprojekt bereits eine REST-Schnittstelle mit ASP.NET entwickelt habe.“ (Your advert on karriere.at appealed to me immediately because I have already developed a REST interface with ASP.NET in my diploma project.)
-   **Main part:** match your skills to the requirements in the job advert and back them up with examples from school, projects, internships or your free time. „Ich bin teamfähig“ (I am a team player) convinces nobody; „Im Schulprojekt X habe ich in einem Team aus vier Personen die Datenbank entworfen und die Aufgabenplanung übernommen“ (in school project X, I designed the database and took over task planning in a team of four) does.
-   **Motivation:** show that you have found out about the company (products, projects, values).
-   **Conclusion:** confident but not arrogant: „Über eine Einladung zu einem persönlichen Gespräch freue ich mich.“ (I look forward to an invitation to an interview.) Piling up subjunctives as in „Ich würde mich freuen, wenn ...“ sounds unsure.

:::tip[Checklist]
One page, contact person's name spelt correctly, no spelling mistakes (have someone proofread it), each cover letter adapted to the specific position, the same font and layout as in the CV.
:::

### Letter of motivation

A **letter of motivation** (Motivationsschreiben) is required above all for university places, scholarships, stays abroad or as a supplement to the cover letter. It is usually longer (one to two pages) and focuses on your **reasons**: why are you interested in this subject or programme? What has shaped you so far? What goals are you pursuing? The structure follows the cover letter; the tone is a little more personal but remains factual.

## CV (Lebenslauf)

### Tabular CV

The **tabular CV** is standard in Austria. It is in **reverse chronological order**: the most recent item comes first.

| Section                    | Content                                                                 |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| personal details           | name, address, phone, e-mail; date of birth and nationality are common but optional |
| work experience / internships | period, company, place, activity with concrete tasks                 |
| education                  | period, school, specialisation (HTL für Informationstechnologie, area of specialisation), expected qualification, topic of the diploma thesis |
| skills                     | IT skills (programming languages, operating systems, tools), languages with CEFR level (A1 to C2), driving licence |
| activities and interests   | pupil representative, clubs, volunteering, projects; concrete rather than "reading, meeting friends" |
| ending (optional)          | place, date, signature                                                  |

A **photo** is customary in Austria but not compulsory. If you use one, it should be a recent, professional portrait. Information on religion, marital status or health does not belong in a CV.

You do not need to hide gaps (such as a repeated year or a change of school); a short factual note is better than an unexplained jump.

### Europass CV

The **Europass CV** is a standard format of the European Union. You create it free of charge online on the Europass platform and can export it as a PDF. It is particularly useful for applications abroad, for Erasmus+ and with international companies because it is known and comparable throughout Europe. Language skills are self-assessed there according to the **Common European Framework of Reference for Languages** (CEFR) for listening, reading, speaking and writing. Disadvantage: it is often longer and less individual than a CV you design yourself.

## Applying for the compulsory internship

The HTL curriculum requires a **compulsory internship** in a company in the relevant field (at least eight weeks during the school holidays before entering the fifth year). For your application this means:

-   **start early:** many companies fill summer internships as early as winter.
-   **state the period:** the exact weeks, and that it is a **compulsory internship according to the curriculum** (Pflichtpraktikum laut Lehrplan).
-   **name your specialisation:** Systemtechnik or Medientechnik, with matching projects and skills.
-   **speculative application:** you can apply even without an advertised position. In that case, find a contact person and explain why this particular company interests you.
-   **clarify the conditions:** working hours, pay, activities and the confirmation the school needs after the internship.

Example opening sentence:

> Im Rahmen meiner Ausbildung an der HTL für Informationstechnologie absolviere ich im Sommer 2026 ein achtwöchiges Pflichtpraktikum. Da Ihr Unternehmen Netzwerklösungen für Gemeinden umsetzt und ich mich im Ausbildungsschwerpunkt Systemtechnik besonders für Netzwerke interessiere, bewerbe ich mich für den Zeitraum vom 6. Juli bis 28. August 2026.
> (As part of my training at the HTL for information technology, I am completing an eight-week compulsory internship in summer 2026. As your company implements network solutions for municipalities and I am particularly interested in networks in my Systemtechnik specialisation, I am applying for the period from 6 July to 28 August 2026.)

## Job interview

### Preparation

-   **research the company:** products, customers, size, locations, current projects
-   **know your own documents:** anything in your CV can come up.
-   think about **answers to typical questions** and practise them aloud
-   prepare **your own questions** (tasks, team, induction)
-   **plan how to get there**, arrive ten minutes early; for online interviews, test the technology, lighting and background
-   **clothing:** neat and one level more formal than is usual at the company

### Typical questions

| Question                                        | What matters                                                         |
| ----------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Erzählen Sie etwas über sich. (Tell me about yourself.) | a short summary related to the position (one to two minutes), not reading out your CV |
| Warum möchten Sie bei uns arbeiten? (Why do you want to work for us?) | show knowledge of the company and genuine interest |
| Was sind Ihre Stärken und Schwächen? (What are your strengths and weaknesses?) | back up strengths with examples; name a real but non-critical weakness and say how you are working on it |
| Erzählen Sie von einem Projekt, auf das Sie stolz sind. (Tell me about a project you are proud of.) | situation, task, your approach, result (STAR method) |
| Wo sehen Sie sich in fünf Jahren? (Where do you see yourself in five years?) | realistic goals that fit the position |
| Haben Sie noch Fragen? (Do you have any questions?) | always ask at least one question                          |

The **STAR method** helps you tell examples in a structured way: **S**ituation, **T**ask, **A**ction (what you did), **R**esult.

Under the **Equal Treatment Act** (Gleichbehandlungsgesetz), questions about pregnancy, religion, sexual orientation or political views are generally not permitted because they can lead to discrimination. You do not have to answer them.

### After the interview

Say thank you (for example in a short e-mail) and follow up if you have not heard anything by the date given. If you are rejected, you can politely ask for feedback.

## Applying by e-mail

Most applications today are sent by e-mail or through an online portal.

-   **Sender address:** professional, ideally firstname.lastname (not `gamer2008@...`).
-   **Subject line:** meaningful, e.g. „Bewerbung Pflichtpraktikum Softwareentwicklung, Juli bis August 2026, Lena Gruber“.
-   **E-mail text:** short, with a salutation, two or three sentences on your request, a reference to the attachments and a closing with contact details. Alternatively, the full cover letter goes directly in the e-mail, in which case you do not have to attach it as well.
-   **Attachments:** as **PDF**, ideally in a single file (cover letter, CV, reports), a few megabytes in total; meaningful file names such as `Gruber_Lena_Bewerbung.pdf`.
-   **Check:** contact person's name, company name (no leftovers from another application), attachments actually attached; send a test e-mail to yourself first.
-   **Online profiles:** HR staff often also look at what can be found about you online. A well-kept LinkedIn or GitHub profile can be an advantage.

General rules for formal e-mails in English can be found under [E-mail](/en/languages/text_types/email/).
