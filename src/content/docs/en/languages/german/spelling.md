German spelling is laid down in the **Official Rules** (Amtliches Regelwerk) of the Council for German Orthography (Rat für deutsche Rechtschreibung). They apply in Germany, Austria, Switzerland, Liechtenstein, South Tyrol and the German-speaking Community of Belgium. Since 1 July 2024, the revised version with a new word list has been binding for schools and public administration. In Austria, the **Österreichisches Wörterbuch** (ÖWB, currently in its 44th edition) is the official reference dictionary for schools; the Duden is also permitted. In the SRDP, spelling mistakes count towards the criterion "normative language accuracy" (Normative Sprachrichtigkeit).

## Capitalisation (Groß- und Kleinschreibung)

### Basic rules

The following are capitalised:

-   the first word of a sentence and of a heading
-   **nouns** and **proper names**: der Computer, Österreich, die Donau
-   **nominalisations**: words of other classes used as nouns
-   the **polite form of address** `Sie` in all its forms
-   after a **colon** if a complete sentence follows: Er sagte: „**Wir** fangen an.“ Merke: **Übung** macht den Meister. (Practice makes perfect.)

### Nominalisation

A word becomes a noun if it has, or could have, a **determiner**. Typical signals are:

| Signal                                | Examples                                                     |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| article                               | das **Lesen** (reading), die **Kleinen** (the little ones), ein **Ja** (a yes) |
| preposition (merged with article)     | beim **Programmieren** (while programming), zum **Lernen**, im **Allgemeinen** (in general), ins **Schwarze** (bullseye) |
| quantity words: etwas, nichts, viel, wenig, alles | etwas **Neues** (something new), nichts **Wichtiges** (nothing important), alles **Gute** (all the best), viel **Interessantes** |
| possessive, demonstrative pronoun     | dein **Zögern** (your hesitation), dieses **Hin und Her** (this back and forth) |
| preceding adjective                   | lautes **Schreien** (loud shouting), das schnelle **Tippen** (fast typing) |

These stay lowercase despite an article or quantity word:

-   numeral-like words such as `viel`, `wenig`, `ein`, `andere` in their usual meaning: die **meisten** (most), die **wenigsten**, die **beiden** (both), die **vielen**. With `der andere / der Andere`, both spellings are allowed.
-   superlatives with `am` that answer the question `Wie?`: Sie löst die Aufgabe **am schnellsten**. (She solves the task fastest.)
-   pronouns: Das hat **jeder** gewusst. Die **beiden** kommen.
-   adjectives that refer to a noun mentioned before or after: Von den Laptops nehme ich den **neuen**. (Of the laptops, I will take the new one.)

Fixed pairs referring to people are capitalised: **Jung** und **Alt** (young and old), **Arm** und **Reich** (rich and poor).

### Times of day and days of the week

| Word class   | Rule                                        | Examples                                            |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| noun         | capitalised after heute, gestern, morgen, vorgestern, übermorgen | heute **Abend** (this evening), gestern **Nacht** (last night), morgen **Früh** or morgen **früh** (tomorrow morning) |
| noun         | capitalised with an article or preposition  | am **Abend**, eines **Morgens**, gegen **Mittag**   |
| compound     | day + time of day: capitalised and one word | am **Montagabend** (on Monday evening), (am) **Freitagnachmittag** |
| adverb       | ending -s: lowercase                        | **abends** (in the evenings), **montags** (on Mondays), **montagabends**, **nachts** |

### Pronouns of address

-   The polite form `Sie` and its forms (Ihnen, Ihr, Ihre) are **always** capitalised: Können **Sie** mir **Ihre** Unterlagen schicken? (Could you send me your documents?)
-   The familiar forms `du`, `dich`, `dein`, `ihr`, `euch`, `euer` are **lowercase**. In letters and e-mails, capitals are also allowed: Ich danke **dir** / **Dir** für **deine** / **Deine** Hilfe.
-   The reflexive pronoun `sich` is always lowercase: Bitte setzen Sie **sich**. (Please sit down.)

### Languages, proper names and adjectives

-   Language names are capitalised as nouns and lowercase as adjectives: Sie spricht gut **Deutsch**. Der Text ist auf **Englisch**. But: die **deutsche** Sprache.
-   Adjectives in proper names and titles are capitalised: der **Erste** Weltkrieg (the First World War), die **Vereinten** Nationen (the United Nations), das **Rote** Kreuz, der **Nahe** Osten (the Middle East).
-   Adjectives in -isch and -sch derived from personal names are lowercase, or capitalised with an apostrophe: die **ohmsche** Regel or die **Ohm'sche** Regel.

## Writing words together or apart (Getrennt- und Zusammenschreibung)

The basic rule is: **word groups are written apart, compounds are written as one word.** You can often tell a compound by its stress (the main stress is on the first part) and by its meaning (a new, figurative overall meaning suggests writing it as one word).

| Combination             | Rule                                                         | Examples                                                  |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| verb + verb             | apart                                                        | spazieren gehen (go for a walk), schwimmen lernen, lesen üben |
|                         | with bleiben, lassen in a figurative sense also together     | sitzen bleiben (stay seated), sitzenbleiben or sitzen bleiben (repeat a year) |
|                         | kennenlernen: both spellings                                 | kennenlernen or kennen lernen (get to know)               |
| noun + verb             | usually apart                                                | Rad fahren (cycle), Auto fahren, Klavier spielen          |
|                         | together if the noun has faded                               | eislaufen (ice-skate), kopfstehen, leidtun, standhalten, teilnehmen |
| adjective + verb        | apart if the adjective can be compared or extended           | schnell laufen, laut lesen                                |
|                         | together with a new overall meaning                          | krankschreiben (sign off sick), schwarzfahren (dodge the fare), festnehmen (arrest), richtigstellen (correct) |
|                         | both for the result of an action                             | kaputt machen or kaputtmachen, sauber machen or saubermachen |
| particle + verb         | together (separable verbs)                                   | abschalten, hinausgehen, zurückgeben, zusammenarbeiten    |
| adjective/participle + adjective | together in fixed combinations, otherwise often both | hellblau, nasskalt; schwer verständlich or schwerverständlich |
| noun + participle       | together if a word is saved                                  | computergesteuert (= vom Computer gesteuert), angsterfüllt |

Some frequent cases: `zurzeit` (= currently) but `zur Zeit Maria Theresias` (in the time of Maria Theresa); `infrage` or `in Frage`; `zugrunde` or `zu Grunde`; `mithilfe` or `mit Hilfe`; `irgendwann`, `irgendetwas` always one word; `so viel`, `wie viel` apart, but the conjunction `soviel` (Soviel ich weiß, ..., as far as I know).

## s, ss and ß

| Sound                                   | Spelling | Examples                                     |
| --------------------------------------- | -------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| voiceless s after a **long** vowel or diphthong | ß  | Straße, Fuß, groß, heißen, außen, beißen     |
| voiceless s after a **short** vowel     | ss       | Fluss, Kuss, Schloss, wissen, müssen, dass   |
| voiced s (as in "Rose")                 | s        | Rose, Hase, lesen, Reise                     |
| at the end of a word if related forms have a voiced s | s | Haus (Häuser), Gras (Gräser), las (lesen) |

Changes within a word family follow vowel length: wissen, er **weiß**, er **wusste**; fließen, der **Fluss**; genießen, der **Genuss**. In capitals, you write `SS` or, since 2017, also the capital `ẞ`: STRASSE or STRAẞE.

## das or dass

| Word    | Word class                               | Substitution test                      | Example                                   |
| ------- | ---------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| das     | article                                  | dieses, ein                            | **Das** Programm läuft.                   |
| das     | relative pronoun                         | welches                                | Das Programm, **das** ich geschrieben habe, ... |
| das     | demonstrative pronoun                    | dies, dieses                           | **Das** weiß ich nicht.                   |
| dass    | conjunction (introduces a subordinate clause) | cannot be replaced                | Ich hoffe, **dass** es funktioniert.      |

Remember: if you can replace the word with `dieses`, `welches` or `dies`, write `das`; otherwise write `dass`. After verbs of saying, thinking and feeling (sagen, glauben, hoffen, wissen), `dass` usually follows.

## Loanwords (Fremdwörter)

Loanwords often keep their original spelling (Computer, Software, Chance, Restaurant). Many, however, have been adapted to German spelling, or there are two permitted variants:

| Original spelling   | adapted spelling (permitted) |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Delphin             | Delfin                       |
| Photographie        | Fotografie                   |
| Portemonnaie        | Portmonee                    |
| Exposé              | Exposee                      |
| Panther             | Panter                       |

With the 2024 version, some rarely used Germanised variants were removed from the word list, such as `Jogurt`, `Ketschup` and `Majonäse`. Only `Joghurt`, `Ketchup` and `Mayonnaise` are correct now.

English compounds made of nouns are written **as one word** or with a **hyphen**, never apart: Homeoffice, Desktop-PC, Open-Source-Software, Know-how, E-Mail. Verbs from English are conjugated the German way: downloaden, ich habe die Datei **downgeloadet**; updaten, **upgedatet**.

## Hyphen (Bindestrich)

The hyphen (`-`) joins parts of words:

-   in compounds with **digits**, **single letters** and **abbreviations**: 5-mal, 3-Zimmer-Wohnung, x-Achse, IT-Abteilung, Kfz-Werkstatt, USB-Stick, 8-jährig (but `achtjährig`)
-   to **link** multi-part compounds throughout: Max-Planck-Gymnasium, Open-Source-Lizenz, Erste-Hilfe-Kurs, 100-Meter-Lauf
-   to **structure** confusing words and for emphasis: Ich-Erzähler (first-person narrator), Lotto-Annahmestelle, Tee-Ei, das Entweder-oder
-   as a **suspension hyphen** for an omitted part of a word: Hin- und Rückfahrt (outward and return trip), Ein- und Ausgabe, Datenbank- und Netzwerktechnik

After the hyphen, normal capitalisation applies: das Make-up, die Ad-hoc-Lösung, der Desktop-Computer.

## Long and short vowels (Dehnung und Schärfung)

### Long vowels (Dehnung)

Most long vowels are **not** marked (Name, Tal, Hose, Brot). If they are, it is done like this:

| Marking               | Examples                                     | Note                                      |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| ie                    | Liebe, Brief, viel, spielen                  | most common spelling for long i           |
| lengthening h         | fahren, Lehrer, ohne, Uhr, Bühne             | almost only before l, m, n, r             |
| double vowel          | Meer, Boot, Saal, Haar, Idee                 | only with a, e, o; umlauts are not doubled: Säle, Bötchen |
| long i without e      | Maschine, Kino, Tiger, mir, dir              | especially in loanwords                   |

### Short vowels (Schärfung)

After a **short, stressed** vowel, a double consonant usually follows if only one consonant is heard: kommen, Mutter, rennen, hoffen. Instead of `kk` you write **ck** (Zucker, packen), instead of `zz` you write **tz** (Katze, sitzen). Exceptions are loanwords (Pizza, Mokka) and some short words (an, in, mit, ab, das, bis).

If two different consonants already follow, there is no doubling: Hand, Kante, Hemd.

## Common mistakes

| wrong                 | correct            | Explanation                                             |
| --------------------- | ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Standart              | Standard           | from English standard                                   |
| nähmlich              | nämlich            | no lengthening h                                        |
| Rytmus, Rhytmus       | Rhythmus           | two h                                                   |
| Reperatur             | Reparatur          | from Latin reparare                                     |
| tod krank, todlachen  | todkrank, sich totlachen | `tod-` from the noun Tod (death), `tot-` from the adjective tot (dead) (but: todernst, todmüde) |
| seit ihr fertig?      | seid ihr fertig?   | `seid` is a form of sein, `seit` is a preposition or conjunction (since) |
| wiederspiegeln, Wiederstand | widerspiegeln, Widerstand | `wider` = against, `wieder` = again           |
| entgültig             | endgültig          | from Ende (end)                                         |
| endscheiden, endwickeln | entscheiden, entwickeln | prefix ent- without d                            |
| Vorraussetzung        | Voraussetzung      | voraus with one r                                       |
| Packet                | Paket              | one k, no ck                                            |
| Maschiene             | Maschine           | long i without e (loanword)                             |
| Gallerie              | Galerie            | one l                                                   |
| des weiteren          | des Weiteren       | nominalisation after an article                         |
| im voraus             | im Voraus          | nominalisation after a preposition with article         |

For some words, two spellings are correct: `selbstständig / selbständig` (independent), `aufwendig / aufwändig` (elaborate), `Recht haben / recht haben` (to be right), `Potenzial / Potential`.

## Austrian features

German is a **pluricentric language** with several equally valid standard varieties (see [Media and language](/en/languages/german/media_and_language/)). Austrian Standard German is neither a dialect nor a mistake: words listed as Austrian in the ÖWB are correct in the Matura.

### Grammar and pronunciation

-   Perfect of sitzen, liegen, stehen with `sein`: Ich **bin** gesessen. (I sat.)
-   Different gender: **das** Cola, **das** E-Mail (both alongside the form with `die`), **der** Polster (cushion).
-   Linking s where Germany usually has none: **Schweinsbraten** (Schweinebraten, roast pork), **Zugsverspätung** (Zugverspätung, train delay), **Rindsuppe** (Rinderbrühe, beef broth).
-   Telling the time: **viertel acht** for 7:15, **dreiviertel acht** for 7:45.
-   Diminutives with **-erl** in colloquial speech (Sackerl, Packerl); in the standard language **-chen** or **-lein**.

In Austria, people speak of the **Bundesland** (federal province), **Landeshauptmann** or **Landeshauptfrau** (provincial governor), **Nationalrat** and **Bundesrat**; such terms from administration and law are also part of the Austrian standard.
