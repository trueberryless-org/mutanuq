English has three time levels (present, past, future) and four aspects: **simple**, **progressive** (also called _continuous_), **perfect** and **perfect progressive**. Together they give twelve tense forms. The aspect expresses how the speaker sees an action: as a fact or habit (simple), as in progress (progressive) or as completed with reference to a later point in time (perfect). German has no progressive form and uses its Perfekt for completed past actions, which is where most mistakes come from.

## Overview

| Tense                          | Form                                     | Example                                    |
| ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Present simple                 | base form, 3rd person `-s`               | She **works** in Linz.                     |
| Present progressive            | `am/is/are` + `-ing`                     | She **is working** on a project.           |
| Present perfect                | `have/has` + past participle             | She **has worked** here for two years.     |
| Present perfect progressive    | `have/has been` + `-ing`                 | She **has been working** all day.          |
| Past simple                    | `-ed` or irregular form                  | She **worked** late yesterday.             |
| Past progressive               | `was/were` + `-ing`                      | She **was working** when I called.         |
| Past perfect                   | `had` + past participle                  | She **had worked** there before.           |
| Past perfect progressive       | `had been` + `-ing`                      | She **had been working** for hours.        |
| Future simple                  | `will` + base form                       | She **will work** from home tomorrow.      |
| Future progressive             | `will be` + `-ing`                       | She **will be working** at 9 am.           |
| Future perfect                 | `will have` + past participle            | She **will have finished** by Friday.      |
| Future perfect progressive     | `will have been` + `-ing`                | She **will have been working** here for ten years. |

Negatives and questions are formed with the first auxiliary (`is not working`, `Has she worked ...?`). Only the present simple and the past simple need the auxiliary `do/does/did`: `She doesn't work.`, `Did she work?`

## Present

### Present simple

**Form:** base form; in the 3rd person singular (`he`, `she`, `it`) add `-s`. After `s`, `ss`, `sh`, `ch`, `x`, `o` add `-es` (`watches`, `goes`); consonant + `y` becomes `-ies` (`tries`). Negative: `do not/does not` + base form.

**Use:**

-   habits and regular actions: `I check my emails every morning.`
-   general facts and laws of nature: `Water boils at 100 °C.`
-   timetables and fixed programmes (also with future reference): `The train leaves at 7:15.`
-   states with [stative verbs](#stative-verbs): `I know the answer.`

**Signal words:** `always`, `usually`, `often`, `sometimes`, `rarely`, `never`, `every day/week`, `on Mondays`

### Present progressive

**Form:** `am/is/are` + verb with `-ing`. Spelling: a silent `-e` is dropped (`make` becomes `making`), a single stressed final consonant after a short vowel is doubled (`run` becomes `running`, `begin` becomes `beginning`), `-ie` becomes `-y` (`lie` becomes `lying`).

**Use:**

-   action at the moment of speaking: `I'm writing an email right now.`
-   temporary situations: `He is living with his parents this year.`
-   developments and changes: `Prices are rising.`
-   fixed future arrangements: `We are meeting the client on Friday.`
-   annoying habits with `always`: `You are always interrupting me!`

**Signal words:** `now`, `right now`, `at the moment`, `currently`, `today`, `this week`, `Look!`, `Listen!`

### Present perfect simple

**Form:** `have/has` + past participle (3rd form: `worked`, `written`, `been`).

**Use:** The present perfect connects the past with the present. The exact time in the past is unimportant or unknown.

-   the result is visible or relevant now: `I have lost my key.` (I do not have it now.)
-   experiences in your life so far: `Have you ever been to London?`
-   a state that began in the past and still continues: `I have known her since 2019.`
-   a period of time that is not finished yet: `I have written three emails today.`

**Signal words:** `already`, `yet`, `just`, `ever`, `never`, `so far`, `up to now`, `recently`, `lately`, `since`, `for`, `this week/year`

### Present perfect progressive

**Form:** `have/has been` + `-ing`.

**Use:** emphasises the **duration** of an activity that continues up to now or has just stopped and left traces.

-   `I have been waiting for you for 20 minutes.`
-   `Your eyes are red. Have you been crying?`

Compare: `I have written five pages.` (result, how much) versus `I have been writing all morning.` (duration, how long).

**Signal words:** `for`, `since`, `all day`, `how long`, `lately`, `recently`

## Past

### Past simple

**Form:** regular verbs `-ed` (`worked`; `stop` becomes `stopped`, British `travel` becomes `travelled`, American `traveled`), irregular verbs 2nd form (`went`, `saw`). Negatives and questions with `did` + base form: `I didn't go.`, `Did you go?`

**Use:** a completed action at a specific, finished time in the past, also sequences of actions in stories.

-   `We launched the website last Monday.`
-   `He opened the laptop, logged in and checked the logs.`

**Signal words:** `yesterday`, `last week/year`, `ago`, `in 2020`, `when`, `then`, `at 5 o'clock`

### Past progressive

**Form:** `was/were` + `-ing`.

**Use:**

-   an action in progress at a point in the past: `At 8 pm I was watching a film.`
-   a background action interrupted by a short action (past simple): `I was installing the update when the power went off.`
-   simultaneous actions: `While I was coding, my sister was studying.`

**Signal words:** `while`, `when`, `as`, `at that time`, `at 8 pm yesterday`

### Past perfect simple

**Form:** `had` + past participle.

**Use:** the past before the past; an action completed **before** another action in the past.

-   `When I arrived, the meeting had already started.`
-   `She had never used Linux before she came to the HTL.`

**Signal words:** `already`, `before`, `after`, `by the time`, `never ... before`, `just`

### Past perfect progressive

**Form:** `had been` + `-ing`.

**Use:** the duration of an activity up to a point in the past.

-   `He had been working on the bug for three hours before he found it.`

## Future

### Future simple with will

**Form:** `will` + base form, negative `won't`.

**Use:**

-   spontaneous decisions: `The phone is ringing. I'll get it.`
-   predictions based on opinion or experience: `I think it will rain tomorrow.`
-   promises, offers, threats: `I'll help you with your homework.`
-   fixed facts in the future: `I will be 18 next year.`

**Signal words:** `probably`, `perhaps`, `I think`, `I'm sure`, `I expect`, `tomorrow`, `next week`, `in 2030`

### Future progressive

**Form:** `will be` + `-ing`.

**Use:** an action that will be in progress at a point in the future, or a normal, expected course of events: `This time tomorrow I will be sitting in the exam.`

### Future perfect

**Form:** `will have` + past participle.

**Use:** an action that will be completed by a point in the future: `By Friday we will have fixed all critical bugs.`

**Signal words:** `by` + point in time, `by then`, `by the time`

### Future perfect progressive

**Form:** `will have been` + `-ing`.

**Use:** the duration of an activity up to a point in the future: `In June I will have been studying at this school for five years.`

## Future forms compared

| Form                   | Use                                                  | Example                                      |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| `will`                 | spontaneous decision, opinion, promise               | I'll send you the file now.                  |
| `going to`             | intention decided before                             | I'm going to study computer science.         |
| `going to`             | prediction based on visible evidence                 | Look at the clouds. It's going to rain.      |
| Present progressive    | fixed arrangement with time and place                | I'm meeting Anna at 6 tonight.               |
| Present simple         | timetable, schedule, official programme              | The exam starts at 8 am.                     |

:::caution[No future in time clauses]
After `when`, `if`, `as soon as`, `before`, `after`, `until` and `unless`, English uses the present simple (or present perfect), not `will`, even if the action is in the future: `I'll call you when I **arrive**.` (not: ~~when I will arrive~~).
:::

## Present perfect or past simple

In spoken German, people usually tell stories in the Perfekt: „Ich habe ihn gestern gesehen.“ In English, the choice does not depend on whether you speak or write but on the **time reference**:

| Past simple                                              | Present perfect                                         |
| -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| finished point or period of time                         | period up to now or no time mentioned                   |
| `I saw him yesterday.`                                   | `I have seen him.` (When does not matter.)              |
| `I lived in Graz for five years.` (I do not live there any more.) | `I have lived in Graz for five years.` (I still live there.) |
| `Did you finish the report last week?`                   | `Have you finished the report yet?`                     |
| `When did you start?`                                    | ~~When have you started?~~ (a question about a point in time never takes the present perfect) |

:::danger[Typical mistake]
„Ich wohne seit 2015 hier.“ is in the present tense in German. In English it must be `I have lived here since 2015.` (not: ~~I live here since 2015.~~). Likewise „Wie lange lernst du schon Englisch?“ becomes `How long have you been learning English?`
:::

### since and for

-   `since` + **point in time**: `since 2015`, `since Monday`, `since I was ten`
-   `for` + **period of time**: `for five years`, `for two hours`, `for a long time`

Both correspond to German „seit“. `since` can also mean "because" (`Since it was late, we left.`).

## Stative verbs

**Stative verbs** describe states, not actions. They are normally **not** used in the progressive form: `I know the answer.` (not: ~~I am knowing~~).

| Area                  | Verbs                                                            |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| feelings, preferences | `like`, `love`, `hate`, `prefer`, `want`, `need`, `wish`         |
| thinking, opinion     | `know`, `believe`, `understand`, `remember`, `mean`, `doubt`, `agree` |
| possession            | `have` (possess), `own`, `belong`, `possess`                     |
| perception            | `see`, `hear`, `smell`, `taste`, `seem`, `appear`                 |
| others                | `cost`, `contain`, `consist`, `depend`, `weigh`, `fit`           |

Some verbs have both a state meaning and an action meaning:

| State (simple)                                | Action (progressive)                            |
| --------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| `I think it's a good idea.` (opinion)         | `I'm thinking about the problem.` (consider)    |
| `She has a new laptop.` (possess)             | `She is having lunch.` (eat)                    |
| `I see what you mean.` (understand)           | `I'm seeing the doctor at 3.` (meet)            |
| `The soup tastes good.` (has a taste)         | `He is tasting the soup.` (tries it)            |
| `He is rude.` (character)                     | `He is being rude.` (behaving so right now)     |
