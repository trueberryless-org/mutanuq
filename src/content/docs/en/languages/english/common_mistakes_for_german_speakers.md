German and English are closely related. This helps when learning, but it also leads to systematic mistakes: words look the same but mean something different, German word order and tenses are transferred, and German comma and capitalisation rules end up in English texts. These mistakes cost marks in the Matura criterion _Lexical and Structural Accuracy_.

## False friends

**False friends** are pairs of words that look similar in both languages but have different meanings.

| English         | means                          | German           | in English                            |
| --------------- | ------------------------------ | ---------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| `become`        | werden (change into)           | bekommen         | `get`, `receive`                      |
| `actual`        | real, true                     | aktuell          | `current`, `up to date`, `latest`     |
| `actually`      | in fact, really                | aktuell (adv.)   | `currently`, `at the moment`          |
| `eventually`    | in the end, finally            | eventuell        | `possibly`, `perhaps`                 |
| `also`          | as well                        | also             | `so`, `therefore`                     |
| `sensible`      | reasonable                     | sensibel         | `sensitive`                           |
| `sympathetic`   | showing sympathy               | sympathisch      | `nice`, `likeable`                    |
| `consequent`    | following as a result          | konsequent       | `consistent`                          |
| `chef`          | professional cook              | Chef             | `boss`, `manager`, `head`             |
| `gift`          | present                        | Gift             | `poison`                              |
| `handy`         | useful, close at hand          | Handy            | `mobile (phone)` (BrE), `cell phone` (AmE) |
| `map`           | plan of an area                | Mappe            | `folder`, `binder`                    |
| `beamer`        | informal: a BMW car            | Beamer           | `projector`                           |
| `oldtimer`      | someone who has been around a long time | Oldtimer | `vintage car`, `classic car`          |
| `public viewing` | viewing of a dead body before a funeral | Public Viewing | `open-air screening`, `fan zone` |
| `home office`   | study at home; in the UK: interior ministry | Homeoffice | `working from home`, `remote work` |
| `undertaker`    | funeral director               | Unternehmer      | `entrepreneur`                        |
| `bald`          | without hair                   | bald             | `soon`                                |
| `brave`         | courageous                     | brav             | `well-behaved`, `good`                |
| `fabric`        | cloth, material                | Fabrik           | `factory`                             |
| `art`           | painting, music etc.           | Art              | `kind`, `type`, `way`                 |
| `control`       | steer, have power over         | kontrollieren    | `check`, `inspect`                    |
| `engagement`    | agreement to marry; appointment | Engagement      | `commitment`, `involvement`           |
| `provision`     | supply, act of providing       | Provision        | `commission`                          |
| `pension`       | retirement income              | Pension (guesthouse) | `guesthouse`, `B&B`               |
| `note`          | short written message          | Note (school grade) | `mark` (BrE), `grade` (AmE)        |
| `meaning`       | sense of a word                | Meinung          | `opinion`                             |
| `spend`         | use money or time              | spenden          | `donate`                              |
| `will` (auxiliary) | future marker               | ich will         | `I want`                              |
| `who`           | which person                   | wo               | `where`                               |
| `where`         | in which place                 | wer              | `who`                                 |
| `mobbing`       | (rare) crowding around someone | Mobbing          | `bullying`                            |

:::caution[„wenn“: when or if]
German „wenn“ has two English equivalents: `when` for something that will certainly happen (`When I get home, I'll call you.`) and `if` for a condition (`If I get the job, I'll move to Vienna.`).
:::

### Commonly confused pairs

| Word pair                 | Difference                                                                  |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `make` / `do`             | `make` = produce, create (`make a mistake`, `make a decision`, `make a phone call`, `make progress`); `do` = activity, work (`do homework`, `do research`, `do a course`, `do business`) |
| `take` / `make` a photo   | `take a photo` (not ~~make a photo~~)                                       |
| `say` / `tell`            | `tell a lie`, `tell the truth`, `tell a story`, `tell somebody`; `say something` |
| `lend` / `borrow`         | `lend` = give for a time, `borrow` = take for a time: `Can you lend me your pen?` / `Can I borrow your pen?` |
| `learn` / `teach`         | `learn` = gain knowledge, `teach` = give knowledge: ~~He learned me English.~~ → `He taught me English.` |
| `rise` / `raise`          | `rise` = go up (no object), `raise` = lift, increase (with object): `Prices rise.`, `They raised prices.` |
| `lie` / `lay`             | `lie` (lay, lain) = be in a flat position, `lay` (laid, laid) = put down    |
| `possibility` / `opportunity` | `possibility` = something that might be true, `opportunity` = a chance to do something: `a job opportunity` |
| `last` / `latest`         | `last` = final (nothing follows), `latest` = newest: `the latest version`   |
| `most` / `the most`       | „die meisten Leute“ = `most people` (not ~~the most people~~)               |

## Typical grammar mistakes

| Wrong                                         | Correct                                         | Rule                                                       |
| --------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| ~~I live here since three years.~~            | `I have lived here for three years.`            | present perfect for „seit“; `for` + duration               |
| ~~Yesterday I have seen him.~~                | `Yesterday I saw him.`                          | finished time → past simple                                |
| ~~If I will have time, I will come.~~         | `If I have time, I will come.`                  | no `will` in the if-clause                                 |
| ~~If I would have known, ...~~                | `If I had known, ...`                           | third conditional with past perfect                        |
| ~~He go to school.~~ / ~~He don't know.~~     | `He goes to school.` / `He doesn't know.`       | `-s` in the 3rd person singular                            |
| ~~I am agree.~~                               | `I agree.`                                      | `agree` is a verb                                          |
| ~~Can you explain me ...?~~                   | `Can you explain to me ...?` / `Can you explain ...?` | `explain something to somebody`                      |
| ~~We discussed about the problem.~~           | `We discussed the problem.`                     | `discuss` without a preposition                            |
| ~~It gives many problems.~~                   | `There are many problems.`                      | „es gibt“ = `there is/are`                                 |
| ~~The life is beautiful.~~                    | `Life is beautiful.`                            | no article for general meaning                             |
| ~~She is teacher.~~                           | `She is a teacher.`                             | article with jobs                                          |
| ~~informations~~, ~~advices~~                 | `information`, `advice`                         | uncountable                                                |
| ~~The people is nice.~~                       | `The people are nice.`                          | `people` is plural                                         |
| ~~I look forward to hear from you.~~          | `I look forward to hearing from you.`           | `to` is a preposition here                                 |
| ~~He suggested me to go.~~                    | `He suggested that I (should) go.`              | pattern of `suggest`                                       |
| ~~more better~~                               | `better`                                        | compare only once                                          |
| ~~I like very much pizza.~~                   | `I like pizza very much.`                       | no adverb between verb and object                          |
| ~~Do you can help me?~~                       | `Can you help me?`                              | no `do` with modal verbs                                   |
| ~~Since when are you here?~~                  | `How long have you been here?`                  | asking about duration                                      |
| ~~I have a question to you.~~                 | `I have a question for you.`                    | `a question for somebody`                                  |
| ~~until Friday~~ (deadline)                   | `by Friday`                                     | „bis spätestens“ = `by`                                    |

## Punctuation

### Commas

German places commas according to grammatical rules before almost every subordinate clause. English uses commas more sparingly and more according to meaning and readability.

| Situation                                        | German                                           | English                                          |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------ |
| before a `that`-clause                           | Ich denke, dass ...                              | `I think that ...` (no comma)                    |
| before a defining relative clause                | Der Mann, der dort steht, ...                    | `The man who is standing there ...` (no comma)   |
| before an indirect question                      | Ich weiß nicht, ob ...                           | `I don't know if ...` (no comma)                 |
| before an infinitive                             | Ich habe vor, ... zu lernen.                     | `I plan to learn ...` (no comma)                 |
| subordinate clause at the end                    | ..., weil ...                                    | `... because ...` (usually no comma)             |
| subordinate clause at the beginning              | Wenn ..., ...                                    | `If ..., ...` (comma)                            |
| after introductory expressions                   | Leider ist ...                                   | `Unfortunately, ...`, `However, ...`, `In my opinion, ...` (comma) |
| non-defining relative clause, insertion          | Mein Bruder, der in Graz lebt, ...               | `My brother, who lives in Graz, ...` (commas)    |

The **serial comma** (_Oxford comma_) before the last `and` in a list (`HTML, CSS, and JavaScript`) is usual in American English and optional in British English. Use it consistently.

Two complete main clauses must not be joined with just a comma (_comma splice_): ~~The server crashed, we lost data.~~ Correct: `The server crashed, and we lost data.`, `The server crashed; we lost data.` or two sentences.

### Other differences

| Area                      | German                        | English                                                     |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| quotation marks           | „...“                         | “...” or, often in British English, ‘...’                   |
| decimal separator         | 3,5                           | `3.5`                                                       |
| thousands separator       | 3.000 or 3 000                | `3,000`                                                     |
| salutation in letters     | Sehr geehrte Frau Huber!      | `Dear Ms Huber,` (comma, then a capital letter)             |
| date                      | 20.01.2024                    | `20 January 2024` (BrE), `January 20, 2024` (AmE)           |
| apostrophe                | rare                          | possession and short forms, **not** for plurals: `CDs`, `PCs` (not ~~PC's~~) |

## Capitalisation

In English, **nouns are written in lower case** unless they are proper nouns. Capital letters are used for:

-   the pronoun `I`, always: `Yesterday I ...`
-   the beginning of a sentence
-   names of people, places, companies, products: `Anna`, `Vienna`, `Microsoft`, `Linux`
-   **days and months**: `Monday`, `January` (but not seasons: `in summer`)
-   **nationalities, languages and their adjectives**: `Austrian`, `English`, `a German car`
-   holidays, events: `Christmas`, `World War II`
-   titles before names: `Professor Smith`, `Dr Huber`
-   headings and titles of works in _title case_ (all important words capitalised): `The Lord of the Rings`; many websites and companies also use _sentence case_ for headings.

School subjects are written in lower case (`maths`, `physics`) unless they are languages (`English`, `German`). `internet` and `email` are mostly written in lower case today.
