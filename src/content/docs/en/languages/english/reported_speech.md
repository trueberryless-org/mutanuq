With reported speech (_indirect speech_) you pass on what someone said without quoting them word for word. German uses the Konjunktiv I for this („Er sagte, er sei krank.“), while English moves the tense one step back into the past (_backshift_). You need reported speech for reports, summaries of interviews and minutes of meetings.

## say and tell

| Verb    | Pattern                                       | Example                                       |
| ------- | --------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| `say`   | `say (that)` ..., `say something to somebody` | `She said (that) she was tired.`              |
| `tell`  | `tell somebody (that)` ...                    | `She told me (that) she was tired.`           |

`tell` always needs a person as its object: ~~She told that ...~~, ~~She said me ...~~. The `that` can be left out.

## Backshift

If the reporting verb is in the past (`said`, `told`, `asked`), the tense of the direct speech moves one step back.

| Direct speech                  | Reported speech                 | Example                                                                |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| present simple                 | past simple                     | "I **work** in Vienna." → He said he **worked** in Vienna.             |
| present progressive            | past progressive                | "I **am coding**." → She said she **was coding**.                      |
| present perfect                | past perfect                    | "I **have finished**." → He said he **had finished**.                  |
| present perfect progressive    | past perfect progressive        | "I **have been waiting**." → She said she **had been waiting**.        |
| past simple                    | past perfect (or unchanged)     | "I **saw** it." → He said he **had seen** it.                          |
| past progressive               | past perfect progressive        | "I **was sleeping**." → She said she **had been sleeping**.            |
| past perfect                   | past perfect (unchanged)        | "I **had left**." → He said he **had left**.                           |
| `will`                         | `would`                         | "I **will call** you." → She said she **would call** me.               |
| `can`                          | `could`                         | "I **can** help." → He said he **could** help.                         |
| `may`                          | `might`                         | "It **may** rain." → She said it **might** rain.                       |
| `must`                         | `had to` (or `must`)            | "You **must** register." → He said I **had to** register.              |
| `shall` (offer, question)      | `should`                        | "**Shall** I open the window?" → She asked if she **should** open the window. |

`would`, `could`, `should`, `might`, `ought to` and `used to` stay unchanged.

### When there is no backshift

-   The reporting verb is in the present or present perfect: `"I'm hungry." → He says he is hungry.`
-   The statement is still true or a general truth; then the backshift is optional: `"The earth is round." → The teacher said the earth is round.`
-   The past simple often stays unchanged when the order of events is clear: `"I was born in 2008." → She said she was born in 2008.`

## Time and place expressions

Since the statement is reported from a different situation, expressions that refer to the moment of speaking change.

| Direct speech               | Reported speech                                 |
| --------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| `now`                       | `then`, `at that time`                          |
| `today`                     | `that day`                                      |
| `tonight`                   | `that night`                                    |
| `yesterday`                 | `the day before`, `the previous day`            |
| `tomorrow`                  | `the next day`, `the following day`             |
| `last week`                 | `the week before`, `the previous week`          |
| `next week`                 | `the following week`, `the week after`          |
| `two days ago`              | `two days before`, `two days earlier`           |
| `here`                      | `there`                                         |
| `this`, `these`             | `that`, `those`                                 |

The **pronouns** change as well: `"I will send you my notes," Tom told Lisa.` becomes `Tom told Lisa that he would send her his notes.`

:::note
These changes are not mechanical. If you report on the same day in the same place, `today` of course stays `today`. What matters is what is logical from the reporter's point of view.
:::

## Reported questions

Reported questions have the word order of a statement (subject before verb), no `do/does/did` and no question mark.

| Question type           | Direct question                         | Reported question                                       |
| ----------------------- | --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| yes/no question         | "**Do you** like Java?"                 | She asked me **if/whether I liked** Java.               |
| yes/no question         | "**Is** the server running?"            | He asked **if the server was** running.                 |
| wh-question             | "Where **do you live**?"                | She asked me where **I lived**.                         |
| wh-question             | "What **has happened**?"                | He wanted to know what **had happened**.                |
| subject question        | "Who **broke** the build?"              | She asked who **had broken** the build.                 |

Yes/no questions are introduced with `if` or `whether` (`whether` is more formal and usual with `or not`). In wh-questions the question word stays.

## Reported commands and requests

Commands, requests and advice are reported with a reporting verb + object + `to`-infinitive. The negative is `not to`.

| Direct speech                               | Reported speech                                      |
| ------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| "Close the door."                           | He **told** me **to close** the door.                |
| "Please send me the file."                  | She **asked** me **to send** her the file.           |
| "Don't touch the cables!"                   | The teacher **told** us **not to touch** the cables. |
| "You should update your system."            | He **advised** me **to update** my system.           |

## Reporting verbs

Instead of always using `say` and `tell`, suitable reporting verbs make your text more precise and varied. Pay attention to the pattern that follows the verb.

| Pattern                                   | Verbs                                                                   | Example                                                     |
| ----------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| verb + `that`-clause                      | `explain`, `admit`, `claim`, `point out`, `mention`, `state`, `reply`, `complain`, `announce` | `She explained that the server was down.` |
| verb + person + `that`-clause             | `tell`, `inform`, `remind`, `assure`, `warn`                            | `He assured us that the data was safe.`                     |
| verb + `to`-infinitive                    | `agree`, `offer`, `promise`, `refuse`, `threaten`, `decide`             | `He offered to help me.`                                    |
| verb + person + `to`-infinitive           | `ask`, `tell`, `advise`, `encourage`, `invite`, `order`, `remind`, `warn`, `persuade` | `She reminded me to save the file.`          |
| verb + `-ing`                             | `admit`, `deny`, `suggest`, `recommend`, `mention`                      | `He denied deleting the database.`                          |
| verb + preposition + `-ing`               | `apologise for`, `insist on`, `complain about`, `object to`             | `She apologised for being late.`                            |
| verb + person + preposition + `-ing`      | `accuse sb of`, `blame sb for`, `congratulate sb on`, `thank sb for`, `warn sb against` | `They accused him of stealing the code.`  |

:::caution[suggest]
`suggest` has no pattern with person + `to`-infinitive: ~~He suggested me to apply.~~ Correct: `He suggested applying.` or `He suggested that I (should) apply.`
:::
