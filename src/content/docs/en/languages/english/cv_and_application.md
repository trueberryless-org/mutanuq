For a compulsory internship abroad, a degree programme or a job at an international company, you need application documents in English: a **CV** (or _résumé_) and a **cover letter**. English-language documents follow different conventions from Austrian ones. A translated Austrian CV with a photo, date of birth and signature looks unusual in the UK or the USA.

## CV

### Terms

| Term            | Meaning                                                                           |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **CV** (_curriculum vitae_) | in the UK, Ireland and Europe: the usual document listing your education and experience |
| **résumé** (also _resume_) | in the USA and Canada: a short document for job applications; a _CV_ there is a detailed academic record |
| **Europass CV** | standardised European format of the European Union                                |

### British and American conventions

| Feature                         | Austria (common)       | UK (CV)                        | USA (résumé)                  |
| ------------------------------- | ---------------------- | ------------------------------ | ----------------------------- |
| length                          | 1 to 2 pages           | usually 2 pages                | usually 1 page                |
| photo                           | often                  | no                             | no                            |
| date of birth, marital status   | often                  | no                             | no                            |
| order                           | usually reverse chronological | reverse chronological   | reverse chronological         |
| profile at the top              | rare                   | _personal statement_ usual     | _summary_ usual               |
| signature                       | often                  | no                             | no                            |

The absence of a photo, age and marital status is about protection against discrimination: employers are meant to decide on qualifications alone, and many even reject documents containing such details to avoid any risk.

### Structure

**Reverse chronological order** means that the most recent item comes first.

1.  **Personal details:** name (as the heading, not "CV"), phone number with country code (`+43 ...`), email address, town, links to LinkedIn or GitHub
2.  **Personal statement / profile:** three to four sentences: who you are, what you can do, what you are looking for
3.  **Education:** school, specialisation, dates, main subjects, diploma thesis
4.  **Work experience:** internships, holiday jobs, projects; for each: company, place, dates, position and tasks as bullet points with action verbs
5.  **Skills:** technical skills (programming languages, tools, operating systems), languages with CEFR level (`German: native`, `English: C1`), soft skills with evidence
6.  **Certificates / awards:** for example Cisco CCNA, ECDL/ICDL, competitions
7.  **Interests** (optional): only if they say something about you
8.  **References:** usually left out today, or `References available on request`

### The HTL qualification in English

| German                                          | English                                                                   |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Höhere Technische Lehranstalt (HTL)             | Higher Technical College (HTL)                                            |
| Abteilung Informationstechnologie               | Department of Information Technology                                      |
| Reife- und Diplomprüfung                        | Matura and Diploma Examination (Austrian higher education entrance qualification) |
| Diplomarbeit                                    | diploma thesis                                                            |
| Pflichtpraktikum                                | compulsory work placement, internship                                     |
| Ausbildungsschwerpunkt                          | specialisation                                                            |

Do not translate school grades literally. If you include them, explain the Austrian grading scale (1 = best, 5 = fail) or write `passed with distinction` (mit ausgezeichnetem Erfolg) or `passed with merit` (mit gutem Erfolg). For HTL Matura certificates there is an official English explanation (_Europass Certificate Supplement_).

### Action verbs

Describe tasks with **action verbs** in the past simple (for past activities) and, where possible, with a measurable result:

| Verbs                                           | Example                                                                |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `developed`, `designed`, `implemented`, `built` | `Developed a web app for booking school rooms (React, ASP.NET).`       |
| `configured`, `installed`, `maintained`, `set up` | `Configured VLANs and firewall rules for a network with 120 clients.` |
| `automated`, `improved`, `reduced`, `optimised` | `Automated backups with PowerShell, saving 2 hours per week.`          |
| `led`, `organised`, `coordinated`, `presented`  | `Led a team of four in our diploma project.`                           |
| `tested`, `analysed`, `documented`              | `Wrote unit tests and documentation for the API.`                      |

### Europass

**Europass** is a European Union initiative to make qualifications comparable across Europe. On the Europass portal you can create a CV online free of charge and download it as a PDF, in all EU languages. Language skills are self-assessed according to the **Common European Framework of Reference for Languages** (CEFR) from A1 to C2, separately for understanding, speaking and writing. A photo is optional in Europass. Many employers in the UK and the USA, however, prefer an individually designed CV or résumé.

## Cover letter

The cover letter (in an email application often the email text itself) accompanies the CV. It does not repeat the CV but shows **why you are right for this particular position**. It is one page at most. You will also find rules and phrases under [Email of application](/en/languages/text_types/email/#email-of-application) and the layout under [Letter](/en/languages/text_types/letter/#layout).

### Structure

| Paragraph                  | Content                                                                         |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| salutation                 | `Dear Ms Smith,` (find out the name), otherwise `Dear Hiring Manager,`          |
| introduction               | the position, where you heard about it, one sentence on why you are applying   |
| main part 1: what you can do | two to three qualifications from the job advertisement with concrete evidence |
| main part 2: why this company | what interests you about the company, what you can contribute               |
| conclusion                 | availability, request for an interview, thanks                                  |
| closing                    | `Yours sincerely,` (name known) or `Yours faithfully,` (name unknown), name     |

### Phrases

| Introduction                                                                                |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| I am writing to apply for the position of junior developer advertised on your website.      |
| I would like to apply for a four-week summer internship in your IT department.              |
| Ms Huber, your Head of IT, suggested that I contact you about ...                           |

| Qualifications                                                                              |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| I am currently in my final year at a Higher Technical College, specialising in ...          |
| During my internship at ..., I gained hands-on experience in ...                            |
| In our diploma project, I was responsible for ...                                           |
| I have a solid knowledge of ... and am familiar with ...                                    |
| I am particularly interested in this position because ...                                   |

| Conclusion                                                                                  |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| I am available from July to September.                                                      |
| I would welcome the opportunity to discuss my application in an interview.                  |
| Thank you for considering my application. I look forward to hearing from you.               |

:::caution
Write each cover letter anew for the specific position. A general text that would fit any company is noticed immediately. Have it proofread: spelling mistakes are a common reason for rejecting applications.
:::

## Job interview

### Preparation

-   Find out about the company (products, customers, values).
-   Read the job advertisement carefully and think of an example for each requirement.
-   Practise answering typical questions aloud in English.
-   Prepare two or three questions of your own.

### The STAR method

For behavioural questions (_Tell me about a time when ..._), the **STAR method** helps you give a clear, concrete answer:

| Step             | Content                                  | Example                                                                  |
| ---------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **S**ituation    | the starting point                       | `During our diploma project, our database crashed two weeks before the deadline.` |
| **T**ask         | your task                                | `I was responsible for restoring the data.`                              |
| **A**ction       | what exactly you did                     | `I restored the last backup, wrote a script to recover the missing entries and set up daily automated backups.` |
| **R**esult       | the outcome, ideally measurable          | `We lost no data and finished on time.`                                  |

### Typical questions

| Question                                                | Tip                                                                      |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `Tell me about yourself.`                               | one to two minutes: education, experience, why this position; not your whole CV |
| `Why do you want to work for us?`                       | refer specifically to the company                                        |
| `What are your strengths?`                              | two strengths with examples that fit the position                        |
| `What is your greatest weakness?`                       | a real but not central weakness and how you are working on it            |
| `Tell me about a time when you worked in a team.`       | STAR method                                                              |
| `Describe a problem you solved.`                        | STAR method, explain technical details clearly                           |
| `Where do you see yourself in five years?`              | development in your field, realistic                                     |
| `Why should we hire you?`                               | summary: the requirements and your matching qualifications               |
| `Do you have any questions for us?`                     | always say yes and ask a question                                        |

| Your own questions                                                    |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| What would a typical day in this position look like?                  |
| Which technologies does the team work with?                           |
| How will my work be evaluated during the internship?                  |
| What are the next steps in the application process?                   |

| Useful phrases                                                        |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| That's a good question. Let me think for a moment.                    |
| Could you rephrase the question, please?                              |
| For example, in my last internship I ...                              |
| Thank you for taking the time to meet me.                             |
