Nouns and their determiners (articles, quantifiers, possessive determiners) look simple but cause many mistakes: English uses articles according to different rules from German, and some nouns that are countable in German are not countable in English (`information`, `advice`, `software`).

## Countable and uncountable nouns

**Countable nouns** have a singular and a plural form and are never used in the singular without a determiner: `a computer`, `two computers`. **Uncountable nouns** have no plural, are not used with `a/an` and take a singular verb: `The information is correct.`

| Area                     | Uncountable nouns                                                         |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| knowledge and communication | `information`, `advice`, `news`, `knowledge`, `research`, `evidence`, `feedback`, `homework`, `progress` |
| IT                       | `software`, `hardware`, `equipment`, `data` (usually), `malware`, `spam`, `storage`, `bandwidth` |
| everyday life            | `furniture`, `luggage`, `baggage`, `money`, `traffic`, `accommodation` (BrE), `work`, `weather` |
| substances and abstracts | `water`, `rice`, `air`, `music`, `fun`, `health`, `safety`                |

To count uncountable nouns, use a unit expression: `a piece of advice`, `a piece of information`, `an item of luggage`, `a bit of news`, `a slice of bread`.

:::caution[Typical mistakes]
~~informations~~, ~~advices~~, ~~softwares~~, ~~equipments~~, ~~an advice~~, ~~a homework~~. Correct: `some information`, `a piece of advice`, `software products`, `two pieces of equipment`.
:::

Some nouns are countable or uncountable depending on their meaning:

| Uncountable                           | Countable                                     |
| ------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| `Paper is made from wood.` (material) | `I bought a paper.` (newspaper)               |
| `I don't have time.` (time)           | `I've been there three times.` (occasions)    |
| `She has a lot of experience.` (knowledge and skill) | `It was a great experience.` (event) |
| `I'm looking for work.` (a job)       | `the works of Shakespeare` (pieces of art)    |
| `Coffee is expensive.` (in general)   | `Two coffees, please.` (two cups)             |

`data` was originally the Latin plural of `datum`. In general and IT English it is mostly used as an uncountable noun with a singular verb (`The data is stored in the cloud.`); in scientific texts the plural also occurs (`The data show ...`).

## Plural forms

| Rule                                           | Examples                                               |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| standard: `-s`                                 | `computer` → `computers`, `key` → `keys`               |
| after `s`, `ss`, `sh`, `ch`, `x`, `z`: `-es`   | `bus` → `buses`, `class` → `classes`, `box` → `boxes`, `switch` → `switches` |
| consonant + `y`: `-ies`                        | `city` → `cities`, `library` → `libraries` (but `day` → `days`) |
| `-f`/`-fe` often `-ves`                        | `knife` → `knives`, `life` → `lives`, `half` → `halves`, `shelf` → `shelves` (but `roof` → `roofs`, `chief` → `chiefs`) |
| `-o`: `-oes` or `-os`                          | `tomato` → `tomatoes`, `hero` → `heroes`; `photo` → `photos`, `video` → `videos`, `radio` → `radios` |
| irregular                                      | `man` → `men`, `woman` → `women`, `child` → `children`, `person` → `people`, `foot` → `feet`, `tooth` → `teeth`, `mouse` → `mice` |
| singular = plural                              | `sheep`, `fish`, `deer`, `series`, `species`, `aircraft` |
| Latin/Greek                                    | `analysis` → `analyses`, `criterion` → `criteria`, `phenomenon` → `phenomena`, `thesis` → `theses`, `matrix` → `matrices`, `index` → `indices` (also `indexes`), `vertex` → `vertices` |

For computer mice, both `mice` and `mouses` are in use.

### Plural-only nouns, collective nouns

-   **plural only:** `trousers`, `jeans`, `glasses`, `scissors`, `clothes`, `goods`, `headphones`; counted with `a pair of`: `a pair of glasses`
-   **plural without `-s`:** `people`, `police`, `staff` (usually with a plural verb): `The police are investigating.`
-   **collective nouns** such as `team`, `government`, `company`, `family` take a singular or plural verb in British English (`The team is/are playing well.`) and usually a singular verb in American English.
-   `news` is singular despite the `-s`: `The news is good.`

## Articles

### a and an

The choice depends on the **sound**, not on the letter:

-   `a` before a consonant sound: `a computer`, `a university` (/juː/), `a European`, `a one-way street`, `a USB stick` (/juː/)
-   `an` before a vowel sound: `an app`, `an hour` (silent h), `an honest answer`, `an MP3 file` (/em/), `an SQL query` (when spoken as letters)

### Indefinite article a/an

-   first mention, one of many: `I've bought a new laptop.`
-   **jobs** and membership (unlike German): `She is an engineer.`
-   `What a ...!`, `such a ...`, `quite a ...`: `What a great idea!`
-   frequency, prices, speed: `twice a week`, `€5 a kilo`, `100 km an hour`

### Definite article the

-   something already mentioned or known from the context: `I bought a laptop. The laptop is fast.`, `Close the door, please.`
-   unique things: `the sun`, `the internet`, `the government`, `the environment`
-   superlatives and ordinal numbers: `the best solution`, `the first version`
-   `the same`, `the only`, `the last`, `the next` (in a sequence)
-   `the` + adjective for groups: `the rich`, `the elderly`, `the unemployed`
-   musical instruments: `She plays the piano.`
-   rivers, seas, mountain ranges, groups of islands: `the Danube`, `the Atlantic`, `the Alps`
-   countries with a plural or a common noun: `the USA`, `the UK`, `the Netherlands`, `the Czech Republic`
-   newspapers, hotels, museums, cinemas: `the Guardian`, `the Hilton`, `the Louvre`

### Zero article

No article is used with:

-   plural and uncountable nouns in a **general sense**: `Computers have changed our lives.`, `Life is short.`, `Software is expensive.`
-   most countries, cities, streets, lakes, single mountains: `Austria`, `Vienna`, `Oxford Street`, `Lake Constance`, `Mount Everest`
-   languages, school subjects, sports, meals: `I speak English.`, `We have maths.`, `I play football.`, `Breakfast is ready.`
-   days, months, holidays: `on Monday`, `in May`, `at Christmas`
-   `next`/`last` + time seen from now: `next week`, `last year`
-   means of transport with `by`: `by car`, `by train`, `by bus` (but `on foot`)
-   institutions used for their typical purpose: `go to school`, `be at university`, `go to bed`, `be at work`, `be at home`, `be in hospital` (BrE), `go to church`, `be in prison`

`I went to school.` (as a pupil) versus `I went to the school to talk to the head.` (as a visitor to the building).

## Quantifiers

| Quantifier                    | with countable nouns (plural) | with uncountable nouns | Example                                          |
| ----------------------------- | ----------------------------- | ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| `many`                        | yes                           | no                     | `How many emails did you get?`                   |
| `much`                        | no                            | yes                    | `How much RAM does it have?`                     |
| `a lot of`, `lots of`         | yes                           | yes                    | `a lot of users`, `a lot of time`                |
| `few`, `a few`                | yes                           | no                     | `a few bugs`                                     |
| `little`, `a little`          | no                            | yes                    | `a little help`                                  |
| `fewer`, `less`               | `fewer`                       | `less`                 | `fewer errors`, `less memory`                    |
| `some`, `any`, `no`           | yes                           | yes                    | `some ideas`, `any information`                  |
| `a large number of`           | yes                           | no                     | `a large number of devices`                      |
| `a great deal of`, `a large amount of` | no                   | yes                    | `a great deal of effort`                         |
| `plenty of`                   | yes                           | yes                    | `plenty of space`                                |

`much` and `many` are mainly used in questions and negative sentences. In positive sentences they sound formal; there `a lot of` is usually preferred: `We have a lot of time.` instead of ~~We have much time.~~

### few and a few, little and a little

-   `a few`, `a little`: **some** (positive, enough): `I have a few friends here, so I'm not lonely.`
-   `few`, `little`: **not many, not much** (negative, too few): `Few people understood the talk.`, `There is little hope.`

### some and any

-   `some` in positive sentences and in offers and requests where you expect a yes: `Would you like some coffee?`, `Could I have some water?`
-   `any` in questions and negative sentences: `Do you have any questions?`, `I don't have any time.`
-   `any` in positive sentences means "it does not matter which": `You can choose any laptop.`

### each, every, all, both, either, neither

-   `every` + singular (all as a group): `Every student has an account.`
-   `each` + singular (each one individually, also with two): `Each team member gets a task.`
-   `both` (the two), `either` (one of the two), `neither` (not one of the two): `Neither solution works.`

## Possessives

| Form                               | Use                                         | Example                                        |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| `'s`                               | people, animals, organisations (singular)   | `the teacher's laptop`, `Austria's capital`    |
| `s'`                               | regular plural ending in `-s`               | `the students' results`                        |
| `'s` after irregular plurals       | `children`, `people`, `women`               | `the children's toys`                          |
| `of`                               | things, long phrases                        | `the end of the film`, `the name of the file`  |
| `'s` with time expressions         | time reference, duration                    | `today's news`, `a week's holiday`, `two hours' delay` |
| double possessive                  | one of several                              | `a friend of mine`, `a colleague of Anna's`    |

With names ending in `-s`, both `James's` and `James'` are possible.

### Possessive determiners and possessive pronouns

| Personal pronoun | Possessive determiner (+ noun) | Possessive pronoun (alone) |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------ | -------------------------- |
| I                | my                             | mine                       |
| you              | your                           | yours                      |
| he / she / it    | his / her / its                | his / hers / (its)         |
| we               | our                            | ours                       |
| they             | their                          | theirs                     |

-   `its` (possessive) has no apostrophe; `it's` = `it is` or `it has`. Likewise `whose` (possessive) and `who's` (`who is`).
-   With parts of the body and clothes, English uses a possessive determiner where German uses the article: `I broke my arm.`, `He put his hands in his pockets.`
