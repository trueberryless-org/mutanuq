At the HTL you need spoken English for presentations in class, for presenting your diploma project, during internships and in the oral Matura and diploma examination. What counts is not error-free English but that you speak coherently, clearly and appropriately for the situation, and that you respond to the other person. The target level at the end of the HTL is **B2** of the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages.

## The oral exam

### Procedure

The oral exam in a modern foreign language is **competence-based** and consists of two parts:

1.  **Topic areas:** The teachers define a pool of topic areas for the final year, taken from the curriculum (for example _Technology and Society_, _Working Life_, _Environment_, _Media_). The list is published in good time before the exam.
2.  **Draw:** In the exam you draw two topic areas and choose one of them. For this topic area you receive a task you have not seen before.
3.  **Preparation:** You get preparation time in which you may take notes. The length of the preparation time and of the exam is set by the examination regulations.
4.  **Exam:** It consists of a **monologue** part and a **dialogue** part.

| Part                    | What you do                                                                                     |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Monologue** (_long turn_) | a coherent contribution based on a prompt: a situation, guiding questions, often pictures, graphs or a short text. You describe, compare, explain and justify your opinion. |
| **Dialogue** (_interaction_) | a conversation with the examiner or, in the paired exam, with a classmate: role play, discussion, reaching a decision together, solving a problem. |

You are assessed on whether you complete the task, how coherently and fluently you speak, how varied and accurate your vocabulary and grammar are, and how well you respond to your partner in the dialogue.

:::note
Details such as the preparation time, the length of the exam and the form of the dialogue part are set out in the examination regulations for vocational schools and may change. Your school and [matura.gv.at](https://www.matura.gv.at/) provide the binding information.
:::

### Tips for the exam

-   **Read the task carefully** and cover every point. If you leave out a point, you lose marks for task achievement.
-   **Notes, not full sentences:** write down key words and linking words, not complete texts to read out.
-   **Show your structure:** announce what you are going to say and connect your points (`First, ... Then I'd like to ... Finally ...`).
-   **Give reasons and examples:** an opinion without a reason is not enough for B2.
-   **Respond in the dialogue:** react to what has been said, ask questions, agree or disagree with reasons. A dialogue is not a second monologue.
-   **Bridge gaps:** if you are missing a word, paraphrase it (`It's a kind of ...`, `the thing you use to ...`) rather than falling silent.

## Presentations

### Structure

| Part                    | Content                                                                        |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| greeting and opening    | greeting, introducing yourself, an opening that creates interest (question, figure, picture) |
| overview                | topic and outline (_signposting_)                                              |
| main part               | two to four points, each with a transition                                     |
| conclusion              | summary, key message, thanks                                                   |
| questions               | answering questions                                                            |

### Phrases

| Opening                                                                   |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Good morning, everyone. Thank you for coming.                             |
| My name is ... and today I'm going to talk about ...                      |
| Have you ever wondered why ...?                                           |
| Did you know that ...?                                                    |

| Overview and transitions                                                  |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| My presentation is divided into three parts.                              |
| First, I'll ... Then I'll ... Finally, I'll ...                           |
| Let's start with ...                                                      |
| That brings me to my next point.                                          |
| Moving on to ...                                                          |
| As I mentioned earlier, ...                                               |

| Visuals                                                                   |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Let's have a look at this slide.                                          |
| As you can see on this chart, ...                                         |
| This diagram shows ...                                                    |
| I'd like to draw your attention to ...                                    |

| Conclusion and questions                                                  |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| To sum up, ... / In conclusion, ...                                       |
| The key point to remember is ...                                          |
| Thank you for your attention.                                             |
| Are there any questions?                                                  |
| That's a good question. / I'm afraid I don't know, but I'll find out.     |

:::tip[Slides]
Few words per slide, large font, one idea per slide. Do not read from your slides; they should support you, not replace you.
:::

## Describing pictures

| Area                | Phrases                                                                         |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| overview            | `The picture shows ...` / `In this photo we can see ...`                        |
| position            | `in the foreground / background`, `in the middle`, `on the left / right`, `in the top left-hand corner`, `next to`, `behind` |
| people, action      | `A young woman is sitting at a desk.` (present progressive for actions in the picture) |
| speculating         | `She seems to be ...` / `He might be ...` / `It looks as if ...` / `It could be ...` / `They must be ...` |
| comparing           | `Both pictures show ...` / `While the first picture ..., the second ...` / `In contrast to ...` |
| link to the topic   | `This reminds me of ...` / `The picture illustrates the problem of ...`         |

Do not only describe what you see; interpret it and link it to the task. For speculating, [modal verbs of deduction](/en/languages/english/modal_verbs/#deduction) are particularly useful.

You describe graphs and charts with the vocabulary under [Technical English](/en/languages/english/technical_english/#describing-graphs).

## Discussions

| Function                       | Phrases                                                                     |
| ------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| giving your opinion            | `In my opinion, ...` / `I strongly believe that ...` / `As far as I'm concerned, ...` |
| asking for opinions            | `What do you think?` / `How do you feel about ...?` / `Would you agree?`     |
| agreeing                       | `I agree with you.` / `That's exactly what I think.` / `You have a point there.` |
| partly agreeing                | `I agree up to a point, but ...` / `That may be true, but ...`               |
| disagreeing                    | `I'm afraid I disagree.` / `I see what you mean, but ...` / `I'm not so sure about that.` |
| making a suggestion            | `What about ...?` / `Why don't we ...?` / `How about ...?` / `Let's ...`     |
| responding to a suggestion     | `That's a good idea.` / `I'm not sure that would work because ...`           |
| finding a compromise           | `Maybe we could combine both ideas.` / `Shall we agree on ...?`              |
| asking for clarification       | `What do you mean by ...?` / `Could you give an example?`                   |
| gaining time                   | `Well, let me think.` / `That's an interesting question.` / `How can I put it?` |
| reaching a result              | `So, we've decided to ...` / `So, we agree that ...`                         |
