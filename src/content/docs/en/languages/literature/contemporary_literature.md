**Contemporary literature** covers literature since around 1990. Unlike earlier epochs, it cannot be pinned down to a common programme: there is a great diversity of themes, styles and voices. It is therefore often described as postmodern, as the boundaries between "high" and popular literature blur and old forms are combined in new ways.

## Key facts

- from c. 1990
- fall of the Berlin Wall (1989) and German reunification (1990)
- globalisation, digitalisation and the internet
- diversity instead of a uniform programme
- themes: identity, memory, migration, family, the climate crisis, media
- new forms: poetry slam, blogs, audiobooks, Instapoetry

## Historical background

With the **fall of the Berlin Wall** on 9 November 1989, the Cold War ended, and in 1990 the two German states were reunited. Austria joined the European Union in 1995. Globalisation and the internet changed work, communication and reading. Migration increasingly shapes society, as do new challenges such as climate change, terrorist attacks, economic crises and the coronavirus pandemic.

## Movements and themes

### Literature of reunification

Many authors dealt with the end of the GDR and reunification (Wendeliteratur). Well-known works are Thomas Brussig's "Am kürzeren Ende der Sonnenallee" (1999), Ingo Schulze's "Simple Storys" (Simple Stories, 1998) and Uwe Tellkamp's "Der Turm" (The Tower, 2008), which describes the life of a Dresden family in the last years of the GDR.

### Pop literature

In the 1990s, young authors such as **Christian Kracht** ("Faserland", 1995) and Benjamin von Stuckrad-Barre ("Soloalbum", 1998) described the consumer world, brand names, music and the attitude to life of their generation.

### Memory literature

The Nazi era and the Holocaust remain important themes, now often from the perspective of the grandchildren's generation exploring their family history. In "**Der Vorleser**" (The Reader, 1995), Bernhard Schlink tells of a teenager's love for an older woman who later turns out to have been a concentration camp guard. Other examples are W. G. Sebald's "Austerlitz" (2001) and Monika Helfer's family history "Die Bagage" (The Bagage, 2020).

### Literature of migration and multilingualism

More and more authors write in German although it is not their first language, or tell of flight, arrival and life between cultures. Examples are **Saša Stanišić** ("Wie der Soldat das Grammofon repariert", How the Soldier Repairs the Gramophone, 2006; "Herkunft", Where You Come From, 2019), Emine Sevgi Özdamar, Feridun Zaimoglu and the Viennese author Julya Rabinowich ("Spaltkopf", 2008; "Dazwischen: Ich", 2016).

### New media and forms

The internet gave rise to new forms such as blogs, social media literature and **poetry slams**, in which authors perform their texts in front of an audience that votes for the winner. Audiobooks and podcasts also reach a large audience.

## Contemporary Austrian literature

- **Christoph Ransmayr** (born 1954): "Die letzte Welt" (The Last World, 1988) transfers Ovid's "Metamorphoses" into a mysterious present.
- **Robert Menasse** (born 1954): "Die Hauptstadt" (The Capital, 2017) is a satirical novel about the European Commission in Brussels.
- **Daniel Kehlmann** (born 1975): "Die Vermessung der Welt" (Measuring the World, 2005) tells ironically of the lives of the scientists Carl Friedrich Gauss and Alexander von Humboldt and became one of the most successful German-language novels of recent decades. "Tyll" (2017) is set in the Thirty Years' War.
- **Wolf Haas** (born 1960): crime novels about the detective Simon Brenner, such as "Komm, süßer Tod" (Come, Sweet Death), written in an unmistakable, colloquial narrative voice.
- **Thomas Glavinic** (born 1972): "Die Arbeit der Nacht" (Night Work, 2006) tells of a man who wakes up one morning to find he is the only person in the world.
- **Arno Geiger** (born 1968): "Der alte König in seinem Exil" (The Old King in His Exile, 2011) about his father's dementia.
- **Robert Seethaler** (born 1966): "Der Trafikant" (The Tobacconist, 2012) about a young man in Vienna in 1937 who gets to know Sigmund Freud; "Ein ganzes Leben" (A Whole Life, 2014).
- **Clemens J. Setz** (born 1982): experimental novels and stories, awarded the Georg Büchner Prize in 2021.
- Teresa Präauer, Raphaela Edelbauer and Tonio Schachinger ("Echtzeitalter", 2023, German Book Prize) are among the important younger voices.

## Genres

- **Epic:** the novel in all its varieties (family, social, historical and crime novel), autofiction
- **Poetry:** diverse forms, poetry slam, Instapoetry
- **Drama:** postdramatic theatre, text surfaces (e.g. Elfriede Jelinek), documentary theatre
