The history of German-language literature is divided into **epochs**. An epoch covers a period in which many works share similar themes, ideas and forms. However, the boundaries are fluid: epochs overlap, and many authors cannot be assigned to just one. Knowing the epochs helps you place a text in its historical context, an important basis for [text interpretation](/en/languages/german_text_types/text_interpretation/).

## Overview

| Epoch                                   | Period           | Keywords                                          | Important authors                      |
| --------------------------------------- | ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Middle Ages                             | c. 750 – 1500    | courtly love poetry, heroic epic, courtly romance | Walther von der Vogelweide             |
| Humanism and Reformation                | c. 1450 – 1600   | printing press, "ad fontes", Bible translation    | Luther, Erasmus, Sebastian Brant       |
| Baroque                                 | c. 1600 – 1720   | vanitas, memento mori, carpe diem, sonnet         | Gryphius, Grimmelshausen               |
| Enlightenment                           | c. 1720 – 1800   | reason, tolerance, education                      | Lessing, Kant                          |
| Sturm und Drang                         | c. 1765 – 1785   | feeling, genius, freedom                          | young Goethe, young Schiller           |
| Weimar Classicism                       | c. 1786 – 1805   | humanity, harmony, antiquity                      | Goethe, Schiller                       |
| Romanticism                             | c. 1795 – 1835   | longing, blue flower, night, fairy tales          | Novalis, Eichendorff, E. T. A. Hoffmann |
| Biedermeier and Vormärz                 | c. 1815 – 1848   | retreat into private life or political literature | Grillparzer, Stifter, Nestroy, Heine, Büchner |
| Realism                                 | c. 1848 – 1890   | reality with transfiguration, novella             | Fontane, Storm, Ebner-Eschenbach       |
| Naturalism                              | c. 1880 – 1900   | milieu, heredity, second-by-second style          | Gerhart Hauptmann, Arno Holz           |
| Impressionism / turn of the century     | c. 1890 – 1920   | moods, Viennese Modernism, interior monologue     | Schnitzler, Hofmannsthal, Rilke        |
| Expressionism                           | c. 1910 – 1925   | big city, war, shattering of language             | Heym, Trakl, Benn, Kafka               |
| New Objectivity                         | c. 1920 – 1933   | sobriety, big city, epic theatre                  | Kästner, Brecht, Döblin, Roth, Horváth |
| Literature under National Socialism and in Exile | 1933 – 1945 | book burning, exile, inner emigration     | Thomas Mann, Anna Seghers, Stefan Zweig |
| Post-war literature                     | c. 1945 – 1970   | rubble literature, short story, Group 47          | Borchert, Böll, Grass, Dürrenmatt      |
| Austrian literature after 1945          | from 1945        | scepticism of language, anti-Heimat literature    | Bachmann, Celan, Bernhard, Handke, Jelinek |
| Contemporary literature                 | from c. 1990     | diversity, memory, migration                      | Kehlmann, Stanišić, Seethaler          |
