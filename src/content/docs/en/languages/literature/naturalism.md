**Naturalism** radicalised [Realism](/en/languages/literature/realism/): reality was to be reproduced without any transfiguration and as precisely as possible, including the ugly, the sick and the miserable. The authors saw themselves almost as scientists observing people as in an experiment.

## Key facts

- c. 1880 – 1900
- centres: Berlin and Munich
- exact depiction of reality, no transfiguration
- people as a product of heredity and milieu
- the social question, misery of the working class, alcoholism, illness
- second-by-second style, dialect and colloquial language
- most important genre: drama

## Historical background

**Industrialisation** led to rapid growth of the cities. Workers lived in cramped tenements, worked up to 14 hours a day and had hardly any social security. The social question became the great issue of the time, and the labour movement and social democracy gained importance. The natural sciences celebrated great successes. Positivism assumed that only what can be observed scientifically counts as knowledge.

## Features

- **Determinism:** According to the French philosopher Hippolyte Taine, people are determined by heredity, milieu (social environment) and the historical moment. They have hardly any free will.
- **Art = nature − x:** Arno Holz formulated this art formula: art tends to become nature again, and the $x$ stands for the unavoidable shortcomings of the means of representation. The aim is to make this $x$ as small as possible.
- **Second-by-second style:** Actions, noises and conversations are described so precisely that narrative time and narrated time almost coincide.
- **Language:** Dialect, colloquial language, incomplete sentences and stuttering are reproduced as they occur in real life.
- **Themes:** poverty, exploitation, alcoholism, prostitution, illness, crime and the lives of "ordinary people" in the big city.
- **Hero:** Instead of an individual, a group or a collective is often at the centre.

## Authors and works

### Gerhart Hauptmann (1862 – 1946)

Hauptmann is the most important playwright of German Naturalism. In 1912, he received the Nobel Prize in Literature.

- **"Vor Sonnenaufgang"** (Before Sunrise, 1889): the drama about a farming family that has become rich through coal deposits and fallen into alcoholism caused a theatre scandal at its premiere.
- **"Die Weber"** (The Weavers, 1892): the drama deals with the uprising of the Silesian weavers in 1844. The hero is not an individual but the group of starving weavers who rise up against the manufacturer Dreißiger. The play was initially banned because it was seen as a call to class struggle. Hauptmann originally wrote it in the Silesian dialect ("De Waber").
- **"Bahnwärter Thiel"** (Flagman Thiel, 1888): the novella-like study tells of a railway signalman who goes mad after the death of his son, caused by his second wife, and kills his wife and child.
- **"Die Ratten"** (The Rats, 1911): a late tragicomedy with naturalistic features.

### Arno Holz (1863 – 1929) and Johannes Schlaf (1862 – 1941)

With the prose sketch "**Papa Hamlet**" (1889) and the drama "Die Familie Selicke" (The Selicke Family, 1890), Holz and Schlaf developed consistent Naturalism and the second-by-second style. Holz also wrote the art theory "Die Kunst. Ihr Wesen und ihre Gesetze" (Art. Its Nature and Its Laws, 1891).

### Models from abroad

- **Émile Zola** (1840 – 1902): the Frenchman is considered the founder of Naturalism. In his 20-volume cycle of novels "Les Rougon-Macquart", he examined how heredity and milieu shape a family over generations, for example in "Germinal" about miners.
- **Henrik Ibsen** (1828 – 1906): the Norwegian wrote socially critical dramas such as "A Doll's House" and "Ghosts".
- Leo Tolstoy and Fyodor Dostoevsky from Russia also had a strong influence on the authors.

## Naturalism in Austria

Naturalism hardly took hold in Austria. As early as 1891, the critic **Hermann Bahr** proclaimed the "overcoming of Naturalism". In Vienna, Viennese Modernism with [Impressionism](/en/languages/literature/impressionism/) developed instead as a countermovement.

## Genres

- **Drama:** social drama, the most important genre, because dialogue and milieu can be depicted most precisely on stage
- **Epic:** novella, sketch, novel (especially in France)
- **Poetry:** big-city poetry, rather unimportant
