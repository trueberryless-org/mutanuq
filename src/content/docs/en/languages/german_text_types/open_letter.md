The open letter (Offener Brief) is an appellative, opinion-based text type written in the present tense and the present perfect. It is addressed to a specific person or institution (for example a mayor, a ministry or a company) but is made **public**, for instance in a newspaper, on a website or on social media. So you write for two groups at once: the person addressed and the public, who read along and are meant to build up pressure.

:::note[Matura]
Since the main exam date in 2020, the open letter is no longer part of the catalogue of text types for the written SRDP in German. It is, however, still set in class tests, and its structure and argumentation technique also help you with the [letter to the editor](/en/languages/german_text_types/letter_to_the_editor/) and the [opinion speech](/en/languages/german_text_types/opinion_speech/).
:::

## Characteristics

An open letter has the following characteristics:

-   a clear addressee (person or institution) who is addressed directly
-   a public concern that affects many people
-   `Ich` (I) or `Wir` (we) may and should be used (you speak as a person or for a group)
-   a factual and polite but firm tone, no insults
-   arguments with evidence and examples
-   concrete demands or proposed solutions and an appeal
-   letter form with header, subject line, salutation, closing and signature
-   reference to the source text if the task provides one
-   write at most 50 words more than required
-   medium-length text type

### Open letter vs letter to the editor

| Feature    | Letter to the editor                              | Open letter                                                  |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Addressee  | editorial team or author of a newspaper text      | a person or institution with decision-making power          |
| Occasion   | reaction to a published text                      | a problem or decision you want to change                     |
| Aim        | express your opinion on the article               | publicly move the addressee to act                           |
| Marking    | none                                              | subject line or heading „Offener Brief an ...“               |
| Length     | rather short                                      | rather longer, with detailed reasoning                       |

## Structure

### Header

As in every letter, the place and date go in the _top right_ corner.

> Linz, 12. März 2026

In a real letter, the sender's and recipient's addresses follow. In a class test, place and date are usually enough unless the task asks for more.

### Subject line

The subject line immediately makes clear that this is an open letter and what it is about.

> Offener Brief an Bürgermeisterin Maria Huber: Bitte erhalten Sie die Stadtbibliothek!
> (Open letter to Mayor Maria Huber: please keep the city library!)

### Salutation

Address the person with their title and function. The same capitalisation rule as for the letter to the editor applies after the salutation: after a comma you continue in lowercase, after an exclamation mark in uppercase.

> Sehr geehrte Frau Bürgermeisterin Huber,

> Sehr geehrte Damen und Herren des Bildungsministeriums!

### Introduction

In the introduction, you briefly introduce yourself (or the group you are writing for), name the occasion and make clear why the letter is public. If the task provides a source text, you include it here with the most important details (title, author, medium, date, topic).

> Als Schülerinnen und Schüler der HTL Linz wenden wir uns mit diesem offenen Brief an Sie, weil der Gemeinderat am 5. März beschlossen hat, die Stadtbibliothek Ende des Jahres zu schließen. Wie der Artikel „Leere Regale“ von Paul Gruber, erschienen am 7. März 2026 in den „Oberösterreichischen Nachrichten“, berichtet, ...

### Main part

In the main part, you first briefly describe the problem and your position. Your arguments follow, each in its own paragraph. Build each argument completely (claim, reason, example, for instance with the 3B method) and anticipate possible counter-arguments of the addressee in order to refute them.

As the public reads along, it pays to refer to shared values and interests (education, safety, health, taxpayers' money). Stay factual, though: an open letter that insults loses the support of its readers.

### Conclusion

Summarise your most important demand once more and formulate a clear appeal to the person addressed. The conclusion often ends with a concrete offer (a meeting, cooperation) or a request for an answer by a certain date.

> Wir bitten Sie daher, die Entscheidung in der nächsten Gemeinderatssitzung noch einmal zu prüfen, und laden Sie herzlich zu einem Gespräch an unserer Schule ein.

### Closing and signature

> Mit freundlichen Grüßen

The signature follows. In an open letter from a group, there are often several names or a designation such as „Die Schülervertretung der HTL Linz“ (the student council of HTL Linz).

## Useful phrases

| Purpose            | Phrase                                                                                                      |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| state the occasion | Mit diesem offenen Brief wende ich mich an Sie, weil ... (I am writing to you with this open letter because ...) |
| going public       | Wir wählen den Weg der Öffentlichkeit, da dieses Thema alle ... betrifft. (We are going public because ...) |
| position           | Wir sind überzeugt, dass ... (We are convinced that ...)                                                    |
| counter-argument   | Zwar wird häufig eingewandt, dass ..., doch ... (It is often objected that ..., but ...)                    |
| demand             | Wir fordern Sie deshalb auf, ... (We therefore call on you to ...)                                          |
| polite appeal      | Wir bitten Sie eindringlich, ... (We urge you to ...)                                                       |
| closing            | Wir freuen uns auf Ihre Antwort und stehen für ein Gespräch gerne zur Verfügung. (We look forward to your reply and are happy to meet.) |
