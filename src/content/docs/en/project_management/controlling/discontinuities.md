Sometimes something happens in a project that can no longer be handled with the normal control measures. Such sudden, fundamental changes are called **discontinuities**. They can be negative (crises) or positive (opportunities).

## Risk, issue and crisis

| Term     | Description                                                                                   | Example                                                     |
| -------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Risk     | An event could occur and endanger the objectives.                                             | The supplier could deliver the servers late.                |
| Issue    | A risk has occurred but can be solved with the project's normal means.                        | The servers arrive a week late, and the float is sufficient. |
| Crisis   | The project objectives or even the project itself are seriously endangered, and the normal means are no longer enough. | The supplier is insolvent, the hardware can no longer be delivered, and the go-live date cannot be kept. |

A crisis can be recognised by the fact that the project organisation, the plans or even the objectives have to be changed fundamentally.

## Possible causes of crises

- absence of the project manager or several key team members
- insolvency of a supplier or customer
- massive cost or schedule overruns
- fundamental technical problems, such as an architecture that cannot meet the requirements
- serious security incidents or data loss
- withdrawal of support by management or a change in company strategy
- escalated conflicts in the team or with the client

## Crisis management

Handling a crisis follows its own sequence:

1. **Recognise and declare the crisis:** The project manager identifies the crisis and officially declares it together with the client. This makes it clear to everyone that special rules now apply.
2. **Take immediate action:** limit the damage, for example take a faulty system offline, stop work or inform important people.
3. **Analyse the causes:** What happened, and why? Which objectives are affected?
4. **Develop strategies:** collect and evaluate possible solutions, such as changed objectives, additional resources, a different technical solution or, in the extreme case, cancelling the project.
5. **Decide and implement:** The client decides on the strategy, the plans are adjusted and the measures are implemented.
6. **End the crisis:** Once the situation is under control again, the end of the crisis is officially declared.
7. **Learn:** The crisis is reviewed, and the findings are documented for future projects.

During a crisis, special rules apply:

- Decision paths are shortened, and often a small crisis team is formed.
- Communication is particularly important: affected stakeholders are informed actively and honestly before they hear about it from other sources.
- Only one designated person speaks to the outside world.

:::caution
The greatest danger is recognising a crisis too late or not wanting to accept it. Problems are then glossed over until there are no options left. An open culture of learning from mistakes and honest status reports help to recognise crises early.
:::

## Cancelling a project

Sometimes cancelling is the best decision, for example if the benefit has disappeared or the costs clearly exceed the benefit. Money that has already been spent must not play a role, because it is lost in any case (sunk costs). The only thing that matters is whether the remaining effort is still worthwhile. Even a cancelled project is closed properly so that results are saved and experience is used.

## Opportunities

Discontinuities can also be positive, for example:

- a new customer who also wants to buy the solution being developed
- a new technology that makes a task much easier
- a funding programme that provides additional resources
- a team member with unexpected expertise

Opportunities also require decisions: will the project be extended? Will objectives change? An opportunity is often pursued as a separate follow-up project so as not to endanger the current project.
