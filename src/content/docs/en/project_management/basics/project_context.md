A project never takes place in a vacuum. It has a history, affects other projects and concerns many people who are not part of the project team. The analysis of this environment is called **project context analysis**.

## Scoping the project

First, it is defined what belongs to the project and what does not. This prevents more and more tasks from being added over the course of the project without adjusting time and budget.

- **Content scope:** Which deliverables belong to the project (_in scope_), and which explicitly do not (_out of scope_)?
- **Time scope:** When does the project begin, and when does it end? A good starting point is the signing of the project order, a good end point is acceptance by the client.
- **Organisational scope:** Who belongs to the project team, and which departments does it have interfaces with?

## Temporal context

The temporal context looks at what happens before and after the project:

- In the **pre-project phase**, the project idea was developed. There may already have been a feasibility study, offers or earlier attempts that failed. These experiences feed into the project.
- In the **post-project phase**, the results are used and maintained. Who maintains the new software? Who answers users' questions? These tasks must be clarified during the project.

## Factual context

The factual context describes the connections to other undertakings and to the company strategy:

- What contribution does the project make to the company's goals?
- Are there other projects that need the same resources or whose results the project needs?
- Which systems, contracts or regulations are affected?

## Stakeholder analysis

**Stakeholders** are all people and groups that can influence the project or are affected by it. These include internal stakeholders such as management, business departments and the works council, but also external ones such as customers, suppliers, authorities or the public.

The stakeholder analysis has four steps:

1. **Identify:** Collect all stakeholders, for example in a brainstorming session.
2. **Analyse:** For each stakeholder, record their expectations and concerns, how much influence they have and what their attitude to the project is.
3. **Assess:** Rate the relationship, for example on a scale from positive (+) through neutral (o) to negative (−).
4. **Plan measures:** For important stakeholders, decide how they will be involved or informed.

### Example: introducing a new ticket system

| Stakeholder         | Expectations and concerns                                      | Influence | Relationship | Measures                                               |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | --------- | ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Management          | faster handling of requests, costs within budget               | high      | +            | monthly status report, invitation to milestones        |
| IT support          | less chaos, but worried about extra work during the changeover | high      | o            | involve early in testing, training before go-live      |
| Employees           | easy to use, fear of being monitored                           | medium    | −            | information event, clear rules on evaluations          |
| Works council       | data protection, no performance monitoring                     | high      | o            | inform early, works agreement                          |
| Software supplier   | follow-up orders, clear requirements                           | medium    | +            | regular coordination meetings                          |

### Stakeholder matrix

A clear way to present stakeholders is a matrix of their **influence** and **interest**:

|                      | low interest                | high interest                   |
| -------------------- | --------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| **high influence**   | keep satisfied              | manage closely (key players)    |
| **low influence**    | monitor                     | keep informed                   |

:::tip
The stakeholder analysis is not a one-off step. Attitudes change during the project, so you should review it at every milestone.
:::
