Projects constantly need new ideas: for the project name, for technical solutions, for collecting risks or stakeholders. **Creativity techniques** help to find many ideas in a short time and to leave habitual ways of thinking.

Most techniques separate two phases: first, as many ideas as possible are collected without evaluating them. Only then are they sorted and evaluated.

## Brainstorming

In **brainstorming**, a group of about 5 to 10 people spontaneously suggests ideas on a question. One person moderates and writes everything down visibly. Four rules apply:

1. No criticism while ideas are being collected.
2. Quantity over quality: the more ideas, the better.
3. Picking up and developing the ideas of others is encouraged.
4. Unusual and crazy ideas are welcome too.

A round usually lasts 15 to 30 minutes. One disadvantage is that quiet participants are often overlooked and dominant people set the direction.

## 6-3-5 brainwriting

The **6-3-5 method** is a written variant of brainstorming:

- 6 people
- each write 3 ideas on a form
- in 5 minutes.

Then the sheet is passed on, and the next person adds to or develops the existing ideas. After six rounds (30 minutes), up to 6 · 3 · 6 = 108 ideas have been created. Because everyone writes, reserved people also get their say.

## Mind map

A **mind map** organises thoughts around a central topic. Main branches go out from the topic in the middle and split into ever finer branches. Mind maps are well suited to structuring a topic, for example as a preliminary step to the [work breakdown structure](/en/project_management/planning/work_breakdown_structure/).

## Morphological box

With the **morphological box**, a problem is broken down into its features. For each feature, possible options are collected. Combining one option per row produces many solution variants.

| Feature        | Option 1       | Option 2        | Option 3        |
| -------------- | -------------- | --------------- | --------------- |
| Platform       | web            | Android and iOS | desktop         |
| Sign-in        | username and password | school account (SSO) | no sign-in |
| Data storage   | own database   | cloud service   | spreadsheet     |
| Notification   | email          | push message    | none            |

One possible combination would be a web app with school account, own database and email notification.

## Walt Disney method

In the **Walt Disney method**, an idea is looked at from three roles one after the other:

- **Dreamer:** What would be ideal if everything were possible?
- **Realist:** How can the idea be put into practice? What is needed for it?
- **Critic:** What could go wrong? What is still missing?

All participants take on the same role at the same time. The round is repeated until the idea survives the criticism.

## Six thinking hats

Edward de Bono's method assigns each way of thinking a hat of a certain colour:

| Hat     | Way of thinking                                     |
| ------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| white   | facts, figures and missing information              |
| red     | feelings, intuition and gut feeling                 |
| black   | risks, concerns and weaknesses                      |
| yellow  | opportunities, advantages and benefits              |
| green   | new ideas, alternatives and creative solutions      |
| blue    | overview and moderation of the thinking process     |

By deliberately switching hats, a topic is examined from all sides without optimists and pessimists blocking each other.

## Further techniques

- **Reverse brainstorming:** The question is turned around ("How do we make sure the project is guaranteed to fail?"). Solutions are then derived from the answers.
- **Osborn checklist:** An existing solution is changed with questions such as "What can be enlarged, reduced, replaced, combined or reversed?"
- **Analogy technique:** You look for solutions in other areas, for example in nature.

## Evaluating ideas

After collecting ideas, the best ones must be selected.

With **dot voting**, each person gets a certain number of dots and distributes them among the ideas they like best.

A **weighted scoring model** (also utility analysis) is more precise: first, criteria are defined and weighted (100 % in total). Then each variant is rated per criterion, for example with 1 to 10 points. The points are multiplied by the weighting and added up.

| Criterion         | Weight | Web app: points | Web app: weighted | Native app: points | Native app: weighted |
| ----------------- | ------ | --------------- | ----------------- | ------------------ | -------------------- |
| Costs             | 40 %   | 8               | 3.2               | 5                  | 2.0                  |
| Usability         | 30 %   | 6               | 1.8               | 9                  | 2.7                  |
| Maintenance effort | 20 %  | 8               | 1.6               | 4                  | 0.8                  |
| Offline capability | 10 %  | 3               | 0.3               | 9                  | 0.9                  |
| Total             | 100 %  |                 | 6.9               |                    | 6.4                  |

In this example, the web app achieves the higher score.
