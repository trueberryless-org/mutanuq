Wherever people with different goals, opinions and interests work together, conflicts arise. Conflicts are not bad in principle: they show that something is wrong and can lead to better solutions. They become dangerous when they are ignored and escalate.

## Types of conflict

| Type of conflict       | Cause                                                            | Example                                                           |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Factual conflict       | different opinions about the right way                           | React or Angular for the front end?                               |
| Conflict of objectives | different goals of the people involved                           | sales wants many features, controlling wants low costs            |
| Distribution conflict  | dispute over scarce resources                                    | two projects need the same database expert at the same time       |
| Role conflict          | unclear or contradictory expectations of a role                  | head of department and project manager give contradictory instructions |
| Relationship conflict  | personal dislike, hurt feelings, lack of trust                   | two team members only communicate by email                        |
| Value conflict         | different fundamental beliefs                                    | quality at any price or pragmatically "good enough"               |

A seemingly factual conflict is often a relationship conflict underneath. As long as that is not resolved, the factual questions can hardly be settled.

### Signs of conflict

- Information is withheld or only exchanged in writing.
- Meetings are cancelled or tense.
- Groups form that talk about each other instead of with each other.
- Deadlines are missed, and motivation drops.

## Glasl's stages of escalation

Friedrich Glasl, an Austrian conflict researcher, describes nine stages in which a conflict can escalate. They are grouped into three levels:

| Level       | Stage | Name                           | Description                                                      |
| ----------- | ----- | ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| win-win     | 1     | Hardening                      | Positions harden, but people still believe in a solution through talking. |
| win-win     | 2     | Debate and polemics            | black-and-white thinking, verbal confrontations, each side wants to appear superior |
| win-win     | 3     | Actions instead of words       | People no longer believe in talking and create facts.            |
| win-lose    | 4     | Images and coalitions          | People look for allies and attribute negative traits to the opponent. |
| win-lose    | 5     | Loss of face                   | The opponent is to be exposed in public.                         |
| win-lose    | 6     | Strategies of threat           | Threats and ultimatums are meant to put the opponent under pressure. |
| lose-lose   | 7     | Limited destructive blows      | The opponent is to be harmed, and one's own losses are accepted. |
| lose-lose   | 8     | Fragmentation                  | The aim is to destroy the opponent's power base.                 |
| lose-lose   | 9     | Together into the abyss        | Destroying the opponent matters more than one's own survival.    |

At the first three stages, the people involved can usually resolve the conflict themselves. From stage 4 onwards, help from outside is usually needed, for example from the project manager, a facilitator or a mediator. From stage 7 onwards, often only a higher authority can intervene.

## Strategies for resolving conflicts

According to the Thomas-Kilmann model, there are five basic ways of behaving in a conflict:

- **Competing:** One side pushes through its position. This is fast but damages the relationship.
- **Accommodating:** One side gives up its interests to protect the relationship.
- **Avoiding:** The conflict is not addressed. This can make sense for unimportant topics, but often only postpones the problem.
- **Compromising:** Both sides give in a little and meet in the middle.
- **Collaborating:** Both sides look for a solution together that meets everyone's interests. This takes effort but leads to the most sustainable results.

## Harvard negotiation method

The **Harvard negotiation method** (principled negotiation) is based on four principles:

1. **Separate the people from the problem:** hard on the issue, soft on the people.
2. **Focus on interests, not positions:** Behind the position "I want front-end framework X" there may be the interest "I want to work with a technology I know so that we meet the deadline".
3. **Invent options for mutual gain:** first collect several possible solutions, then decide.
4. **Insist on objective criteria:** make decisions on the basis of comprehensible standards, such as costs, measurements or norms.

## Escalation in a project

If a conflict cannot be resolved at one level, it is passed on to the next higher level. This **escalation path** should already be defined at the start of the project:

1. The team members concerned try to resolve the conflict themselves.
2. The project manager moderates or decides.
3. The client or the steering committee decides.

Escalation is not a sign of weakness, but a regulated way of resolving deadlocks.

## Mediation

In **mediation**, a neutral third party helps the parties to the conflict find a solution themselves. The mediator does not decide, but structures the conversation. A prerequisite is that both sides take part voluntarily.
