For a successful company foundation, it is essential for the founder to have an interesting business **idea** and a strong **will**. In addition, **personal** and **trade-law requirements** must be met to be able to found a company. It is also strongly recommended to involve the Austrian Economic Chamber (for example the [WKO founder service](https://www.gruenderservice.at/)) for support during the founding phase.

## Advantages

- realising your own ideas
- being your own boss
- choosing your own working hours and environment
- profit for your own performance
- social recognition if successful
- self-fulfilment, because you can organise your life (to a limited extent) freely

## Disadvantages

- high time commitment
- liability often with private assets + responsibility
- variable income depends on the economy
- often on your own

## Business idea

Without its own business idea, a company cannot stand out from the competition. There are different ways to come up with a business idea.

### Finding your own business idea

#### New idea

- paying attention to the needs, trends and problems of society

#### Improving proven ideas

- Not every company has to develop a new product. Often, they just improve existing ideas and develop them further.
- offering services

#### Taking over outsourced tasks

- Sometimes, when a company outsources tasks, the employee who was previously responsible for this task can now work self-employed.

### Using existing business ideas

#### Company takeover

A company takeover means taking over an already existing company, basically replacing the managing director and becoming self-employed.

> [Succession exchange](https://firmen.wko.at/suche_nachfolgeboerse/), [franchise exchange](https://www.wko.at/gruendung/franchiseboerse?shorturl=franchiseb%F6rseat), [Komma-net](https://www.wirtschaftswissen.de/unternehmensfuehrung/marketing/unternehmenskommunikation/)

##### Advantages

- less investment
- existing customer base / reputation / name of the company
- saving bureaucracy (existing administration)
- cheaper than starting from scratch

##### Disadvantages

- possibly bad reputation
- process changes usually unwelcome (existing structures / hierarchies)  
  _"We've always done it this way"_
- defects or liabilities (e.g. debts) are also taken over
- outdated resources

#### Franchising

Franchising refers to taking over an already proven business concept. The franchisee is legally an independent company, but may use the name, the know-how and the marketing measures of the franchisor in return for a fee.

##### Advantages

- the brand is already established (McDonald's)
- support from the franchisor

##### Disadvantages

- fees for the know-how provided
- little room for manoeuvre

### Checking the business idea

Before implementing an idea, you should check whether there is a market for the product at all. The idea should meet the following requirements:

- a benefit for potential customers
- standing out from the competition
- innovative (customers must see a reason to buy the product / service)
- clear and well thought out
- profitable in the longer term

## USP

The unique selling proposition (USP) is a marketing concept that highlights the unique characteristics of a product, service or brand in order to set it apart from the competition. The USP should emphasise something unique and valuable, be clear and concise, be tailored to the needs of the target group, be communicated consistently and ideally remain relevant in the long term.

> Volvo, safest car; Apple, iPhone offers an unparalleled user experience; FedEx, delivers overnight; Starbucks, handcrafted coffee made to individual wishes

##### Advantages

### Trade-law requirements

A **trade licence** (Gewerbeberechtigung) is required for a commercial activity. It is issued by a trade authority.

An entrepreneurial activity is a commercial activity if it is carried out

- legally (not against laws or good morals)
- independently (on your own account and at your own risk)
- regularly (as repeated actions)
- for profit (intention to earn something)

#### General requirements

- legal capacity (of age and able to enter into contracts)
- citizenship of an EEA state or Switzerland, or a residence permit that allows self-employment
- no grounds for exclusion (court convictions, criminal offences, bankruptcy, insolvency)

#### Proof of qualification

For certain activities, a proof of qualification (Befähigungsnachweis) must also be provided. This means that the entrepreneur must have special professional and entrepreneurial skills and experience.

#### Reliability check

For businesses with a high level of responsibility, a reliability check (Zuverlässigkeitsprüfung) is also necessary. There must be no violations in connection with the trade in question.

#### Types of trades

There are different types of trades, each with different requirements.

##### Free trades

- general requirements

> advertising agency, petrol station, tanning studio, entertainers, babysitting, market research

##### Regulated trades

###### Without reliability check

- general requirements
- proof of qualification

> crafts (bakers, locksmiths, confectioners), catering and hospitality businesses, tourist guides

###### With reliability check

- general requirements
- proof of qualification
- reliability check

> master builders, travel agencies, arms trade, financial advice, electrical engineering

#### Activities outside the Trade Act

Entrepreneurial activities that do not fall under the Trade Act (Gewerbeordnung) are subject to other laws and do **not** require a trade licence.

> liberal professions (doctors, notaries, physiotherapists), new self-employed (lecturers)

## Legal forms

When founding a company, you have to choose a legal form. Among other things, it determines who is liable, how much capital is needed and how profits are taxed.

| Legal form                                 | Founders            | Minimum capital | Liability                                                           |
| ------------------------------------------ | ------------------- | --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Sole proprietorship (e. U.)**            | 1 person            | none            | unlimited with all private assets                                   |
| **General partnership (OG)**               | at least 2 people   | none            | all partners unlimited and jointly                                  |
| **Limited partnership (KG)**               | at least 2 people   | none            | general partner unlimited, limited partner only up to their contribution |
| **Limited liability company (GmbH)**       | 1 or more people    | €10,000         | only with the company's assets                                      |
| **Flexible company (FlexCo)**              | 1 or more people    | €10,000         | only with the company's assets; easier employee participation       |
| **Stock corporation (AG)**                 | 1 or more people    | €70,000         | only with the company's assets                                      |

Sole proprietorships, OG and KG are **partnerships** in which the founders are personally liable. GmbH, FlexCo and AG are **corporations** that are liable themselves as separate legal entities. A sole proprietorship only has to be entered in the commercial register once its annual turnover exceeds €700,000, but can do so voluntarily (then with the suffix "e. U.").

## Founding steps

In Austria, founding a company typically works like this:

1. **Advice** from the founder service of the Economic Chamber and writing the business plan.
2. Obtaining the **NeuFöG confirmation**: with the confirmation under the New Business Promotion Act (Neugründungs-Förderungsgesetz), certain fees and charges for the foundation are waived.
3. **Trade registration** with the district administrative authority or online via the Trade Information System Austria (GISA). This automatically makes you a member of the Economic Chamber.
4. **Social insurance**: the self-employed are insured with the social insurance institution for the self-employed (SVS).
5. **Tax office**: notification of the start of the activity and assignment of a tax number and, if required, a VAT identification number.
6. For corporations: articles of association (for a GmbH via a notary) and **entry in the commercial register**.

## Business plan

The business plan helps the founder to write down the idea of the company in a clear and understandable way. It is also required by banks, investors and funding bodies. It should contain goals, expectations, the difference from competitors and everything else the reader might be interested in.

In addition, thanks to the planning and steering in the business plan, you can expect a better and easier implementation.

### Structure and content of the business plan

- Executive summary: This part is a condensed summary of the business plan. It is decisive for whether the reader looks at the business plan in more detail. Although the executive summary comes first, it is written last.

- Company: Describe the foundation on which the company is built and also list the company and founder profile as well as the company goals.

- **Products / services**: Describe the idea of the product / service and the benefit for potential customers.

- Industry, market, competition: State whether the market potential of the company is high enough and whether enough products / services can be sold, since the success of a company depends on its sales market.

- Marketing: Describe a cost-effective way of conveying information to customers.

- Management, key people and organisation: Describe the planned steps and activities needed to sell the desired product to the customer. Which people will you involve? Perhaps you have already won over a business angel who contributes not only capital but also experience and contacts to other companies.

- **Implementation roadmap**, **milestone plan**: The more detailed your planning, the easier the implementation. In addition, investors are all the more convinced of the company if there is a precise, efficient and as realistic as possible plan.

- Opportunities and risks: Analyse economic risks and opportunities that could influence the success of the company.

- **Financial plan**: Good financial planning is essential for building and developing any successful company. In financial planning, the considerations you made in the previous chapters are expressed in figures. Ask yourself the questions `How high is my capital requirement?` and `How is the capital to be raised?` Make sure to plan sufficient funds to remain solvent.
