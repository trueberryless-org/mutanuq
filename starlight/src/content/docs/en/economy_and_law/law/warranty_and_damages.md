## Warranty

The **warranty** is the seller's (or contractor's) statutory liability, regardless of fault, for the goods being free from defects when handed over. It applies to every contract for consideration, no matter whether the seller caused the defect or knew about it.

A **defect** exists if the goods do not have the agreed or usually expected properties:

- **Material defect:** the goods are damaged, do not work or do not have the promised properties (e.g. too little storage).
- **Defect of title:** the seller cannot transfer ownership, for example because the goods are stolen.
- **Quantity defect:** too little is delivered.

The warranty only covers defects that already existed **at the time of handover**, even if they only become visible later. Damage caused by improper handling or normal wear and tear is not covered.

### Periods

| Thing                         | Warranty period                                            |
| ----------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| movable things                | 2 years from handover                                  |
| immovable things              | 3 years                                                |

In consumer transactions, the period cannot be shortened, except to one year for used movable things if this has been individually negotiated. After the period has expired, the buyer still has **3 months** to assert their claims in court.

### Reversal of the burden of proof

In principle, the buyer must prove that the defect already existed at the time of handover. However, if the defect appears **within the first year** in a consumer transaction, it is presumed to have existed at handover. The seller would have to prove the opposite.

:::note[Consumer Warranty Act]
For consumer contracts for goods and digital services concluded from 2022, the **Consumer Warranty Act (VGG)** applies. Among other things, it also regulates an obligation to provide updates for goods with digital elements, such as security updates for smartphones.
:::

### Warranty remedies

The buyer's rights are divided into two stages:

1. **Primary remedies:** The buyer can initially only demand repair or replacement. The seller can refuse one of the two options if it is impossible or disproportionately expensive.
2. **Secondary remedies:** Only if repair and replacement are impossible, are refused, are not carried out within a reasonable period or are unreasonable for the buyer can the buyer demand a price reduction or, for defects that are not merely minor, rescission (withdrawal from the contract, return of the goods for a refund).

:::tip[Example]
After three months, a new laptop has a defective battery. The buyer asks the retailer (not the manufacturer!) to replace the battery. The retailer repairs the device within a week. If the laptop then fails again, the buyer can demand a price reduction or the reversal of the purchase.
:::

### Duty to give notice of defects

In mutual business transactions, the buyer must **inspect the goods immediately** after delivery and give notice of defects within a reasonable period (notice of defects, § 377 UGB). If they fail to do so, they lose their warranty claims.

## Guarantee

A **guarantee** is a voluntary promise, usually by the manufacturer, sometimes also by the retailer. Its content, duration and conditions are freely determined by the guarantor, for example "5-year guarantee on the motor".

| Warranty                                        | Guarantee                                            |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| required by law                                 | voluntary                                            |
| towards the contracting party (seller)      | usually towards the manufacturer                 |
| the defect must exist at handover               | can also cover defects that arise later              |
| 2 years (movable things)                        | any duration                                         |

The guarantee applies **in addition to** the warranty and must not restrict it. The buyer can choose which claim to assert.

## Damages

Anyone who unlawfully and culpably causes damage to another person must pay **damages**.

### Requirements

1. **Damage:** a disadvantage to assets, rights or the person (e.g. injury). A distinction is made between positive damage (reduction of existing assets) and loss of profit.
2. **Causation:** the conduct must have caused the damage.
3. **Unlawfulness:** the conduct violates a law, a contract or public morals.
4. **Fault:** the person causing the damage acted intentionally or negligently.

The extent of compensation depends on the degree of fault: with **slight negligence**, in principle only positive damage must be compensated; with gross negligence and intent, loss of profit must be compensated too (full satisfaction). If the injured party is also at fault, the compensation is divided.

Damage is primarily to be compensated by **restitution in kind** (restoring the previous state); if that is not possible or not practical, by monetary compensation.

:::tip[Example]
A technician negligently installs the wrong power supply at a customer's premises, destroying a server. The technician (or their employer) is liable for the value of the server. If they acted with gross negligence, they must also compensate the revenue the customer lost because of the outage.
:::

### Strict liability

In some areas, you are liable even without fault (**strict liability**), because a thing poses particular dangers, for example as the keeper of a motor vehicle or as the operator of a railway.

### Product liability

Under the **Product Liability Act**, the manufacturer (or the importer into the EEA) is liable regardless of fault if a defective product injures a person or damages a thing other than the product itself. If the manufacturer cannot be identified, the retailer is liable unless they name the manufacturer. Liability cannot be excluded by contract. For property damage, there is a deductible of €500.
