The **Unternehmensgesetzbuch (UGB, Business Code)** contains special rules for businesses. It supplements the ABGB: where the UGB does not regulate something, general civil law applies.

## Business status

A **business owner** (Unternehmer) is anyone who runs an **enterprise** – i.e. a permanent organisation of independent economic activity, even if it is not aimed at making a profit (§ 1 UGB).

- Corporations (GmbH, AG) are businesses **by virtue of their legal form**.
- Sole proprietors must be entered in the company register once their turnover exceeds **€700,000** per financial year and are then **obliged to keep accounts**; below that, registration is voluntary.
- **Liberal professions** (e.g. doctors, lawyers), farmers and foresters are partly exempt from the UGB.

Stricter rules apply to businesses than to private persons, for example the [duty to give notice of defects](/en/economy_and_law/law/warranty_and_damages/#duty-to-give-notice-of-defects) or higher default interest.

## Company name

The **company name** (Firma) is the **name** entered in the company register under which a business operates and signs. In everyday language, "Firma" is often used for the whole company – legally, however, it only means the name.

The company name can be formed as

- a **personal name** (from the owner's name): "Anna Huber e. U.",
- a **descriptive name** (from the object of the business): "Webdesign Krems GmbH",
- a **fantasy name**: "Mutanuq OG",
- or a combination of these.

The company name must include a **legal form suffix** (e.g. e. U., OG, KG, GmbH, AG).

### Principles of company names

| Principle                     | Meaning                                                                 |
| ----------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Truthfulness**              | The name must not mislead about essential business circumstances.       |
| **Exclusivity**               | It must be clearly distinguishable from other company names in the same place. |
| **Continuity**                | It may be continued with consent when the owner changes.                |
| **Uniqueness**                | There is only one company name for one business.                        |
| **Publicity**                 | The company name must be entered in the company register.               |

## Company register

The **company register** (Firmenbuch) is a public register kept by the regional courts (in Vienna by the Commercial Court). It contains, among other things,

- company name, legal form, registered office and business address,
- company register number (e.g. FN 123456 a),
- line of business,
- persons authorised to represent the company (managing directors, board members, holders of Prokura) and the type of their authority,
- shareholders and, for corporations, the share capital,
- for corporations, the submitted **annual accounts**.

Anyone can inspect the company register, for example via the Business Service Portal or authorised query services. This allows you, for example, to check who is authorised to represent a company before concluding a deal.

**Publicity effect:** Everyone must accept facts that have been entered and published. A business cannot rely on changes that have not been entered (e.g. the revocation of a Prokura) against third parties acting in good faith.

## Representation

A business owner cannot conclude all transactions personally. They are represented by **agents**: a transaction that an agent concludes in the name of the business within their **power of attorney** directly binds the business.

### Prokura

**Prokura** is the most comprehensive power of attorney in business law.

- It authorises **all judicial and extrajudicial transactions** involved in running **any** business.
- The only exceptions are selling and encumbering real estate (which requires special authorisation) and fundamental transactions concerning the business itself (e.g. selling the business, filing for insolvency, signing the annual accounts).
- It must be granted **expressly** and entered in the **company register**.
- Its scope **cannot be restricted** towards third parties (restrictions only apply internally).
- Holders of Prokura sign with the addition "**ppa.**" (per procura).
- **Joint Prokura** is also possible, in which two persons may only act together.

### Commercial power of attorney

The **commercial power of attorney** (Handlungsvollmacht) authorises transactions that **such** a business **usually** involves. It can be granted as a general commercial power of attorney (for all usual transactions), a specific power (for a certain type, e.g. purchasing) or a single power (for one transaction). It is not entered in the company register. Holders sign with "**i. V.**" (in Vertretung – on behalf of) or "**i. A.**" (im Auftrag – by order of).

## Legal forms compared

An overview of founders, minimum capital and liability can be found under [founding a company](/en/economy_and_law/company_foundation/#legal-forms). The following table adds the bodies, management and important advantages and disadvantages:

| Legal form | Management and representation | Advantages | Disadvantages |
| ---------- | ----------------------------- | ---------- | ------------- |
| **Sole proprietorship** | the owner alone | simple, inexpensive founding; sole decision-making | unlimited personal liability; capital only from the owner |
| **OG** (general partnership) | every partner (unless agreed otherwise) | no minimum capital; several people contribute capital and know-how | all partners are liable without limitation, jointly and severally, even for the others' mistakes |
| **KG** (limited partnership) | only the general partners | limited partners can contribute capital without full liability | the general partner is liable without limitation |
| **GmbH** (limited company) | **managing directors**; general meeting of shareholders; supervisory board only from a certain size | liability limited to the company's assets | founding with a notarial deed; costs; disclosure obligations; banks often demand personal guarantees |
| **AG** (public limited company) | **management board**; **supervisory board** controls; **general meeting** of shareholders | raising large amounts of capital via the stock exchange is possible; shares are easily transferable | high minimum capital, complex founding and strict regulations |

The profits of corporations are subject to **corporate income tax**; for sole proprietorships and partnerships, they are subject to the partners' **income tax** (see [taxes](/en/economy_and_law/accounting/taxes/)).
