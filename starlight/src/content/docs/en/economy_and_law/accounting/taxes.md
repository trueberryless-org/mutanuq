**Taxes** are payments to the state for which there is no direct service in return. They are used to finance schools, roads, healthcare and pensions, for example. In Austria, most taxes are collected by the tax office.

A distinction is made between

- direct taxes, which are borne and paid by the taxpayer themselves (e.g. income tax, corporate income tax), and
- indirect taxes, which are passed on to others via the price (e.g. VAT, mineral oil tax).

**Taxes on income** tax income or profit: income tax (including its forms of collection, wage tax and capital gains tax) and corporate income tax.

## Income tax

Natural persons resident in Austria are subject to **income tax** on their entire income. The Income Tax Act distinguishes seven types of income:

| Business income (profit)                   | Non-business income (surplus)            |
| ------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------- |
| agriculture and forestry                   | employment (wages, salaries, pensions)   |
| self-employment (liberal professions)      | capital assets                           |
| trade or business                          | letting and leasing                      |
|                                            | other income (e.g. speculative transactions) |

### Tax rates

Income tax is **progressive**: the higher the income, the higher the tax rate for the part above each threshold (marginal tax rate). The following brackets apply for 2025:

| Part of income (per year)        | Tax rate   |
| -------------------------------- | ---------- |
| up to €13,308                    | 0 %        |
| €13,308 to €21,617               | 20 %       |
| €21,617 to €35,836               | 30 %       |
| €35,836 to €69,166               | 40 %       |
| €69,166 to €103,072              | 48 %       |
| €103,072 to €1 million           | 50 %       |
| over €1 million                  | 55 %       |

The thresholds are adjusted for inflation every year to offset **bracket creep**.

:::tip[Example]
The web designer from the [cash-basis accounting](/en/economy_and_law/accounting/cash_basis_accounting/) example has a profit of €26,750 (her only income):

$$
\begin{aligned}
(21\,617 - 13\,308) \cdot 20\,\% &= 1\,661.80\ \text{€} \\
(26\,750 - 21\,617) \cdot 30\,\% &= 1\,539.90\ \text{€} \\
\hline
\text{Income tax} &= 3\,201.70\ \text{€}
\end{aligned}
$$

This corresponds to an **average tax rate** of around 12 %, although her marginal tax rate is 30 %.
:::

The tax burden is reduced by **tax credits** (which are deducted directly from the tax, e.g. the family bonus or the commuter tax credit) and by special expenses and extraordinary burdens (which reduce the income).

**Self-employed people** must submit an income tax return after the end of the year and make quarterly advance payments during the year.

### Wage tax and employee tax assessment

For employees, income tax is withheld directly by the employer as **wage tax** and paid to the tax office (see [payroll](/en/economy_and_law/accounting/payroll/)). For the calculation, the annual income is extrapolated.

With the **employee tax assessment**, you can get back wage tax that you paid in excess, for example if you did not work the whole year or want to claim income-related expenses (e.g. specialist literature, further training, commuter allowance), special expenses or tax credits. It can be filed up to 5 years retrospectively. People who earn little can receive a negative tax (a refund of social insurance contributions).

### Capital gains tax

**Capital gains tax** is a form of collecting income tax on investment income. It is deducted directly by the bank or company and is therefore usually final: the income does not have to be declared in the tax return.

- 25 % on interest from savings and bank accounts,
- 27.5 % on most other investment income, such as dividends, bond interest and capital gains from shares and funds.

## Corporate income tax

Corporations (limited companies and public limited companies) pay **corporate income tax** of 23 % on their profits. Even if they make losses, they pay a minimum corporate income tax (for a limited company currently €500 per year), which is credited against later profitable years.

If the profit is distributed to the shareholders, an additional 27.5 % capital gains tax is due. The total burden is therefore around 44 %, similar to the top rate of income tax.

## Value added tax

**Value added tax (VAT)** is levied on almost all supplies and services provided by businesses. It is ultimately borne by the end consumer.

| Tax rate     | Application (examples)                                           |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 20 %     | standard rate: most goods and services, including IT services    |
| 10 %     | food, books and newspapers, residential rent, passenger transport, accommodation |
| 13 %     | cultural events, animal feed, plants and flowers, works of art   |

### Input VAT deduction

Businesses pay VAT to their suppliers when they buy. They can reclaim this **input VAT** from the tax office. Only the difference, the VAT payable, is paid to the tax office:

$$
\text{VAT payable} = \text{VAT (on sales)} - \text{Input VAT (on purchases)}
$$

This means that only the **added value** is taxed at each stage, and the VAT is cost-neutral for the business itself (a "transitory item").

:::tip[Example]
| Stage       | Net price | VAT 20 % | Input VAT | VAT payable |
| ----------- | --------- | -------- | --------- | ----------- |
| manufacturer sells to retailer | €100 | €20 | €0 | €20 |
| retailer sells to customer     | €150 | €30 | €20 | €10 |
| Total paid to the tax office |    |     |     | €30 |

The end customer pays €180, which includes €30 VAT, exactly 20 % of the net final price.
:::

Businesses report VAT in the **advance VAT return**, monthly or, with a turnover of up to €100,000 in the previous year, quarterly, and pay the VAT due by the 15th of the second following month. After the end of the year, the annual VAT return follows.

### Small business exemption

Businesses with an annual turnover of no more than **€55,000 gross** (the threshold since 2025) are exempt from VAT. They do not charge VAT but may not deduct input VAT either. They can opt out of the exemption (standard taxation), which can be worthwhile, for example, with high investments or mainly business customers.

### Reverse charge

For certain services between businesses, such as services to businesses in other EU states, liability for the tax passes to the **recipient** (reverse charge). The invoice is then issued without VAT but with a note on the transfer of tax liability and the VAT identification numbers of both parties.

## Valid VAT invoices

For a business to deduct input VAT, the invoice must contain certain information (§ 11 of the Austrian VAT Act):

1. name and address of the supplier
2. name and address of the recipient
3. quantity and usual trade description of the goods or the type and scope of the service
4. date or period of the supply or service
5. consideration (net amount) and the applicable tax rate
6. tax amount
7. date of issue
8. consecutive invoice number
9. VAT identification number of the supplier
10. for a total amount over €10,000 (gross), also the recipient's VAT identification number

For **small-amount invoices** up to €400 (gross), simplified information is sufficient: the supplier's name and address, quantity and description, date of supply, gross amount and tax rate, and the date of issue.
