The success of a business depends to a large extent on motivated employees. Leading people means influencing their behaviour in a targeted way, so that they do their work well and willingly.

## Motivation

Motivation is the willingness to commit to a goal. A distinction is made between

- **intrinsic motivation**: the activity itself is enjoyable or seems meaningful,
- **extrinsic motivation**: people act because of external incentives such as money or praise, or to avoid punishment.

Intrinsic motivation usually lasts longer. Rewards that are too strong can even crowd it out.

## Motivation theories

### Maslow's hierarchy of needs

The psychologist Abraham Maslow arranged human needs in a hierarchy. Only when the lower needs are largely met do the higher ones start to drive behaviour:

| Level | Need                        | Examples at work                                           |
| ----- | --------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| 5     | self-actualisation          | implementing your own ideas, challenging tasks, personal growth |
| 4     | esteem                      | recognition, praise, titles, promotion                     |
| 3     | social needs                | a good working atmosphere, belonging to a team             |
| 2     | safety needs                | a secure job, insurance, occupational safety               |
| 1     | physiological needs         | income for food, housing, clothing                         |

Maslow called levels 1 to 4 deficiency needs and self-actualisation a growth need. A common criticism is that the rigid order can hardly be proven empirically.

### Herzberg's two-factor theory

Frederick Herzberg distinguished two kinds of factors:

- Hygiene factors prevent dissatisfaction but do not create satisfaction: salary, working conditions, job security, company policy, the relationship with superiors.
- Motivators create real satisfaction and willingness to perform: achievement, recognition, the work itself, responsibility, opportunities for advancement and development.

So a good salary alone does not motivate in the long term, but its absence causes dissatisfaction.

### McGregor's Theory X and Theory Y

Douglas McGregor described two opposing views of human nature held by managers:

| Theory X                                               | Theory Y                                                  |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| People are lazy by nature and avoid work.              | Work is as natural for people as play and rest.           |
| They must be controlled, directed and threatened with punishment. | They are self-motivated and like to take on responsibility. |
| leads to an authoritarian leadership style             | leads to a cooperative leadership style                   |

The view of human nature often acts as a self-fulfilling prophecy: managers who do not trust their employees and control them strictly create exactly the passive behaviour they expect.

## Ways of motivating employees

- Financial incentives: fair pay, bonuses, profit sharing, benefits in kind (company phone, meal vouchers, public transport ticket)
- Non-financial incentives: praise and recognition, interesting tasks, responsibility, further training, career opportunities, flexible working hours, working from home, a good working atmosphere
- Job design:
  - job rotation: regularly switching between workplaces
  - job enlargement: adding tasks of the same kind
  - job enrichment: adding more demanding tasks with more decision-making freedom
  - semi-autonomous work groups that organise their work themselves
- Management by objectives: managers and employees agree on goals together, and the way to reach them is left to the employee.

## Leadership styles

Following the psychologist Kurt Lewin, three classic leadership styles are distinguished:

| Leadership style | Characteristics                                           | Advantages                            | Disadvantages                          |
| ---------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Authoritarian    | the manager decides alone, gives orders and controls strictly | quick decisions, clear conditions, good in crises | low motivation, employees' knowledge remains unused, dependence |
| Cooperative (democratic) | employees are involved in decisions, tasks are delegated | high motivation and identification, better decisions | decisions take longer |
| Laissez-faire    | employees work largely without guidelines, management stays in the background | a lot of freedom, creativity | lack of direction, conflicts, chaos with employees who are not independent |

### Situational leadership

No leadership style is always the best. According to the situational leadership model by Paul Hersey and Ken Blanchard, managers adapt their style to the maturity of their employees, i.e. to their ability and motivation for a particular task:

| Maturity of the employees                         | suitable style   | Behaviour of the manager                   |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ---------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| inexperienced and insecure                        | telling          | clear instructions, close control          |
| little experience, but motivated                  | selling          | explaining and justifying decisions        |
| experienced, but insecure or not very motivated   | participating    | deciding together, supporting              |
| experienced and motivated                         | delegating       | handing over responsibility                |

## Employee appraisal

The employee appraisal (annual review, development talk) is a regular, prepared conversation between manager and employee, usually once a year. It is used to

- look back on the past year and acknowledge achievements,
- give mutual feedback, including to the manager,
- agree on goals for the coming year,
- discuss further training and development opportunities,
- raise problems and wishes.

### Procedure

1. Preparation by both sides (often with a guideline)
2. Opening in a pleasant atmosphere, without time pressure or interruptions
3. Review of tasks, goals and cooperation
4. Outlook: agreeing on new goals (ideally SMART: specific, measurable, achievable, realistic, time-bound) and development measures
5. Conclusion with a written summary of the agreements

### Rules for feedback

- "I" messages instead of "you" messages ("I noticed that …")
- describe concrete, observable behaviour instead of judging the person
- give feedback promptly and constructively, with suggestions for improvement
- mention positive things too
- active listening: asking questions, summarising, letting the other person finish

Besides the annual review, there are other occasions such as critical conversations, conflict talks or conversations about absences. In agile teams in particular, regular short feedback is becoming more important than a single big annual review.
