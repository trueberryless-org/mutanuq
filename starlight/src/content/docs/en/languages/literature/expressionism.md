In the epoch of Expressionism, artists strongly criticise several topics, such as the big city, mass society, industrialisation, militarisation, manipulation and the First World War. This epoch is opposed to Realism and Naturalism, which only depicted reality. In addition, it turns away from the fin de siècle (escape into aesthetics) and from Goethe.

> _[...] Weg überhaupt mit den sogenannten Dichtern! Schluss! Unsere Kultur ist Gerümpel. [...] Der junge Dichter muss demolieren. [...]_

Role models of Expressionism are the Sturm und Drang, the Baroque, the art of children and of indigenous peoples as well as literary outsiders (Hölderlin, Kleist, Büchner and E.T.A. Hoffmann). Many authors and artists in general call themselves activists, "Sturm" artists and abstract artists.

## Key facts

-   beginning of the 20th century
-   shattering of language
-   poems that cannot be interpreted

## Expressionism and co.

The term Expressionism originally comes from the visual arts, as practised by Egon Schiele or Oskar Kokoschka. The pictures are meant to express how the artist experiences the world.

The term Dadaism emerged during the epoch of Expressionism and describes a destruction of language. It includes poems that cannot be interpreted and have no meaning.

## Artists

The foundation of this epoch is [Friedrich Nietzsche](/en/languages/literature/impressionism/#friedrich-nietzsche-1844-1900)'s [Also sprach Zarathustra](/en/languages/literature/impressionism/#also-sprach-zarathustra-1883) and Søren Kierkegaard's "freedom is central", who puts freedom at the centre of thinking. Unlike animals, humans can decide on a particular way of life. They can remain in a superficial life of pleasure, the existence Kierkegaard called "aesthetic". But they can also work in solidarity on building a humane society in an "ethical" existence.

The Expressionists also contrast the superficial old person with a coming new person who is fundamentally different from the old one and shows solidarity with fellow human beings. The "old" person is hard-hearted, harsh, evil, alien to fellow human beings and separated by boundary posts. In addition, the terms **society** and **community** are contrasted: the former defines a construct with rules in which you do not know your fellow human beings, the latter describes a more harmonious, smaller group of people who consciously know each other.

### Hermann Bahr (1863-1934)

Hermann Bahr was a critic with a desire for destruction. He saw Expressionism as an epoch of destruction and thought that art should help with it.

### August Stramm (1874-1915)

August Stramm was a German Expressionist poet and playwright. He died in the First World War. Known for his innovative language and experimental style, he left behind collections of poems such as "Du" and "Tropfblut". Stramm's radical approach to literature had a decisive influence on Expressionism. Some of his well-known works are ["Zwist" (1914)](#zwist-1914) and ["Patrouille" (1915)](#patrouille-1915).

### Franz Kafka (1883-1924)

Franz Kafka was an influential German-language writer of the 20th century. Known for his unique literary world, he created works such as ["Die Verwandlung" (The Metamorphosis, 1915)](#die-verwandlung-1915), in which the protagonist turns into a beetle, and ["Der Prozess" (The Trial, 1914)](#der-prozess-1914), which deals with the absurdities of an opaque judicial system. In his ["Brief an den Vater" (Letter to His Father, 1919)](#brief-an-den-vater-1919), Kafka reflects on his relationship with his authoritarian father.

Although he lived at the time of Expressionism, Kafka's works defy easy classification in this epoch. His works are characterised by existential themes, isolation and the breaking out of reality. In almost all of his works, an incident occurs that interrupts everyday life.

Kafka died in 1924 and left behind a literary legacy of great depth and complexity that continues to fascinate readers worldwide. Because of his unique writing style and content, Kafka even has his own adjective: "Kafkaesque" (_in the manner of Kafka's depictions; mysteriously uncanny, threatening_).

#### Kafka's life

In many works, Kafka builds his own name into the main character with similarities. For example, `Samsa` has the same vowel sequence and number of letters as `Kafka`. There are also similarities between Gregor's flat in ["Die Verwandlung"](#die-verwandlung-1915) and Kafka's own flat. This suggests that the hopelessness of Kafka's characters in the face of powers that exceed their own strength has personal roots. Some of Kafka's traumas can be read from the [letter to his father](#brief-an-den-vater-1919).

### Gottfried Benn (1886-1956)

Gottfried Benn strongly criticised the progress of militarisation. He said he saw no progress if the murderous rifle bullet became an even more murderous grenade. His best-known works include [Kleine Aster](#kleine-aster-1912) and [Schöne Jugend](#schöne-jugend-1912).

### Georg Heym (1887-1912)

Georg Heym was a German poet and writer of Expressionism. Born in Hirschberg, he studied law, art history and philosophy in Berlin. There he joined the Expressionist movement of artists and writers.

Heym's literary work is characterised by a dark, often apocalyptic atmosphere. Well-known works are the collection of poems "Der ewige Tag" (1911) and the poem ["Der Gott der Stadt" (1910)](#der-gott-der-stadt-1910); he long did not want to publish the latter, although little criticism was voiced. His writing style reflects the unrest and dissatisfaction of the time before the First World War.

The poet tragically died young, drowning in the river Havel at the age of only 24. Despite his short life, Georg Heym had a lasting influence on German Expressionist literature.

### Jakob van Hoddis (1887-1942)

Jakob van Hoddis was a German Expressionist poet. Born Hans Davidsohn in Berlin, he later changed his name to Jakob van Hoddis. His life was marked by mental instability, and he spent some time in psychiatric institutions.

Van Hoddis was a member of the Expressionist movement and part of the group of poets "Der Neue Club". His best-known work is the poem ["Weltende" (1911)](#weltende-1911), which is considered one of the most important Expressionist poems. This work reflects the unrest and uncertainty of the time before the First World War.

Jakob van Hoddis' literary career was short, and he was forgotten after the outbreak of the First World War. He became a victim of the National Socialist regime and was murdered in the Sobibor extermination camp in 1942.

Despite his short creative period, van Hoddis had a lasting influence on Expressionist poetry and is regarded as an important representative of this literary movement.

### Alfred Lichtenstein (1889-1914)

Alfred Lichtenstein was a German Expressionist poet. Born in Berlin, he became known for his sarcastic and socially critical poems. Lichtenstein was part of the Expressionist literary movement and belonged to the group of poets "Der Neue Club". His work reflects the unrest and tensions of the time before the First World War. He was killed in the war in 1914 at the age of 25. Lichtenstein's poems, including ["Abschied" (1914)](#abschied-1914) and "Die Dämmerung", show a unique style characterised by a dark world view and a pronounced linguistic artistry.

## Works

### Der Gott der Stadt (1910)

In this poem, [Georg Heym](#georg-heym-1887-1912) describes God (Baal) with dark colours, such as "schwarzen Winden" (black winds), "roter Bauch" (red belly), "schwarzen Türmen" (black towers) and "dunkler Abend" (dark evening). God is angry, sour, gloomy, red. In addition, the city is described with rather negative aspects: factories, exhaust fumes, fire, noise, industrialisation, mass society, flames, smouldering smoke.

<blockquote>

Auf einem Häuserblocke sitzt er breit.  
Die Winde lagern schwarz um seine Stirn.  
Er schaut voll Wut, wo fern in Einsamkeit  
Die letzten Häuser in das Land verirrn.

Vom Abend glänzt der rote Bauch dem Baal,  
die großen Städte knien um ihn her.  
Der Kirchenglocken ungeheure Zahl  
Wogt auf zu ihm aus schwarzer Türme Meer.

Wie Korybanten-Tanz dröhnt die Musik  
Der Millionen durch die Straßen laut.  
Der Schlote Rauch, die Wolken der Fabrik  
Ziehn auf zu ihm, wie Duft von Weihrauch blaut.

Das Wetter schwelt in seinen Augenbrauen.  
Der dunkle Abend wird in Nacht betäubt.  
Die Stürme flattern, die wie Geier schauen  
Von seinem Haupthaar, das im Zorne sträubt.

Er streckt ins Dunkel seine Fleischerfaust.  
Er schüttelt sie. Ein Meer von Feuer jagt  
Durch eine Straße. Und der Glutqualm braust  
Und frißt sie auf, bis spät der Morgen tagt.

</blockquote>

### Weltende (1911)

In the poem Weltende (End of the World), the storm describes destruction, as the waves become tsunamis, houses collapse and the world falls apart. The penultimate verse stands out in particular, since it is a comparatively small problem in contrast to the end of the world. [Jakob van Hoddis](#jakob-van-hoddis-1887-1942) wants to express that people do not care about anything they only read about in the newspaper.

<blockquote>

Dem Bürger fliegt vom spitzen Kopf der Hut,  
In allen Lüften hallt es wie Geschrei,  
Dachdecker stürzen ab und gehn entzwei  
Und an den Küsten – liest man – steigt die Flut.

Der Sturm ist da, die wilden Meere hupfen  
An Land, um dicke Dämme zu zerdrücken.  
Die meisten Menschen haben einen Schnupfen.  
Die Eisenbahnen fallen von den Brücken.

</blockquote>

### Schöne Jugend (1912)

The title may be misleading, since this poem is not about people but about **rats**.

<blockquote>

Der Mund eines Mädchens, das lange im Schilf gelegen hatte,  
sah so angeknabbert aus.  
Als man die Brust aufbrach, war die Speiseröhre so löcherig.  
Schließlich in einer Laube unter dem Zwerchfell  
fand man ein Nest von jungen Ratten.  
Ein kleines Schwesterchen lag tot.  
Die andern lebten von Leber und Niere,  
tranken das kalte Blut und hatten  
hier eine schöne Jugend verlebt.  
Und schön und schnell kam auch ihr Tod:  
Man warf sie allesamt ins Wasser.  
Ach, wie die kleinen Schnauzen quietschten!

</blockquote>

### Kleine Aster (1912)

In this poem, the aster symbolises a flower and _not_ a bird, so that, just like with [Schöne Jugend](#schöne-jugend-1912), the reader is deceived.

<blockquote>

Ein ersoffener Bierfahrer wurde auf den Tisch gestemmt.  
Irgendeiner hatte ihm eine dunkelhelllila Aster zwischen die Zähne geklemmt.  
Als ich von der Brust aus  
unter der Haut  
mit einem langen Messer  
Zunge und Gaumen herausschnitt,  
Muss ich sie angestoßen haben, denn sie glitt in das nebenliegende Gehirn.  
Ich packte sie ihm in die Brusthöhle zwischen die Holzwolle,  
als man zunähte.  
Trinke dich satt in deiner Vase!  
Ruhe sanft,  
Kleine Aster!

</blockquote>

### Der Prozess (1914)

A story in which Josef K. is arrested although he has done nothing wrong. Throughout the entire plot, no guilt on Josef K.'s part becomes apparent. For this reason, "Der Prozess" does not end with a trial either. Just as at the beginning of the novel two men appear in K.'s room and throw him out of his usual life, two men appear in the final chapter to end the "trial". Josef K. knows that it would be his duty to kill himself with the knife, but he does not do it. Instead, the two men take him to a quarry, where they stab him.

### Zwist (1914)

Not everything in the poem can be interpreted, but some stylistic devices can be identified, e.g. antitheses (Quälen küsst, Kosen schelten, ...). Another (possibly noteworthy) feature is the excessive use of verbs.

### Abschied (1914)

[Alfred Lichtenstein](#alfred-lichtenstein-1889-1914) wrote this linguistically excellent poem shortly before his death in the First World War.

<blockquote>

Vorm Sterben mache ich noch mein Gedicht.  
Still, Kameraden, stört mich nicht.

Wir ziehn zum Krieg. Der Tod ist unser Kitt.  
Oh; heulte mir doch die Geliebte nit. -

Was liegt an mir. Ich gehe gerne ein.  
Die Mutter weint. Man muß aus Eisen sein.

Die Sonne fällt zum Horizont hinab.  
Bald wirft man mich ins milde Massengrab.

Am Himmel brennt das brave Abendrot.  
Vielleicht bin ich in dreizehn Tagen tot.

</blockquote>

### Die Verwandlung (1915)

Kafka's stories and novels often begin with situations that are inexplicable and threatening for those affected. A completely realistic situation suddenly bursts the boundaries of reality. There are many parodies of Die Verwandlung, for example [Jan Böhmermann's satire](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8F2IXNErOas). "Die Verwandlung" begins with the following sentences:

<blockquote>

Als Gregor Samsa eines Morgens aus unruhigen Träumen erwachte, fand er sich in seinem Bett zu einem ungeheueren Ungeziefer verwandelt. Er lag auf seinem panzerartig harten Rücken und sah, wenn er den Kopf ein wenig hob, seinen gewölbten, braunen, von bogenförmigen Versteifungen geteilten Bauch, auf dessen Höhe sich die Bettdecke, zum gänzlichen Niedergleiten bereit, kaum noch erhalten konnte. Seine vielen, im Vergleich zu seinem sonstigen Umfang kläglich dünnen Beine flimmerten ihm hilflos vor den Augen. »Was ist mit mir geschehen?« dachte er. Es war kein Traum.

</blockquote>

The story is divided into three parts, the first of which deals with the first hours after the transformation. The second part describes the evening of the day of the transformation. The last part tells of long-term consequences that only happen months after the transformation.

#### Part 1

Gregor Samsa wakes up in the morning and realises that he is a beetle the size of a human. He thinks about his job as a travelling cloth salesman. He hates it, but it is necessary to support the family. Mother, father and sister Grete knock on the door because they want to ask anxiously how he is. It takes Gregor so long to get out of bed that in the meantime a chief clerk from Gregor's employer has turned up to ask about him. In addition, his professional performance is criticised. Gregor then wants to assure them that he will take the next train, but those present only hear animal sounds. A doctor and a locksmith are called to open the door. But Gregor opens the door with his jaws so that his family can help him. Father, mother and the chief clerk react in horror to the sight of him. Gregor is pushed back with a stick and injured on his left leg.

#### Part 2

In the evening, Gregor wakes up because of the smell of delicious food. His sister has put his favourite drink at the door, but he feels an aversion to the milk. He crawls under the sofa and finally feels comfortable. Early in the morning, Grete brings him a large selection of food, of which he only eats the half-rotten items with pleasure.

In the following days, the family worries about money. It is decided that Grete should look after the beetle, which is why she notices that Gregor likes to crawl up the walls. Then all the furniture except a picture of a lady is removed. The mother faints and Gregor wants to help her. The sister says that the beetle has broken out, and the father pelts him with apples.

#### Part 3

One of the apples remains stuck in his body for months, so that the spot becomes inflamed. Gregor is increasingly neglected and his room becomes a storage room. As an additional source of income, the family rents out rooms of the flat to three gentlemen, which is why Gregor's door remains closed during dinner. But when his sister plays the violin, Gregor opens the door and the gentlemen threaten to give notice. Grete says that they must get rid of **it** (Gregor). That night, Gregor dies from the exertion of crawling with the apple in his body. The new charwoman, the only one who does not detest Gregor, informs the family and disposes of him.

The family is happy: they give notice to the three gentlemen and the charwoman and hope that Grete will soon find a husband.

### Brief an den Vater (1919)

In the night of 22 to 23 September 1912, Kafka writes the story "Das Urteil" (The Judgement). The two acting characters are father and son. At the end of a long conversation, the father says to the son: _"Ich verurteile dich jetzt zum Tode des Ertrinkens!"_ (I now sentence you to death by drowning!) Without resistance, the son accepts the verdict and lets himself fall from the bridge into the river. At the age of 36, in 1919, Kafka writes the "Brief an den Vater" (Letter to His Father). It comprises more than 100 pages and was actually intended to be handed to his father. Kafka probably asked his mother to hand it over, but this never happened.

<blockquote>

Deine äußerst wirkungsvollen, wenigstens mir gegenüber niemals versagenden rednerischen Mittel bei der Erziehung waren: Schimpfen, Drohen, Ironie, böses Lachen und merkwürdigerweise - Selbstbeklagung. Das Schimpfen verstärktest Du mit Drohen [...]. Schrecklich war mir zum Beispiel dieses: ich zerreiße Dich wie einen Fisch, trotzdem ich ja wusste, dass dem nichts Schlimmeres nachfolgte (als kleines Kind wusste ich das allerdings nicht), aber es entsprach fast meinen Vorstellungen von Deiner Macht, dass Du auch das imstande gewesen wärest. Schrecklich war es auch, wenn Du schreiend um den Tisch herumliefst, um einen zu fassen, offenbar gar nicht fassen wolltest, aber doch so tatest und die Mutter einen schließlich scheinbar rettete. Wieder hatte man einmal, so schien es dem Kind, das Leben durch Deine Gnade behalten und trug es als Dein unverdientes Geschenk weiter. [...]

Deine Erziehungsmittel in den allerersten Jahren kann ich heute natürlich nicht unmittelbar beschreiben, aber ich kann sie mir etwa vorstellen durch Rückschluss aus den später erfahrenen [...]. Direkt erinnere ich mich nur an einen Vorfall aus den ersten Jahren. Du erinnerst Dich vielleicht auch daran. Ich winselte einmal in der Nacht immerfort um Wasser, gewiss nicht aus Durst, sondern wahrscheinlich teils um zu ärgern, teils um mich zu unterhalten. Nachdem einige starke Drohungen nicht geholfen hatten, nahmst Du mich aus dem Bett, trugst mich auf die Pawlatsche[^2] und ließest mich dort allein vor der geschlossenen Tür ein Weilchen im Hemd stehn. Ich will nicht sagen, dass das unrichtig war, vielleicht war damals die Nachtruhe auf andere Weise wirklich nicht zu verschaffen, ich will aber damit Deine Erziehungsmittel und ihre Wirkung auf mich charakterisieren. Ich war damals nachher wohl schon folgsam, aber ich hatte einen inneren Schaden davon. [...] Noch nach Jahren litt ich unter der quälenden Vorstellung, dass der riesige Mann, mein Vater, die letzte Instanz, fast ohne Grund kommen und mich in der Nacht aus dem Bett auf die Pawlatsche tragen konnte und dass ich also ein solches Nichts für ihn war.

</blockquote>

## Genres

### Poetry

The poems should not "move" (rühren) but "stir up" (aufrühren) and create a belief in the "new" person. The confrontation with the threatening, repulsive and grotesque forms the second facet of poetry, as in the poems ["Weltende" (1911)](#weltende-1911) by [Jakob van Hoddis](#jakob-van-hoddis-1887-1942) and ["Schöne Jugend" (1912)](#schöne-jugend-1912) by [Gottfried Benn](#gottfried-benn-1886-1956).

The form of the poems varies greatly: some show metaphor-rich and emotional language, while others shatter sentence structure and negate logic.

In 1919, the most representative collection of Expressionist poems appeared. It is called "Menschheitsdämmerung" (Twilight of Humanity). Its four sections show the themes of Expressionist poetry: "Sturz und Schrei" (fall and scream), "Erweckung des Herzens" (awakening of the heart), "Aufruf und Empörung" (call and outrage) and "Liebe den Menschen" (love to people).

### Drama

In drama, there is a struggle between the "old" and the "new" person, for example the generational conflict and the struggle of sons against fathers. Parricide is the symbol of the downfall of authorities. There is also a conflict between the sexes. Here, "Frühlings Erwachen" (Spring Awakening) by Frank Wedekind is regarded as a model because of its criticism of sexual morality and school.

### Prose

In Expressionism, only a few works are devoted to prose. The shattering of language, enthusiasm and pathos can be applied much better in poetry and drama. However, one prose writer from this time is very famous: Franz Kafka. However, he cannot be clearly assigned to Expressionism. He writes about the alienation of people in a world they do not understand. Some of his most important works are "Die Verwandlung", "Der Prozess" and "Eine alltägliche Verwirrung". He also writes a "Brief an den Vater", which comprises more than 100 pages and describes the character of Franz Kafka.

## Magazines

In this epoch, many literary magazines were written, such as "Die Aktion" or "Der Sturm".

## Dadaism

Dadaism was born when Hugo Ball, Huelsenbeck, Hans Arp and Tristan Tzara met in Zurich in 1916 and discovered the word "Dada" for their art in a dictionary. This "anti-art" was directed against everything that had come before. The "Dadaist Manifesto" of 1918 states that language should express the "explosion" of the time.

To demonstrate this destruction of language, the four founders used various methods, such as "unreason" (no logic in texts), sound poems (no words, but individual sounds), letter poems (focus on one word) and chance. Particularly striking poems are, firstly, the **bruitist** poem, also called noise poem, which is supplemented by sounds and acting, and secondly, the **simultaneous** poem, in which several poems are read at the same time.

### The Vienna Group

At the end of the 1950s, a group of poets appeared in Austrian literature who initially met with massive public resistance. Friedrich Achleitner, H. C. Artmann, Konrad Bayer, Gerhard Rühm, Oswald Wiener as well as Ernst Jandl and Friederike Mayröcker, who were close to the group, provoked. Words and sentences are broken apart and swapped, and the grammatical, orthographic and syntactic norms of language are destroyed. Absolutely "unpoetic" vocabulary from everyday up to vulgar language finds its way into poetry; they write radically in lower case so as not to favour nouns (which are capitalised in German). The inclusion of acoustic and graphic elements of language in poetry is just as astonishing as the discovery of dialect for poetry, which can be seen, among other things, in H. C. Artmann's work "med ana schwoazzn dintn", which later became a poetry bestseller.
