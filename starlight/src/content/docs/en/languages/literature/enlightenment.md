In the **Enlightenment**, reason takes the place of tradition, superstition and church authority. People should think for themselves, overcome prejudices and actively shape their world. Literature becomes a means of education: it should teach and entertain at the same time.

## Key facts

- c. 1720 – 1800
- reason, tolerance, belief in progress
- literature should "instruct and delight" (_prodesse et delectare_)
- rise of the middle classes
- important genres: fable, didactic poem, bourgeois tragedy, drama of ideas

## Historical background

Scientific discoveries, for example by Isaac Newton, showed that the world can be explained through observation and reason. The **middle classes** became economically stronger through trade but remained politically dependent on the nobility. In Austria, Maria Theresa and Joseph II carried out reforms in the spirit of enlightened absolutism: in 1774, compulsory schooling was introduced, and in 1781 Joseph II issued the Patent of Toleration, which allowed Protestants and Orthodox Christians to practise their religion. The epoch ends with the French Revolution (1789) and its demands for liberty, equality and fraternity.

## Philosophical foundations

In 1784, **Immanuel Kant** (1724 – 1804) answered the question "What is Enlightenment?" with a famous definition:

> Aufklärung ist der Ausgang des Menschen aus seiner selbstverschuldeten Unmündigkeit. Unmündigkeit ist das Unvermögen, sich seines Verstandes ohne Leitung eines anderen zu bedienen. […] _Sapere aude!_ Habe Mut, dich deines eigenen Verstandes zu bedienen!

(Enlightenment is man's emergence from his self-incurred immaturity. Immaturity is the inability to use one's own understanding without the guidance of another. […] _Sapere aude!_ Have the courage to use your own understanding!)

Other important ideas of the time are:

- rationalism (René Descartes: "I think, therefore I am") and empiricism (John Locke: knowledge comes from experience)
- the separation of powers (Montesquieu) as the basis of modern democracies
- tolerance towards other religions and opinions
- the belief that people can be educated and improved

## Authors and works

### Gotthold Ephraim Lessing (1729 – 1781)

Lessing is the most important writer of the German Enlightenment.

- **Fables:** short stories in which animals embody human characteristics and which end with a lesson (moral), such as "Der Rabe und der Fuchs" (The Raven and the Fox).
- "Miß Sara Sampson" (1755) and "Emilia Galotti" (1772) are bourgeois tragedies: for the first time, the tragic heroes are members of the middle class and not only nobles. In "Emilia Galotti", the bourgeois Odoardo kills his daughter to protect her from an arbitrary ruling prince. This is a clear criticism of the nobility.
- "Minna von Barnhelm" (1767) is a comedy about honour and love after the Seven Years' War.
- "Nathan der Weise" (Nathan the Wise, 1779) is a drama of ideas about religious tolerance. It is set in Jerusalem at the time of the Crusades. At its centre is the Ring Parable: a father bequeaths a ring that makes its wearer "pleasing to God and people". Because he loves his three sons equally, he has two identical copies made. None of the sons can prove that he owns the real ring. The judge advises each of them to prove himself worthy through good deeds. The three rings stand for Judaism, Christianity and Islam: no religion can prove that it is the only true one. What matters is how people act.

### Other representatives

- **Christian Fürchtegott Gellert** (1715 – 1769): fables and stories, one of the most widely read authors of his time
- **Johann Christoph Gottsched** (1700 – 1766): rules of poetry in "Versuch einer critischen Dichtkunst"; called for a theatre based on the French model and adherence to the three Aristotelian unities
- **Christoph Martin Wieland** (1733 – 1813): novels such as "Geschichte des Agathon", translator of Shakespeare
- **Georg Christoph Lichtenberg** (1742 – 1799): physicist and master of the aphorism

## Sentimentality

Parallel to the Enlightenment, **Empfindsamkeit** (sentimentality) developed. Besides reason, it emphasised feeling, friendship and nature. With his epic "Der Messias" and his odes, Friedrich Gottlieb Klopstock (1724 – 1803) became its most important representative and influenced the following [Sturm und Drang](/en/languages/literature/storm_and_stress/).

## Genres

- **Epic:** fable, parable, novel, aphorism
- **Poetry:** didactic poem, ode
- **Drama:** bourgeois tragedy, drama of ideas, comedy
