Between the **Congress of Vienna** (1815) and the Revolution of 1848, two opposing movements developed: faced with censorship and oppression, the Biedermeier withdrew into private life, while the Vormärz ("pre-March") fought for political freedom with literature. Austria played a special role at this time, because Vienna was the centre of the Metternich system.

## Key facts

- c. 1815 – 1848
- Restoration under Chancellor Metternich
- censorship and informers
- Biedermeier: retreat into private life, order, modesty
- Vormärz / Young Germany: political, critical literature
- Viennese popular theatre

## Historical background

After the Napoleonic Wars, the Austrian Chancellor **Klemens von Metternich** wanted to secure the old order. The Carlsbad Decrees (1819) introduced censorship and surveillance, banned student fraternities and put universities under control. Disappointed, the middle classes withdrew into the family and the cosy home. At the same time, the beginnings of industrialisation worsened social hardship, as in the weavers' uprising in Silesia (1844). In March 1848, revolution broke out in Vienna, Berlin and other cities. Hence the name "Vormärz" for the period before it. Metternich had to flee.

The name "Biedermeier" goes back to the satirical figure of the bland village schoolteacher Gottlieb Biedermaier, who appeared in a magazine.

## Biedermeier

### Features

- retreat into private life, family, home and nature
- appreciation of the small and everyday, order, moderation and duty
- resignation, melancholy and renunciation
- piety and a conservative attitude
- precise, loving descriptions of nature

### Franz Grillparzer (1791 – 1872)

Grillparzer is the most important Austrian playwright of the 19th century. His dramas often deal with the inner conflict of people and the tension between acting and renouncing. Well-known works are "**König Ottokars Glück und Ende**" (King Ottokar's Rise and Fall, 1825), which depicts the rise of the Habsburgs and was initially held back by the censors, "Des Meeres und der Liebe Wellen" (The Waves of Sea and Love, 1831) and the story "Der arme Spielmann" (The Poor Fiddler, 1848), the story of a poor Viennese street musician.

### Adalbert Stifter (1805 – 1868)

Stifter, who came from Bohemia, lived in Vienna and Linz. In the preface to his collection "**Bunte Steine**" (Colourful Stones, 1853), he formulated the "gentle law": what is great is not the mighty and loud, but the steady and inconspicuous, "the blowing of the air, the rippling of the water, the growing of the grain". His stories, such as "Bergkristall" (Rock Crystal), describe nature with great precision. His novel "Der Nachsommer" (Indian Summer, 1857) is a Bildungsroman.

### Annette von Droste-Hülshoff (1797 – 1848)

The Westphalian noblewoman is considered one of the most important German women poets. Her novella "**Die Judenbuche**" (The Jew's Beech, 1842) tells of an unpunished murder in a village and of guilt and conscience. Her ballads and nature poems, such as "Der Knabe im Moor" (The Boy on the Moor), are also well known.

### Eduard Mörike (1804 – 1875)

The Swabian pastor wrote atmospheric poems such as "Er ist's" ("Frühling lässt sein blaues Band …", Spring lets its blue ribbon …) and the novella "Mozart auf der Reise nach Prag" (Mozart's Journey to Prague, 1855).

## Viennese popular theatre

In Vienna, a popular theatre emerged that combined entertainment with hidden criticism.

- Ferdinand Raimund (1790 – 1836) wrote magic plays in which spirits and fairies intervene in the human world, such as "Der Alpenkönig und der Menschenfeind" (The King of the Alps and the Misanthrope) and "Der Verschwender" (The Spendthrift).
- Johann Nestroy (1801 – 1862) was an actor and the author of numerous farces. With wit, word play and improvised couplets, he circumvented censorship and criticised the middle classes and the authorities. Well-known works are "Der Talisman" (1840), "Einen Jux will er sich machen" (He Wants to Have a Lark, 1842) and "Der Zerrissene" (The Torn Man, 1844).

## Vormärz and Young Germany

### Features

- **political literature**: criticism of princes, censorship and social injustice
- demands for freedom of the press, a constitution and national unity
- **journalistic forms**: travel sketches, pamphlets, feuilletons, political poetry
- irony and satire
- In 1835, the writings of "Young Germany" were banned by the German Federal Assembly.

### Heinrich Heine (1797 – 1856)

Heine became famous with the "**Buch der Lieder**" (Book of Songs, 1827), which often breaks Romantic motifs ironically. From 1831, he lived in exile in Paris. In "Deutschland. Ein Wintermärchen" (Germany. A Winter's Tale, 1844), he satirically describes a journey through backward Germany. The poem "Die schlesischen Weber" (The Silesian Weavers, 1844) takes up the weavers' uprising and became one of the best-known political poems. "Die Lorelei" ("Ich weiß nicht, was soll es bedeuten …") is also by Heine.

### Georg Büchner (1813 – 1837)

Together with Friedrich Ludwig Weidig, Büchner wrote the pamphlet "**Der Hessische Landbote**" (The Hessian Courier, 1834) with the call "Friede den Hütten! Krieg den Palästen!" (Peace to the huts! War on the palaces!) and then had to flee. He died in Zurich at the age of only 23. His works were far ahead of their time:

- "Dantons Tod" (Danton's Death, 1835): drama about the French Revolution
- "Woyzeck" (fragment, written 1836/37): the simple soldier Woyzeck is humiliated by his captain and abused for experiments by a doctor. When his lover Marie cheats on him, he stabs her. The open drama shows a person from the lower class as a tragic hero and anticipates Naturalism.
- "Lenz" (written in 1835, published in 1839): story about the mental illness of the poet Jakob Michael Reinhold Lenz

### Other representatives

- Georg Herwegh (1817 – 1875) and Ferdinand Freiligrath (1810 – 1876): political poetry
- **Hoffmann von Fallersleben** (1798 – 1874): "Das Lied der Deutschen" (The Song of the Germans, 1841), whose third stanza is today the German national anthem

## Genres

- **Biedermeier:** novella, story, mood poetry, ballad, folk play and farce
- **Vormärz:** political poetry, travel writing, pamphlets, drama
