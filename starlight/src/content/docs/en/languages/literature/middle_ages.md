The literature of the Middle Ages covers a very long period from about 750 to 1500. For a long time, it was mainly passed on orally and only gradually written down in monasteries and at princely courts – first in Latin, later also in the vernacular. Most works were not read but **recited** or sung.

## Key facts

- c. 750 – 1500
- Language stages: **Old High German** (c. 750 – 1050), **Middle High German** (c. 1050 – 1350), **Early New High German** (from c. 1350)
- Centres: monasteries and princely courts
- religious and courtly literature

## Historical background

Medieval society was divided into **estates**: clergy, nobility and peasants. The **feudal system** bound vassals to their lord through loyalty. The Church shaped the world view, and almost only clerics could read and write. In the High Middle Ages, a distinct **chivalric, courtly culture** developed at the courts, with ideals such as loyalty, moderation (_mâze_), honour and courtly love (_minne_). The Crusades brought contact with other cultures.

## Old High German literature

Only a few texts have survived from the Old High German period, most of them religious. The best-known secular works are:

- **Merseburg Charms** (written down in the 10th century, but older): two pre-Christian magic spells, for example to heal an injured horse.
- **Hildebrandslied** (written down around 830): the only surviving Germanic heroic lay in German. The hero Hildebrand meets his son Hadubrand after 30 years in exile; the son does not recognise him and forces him to fight. The ending has not survived. The lay is written in **alliterative verse** (Stabreim).

## Courtly literature of the High Middle Ages

Around 1200, Middle High German literature reached its peak, known as the **Staufer classical period**.

### Minnesang

**Minnesang** is courtly love poetry. A knight (the lyrical I) worships a usually married, unattainable noble lady (_frouwe_). This **high minne** is a service that educates and refines the man but remains unfulfilled. **Low minne**, on the other hand, celebrates fulfilled love for a girl who is not of noble birth.

**Walther von der Vogelweide** (c. 1170 – c. 1230) is the best-known Minnesinger. He travelled from court to court as a wandering singer, including the Babenberg court in Vienna. Besides love songs such as "Under der linden", he wrote **Sangsprüche** (political and moral songs) in which he commented on political issues, for example in the "Reichston" ("Ich saz ûf eime steine").

**Under der linden** (beginning) – Walther von der Vogelweide

### Courtly romance (Arthurian romance)

Courtly romances tell in verse of knights at the court of the legendary **King Arthur**. The hero goes through a **double cycle**: he gains honour, loses it through misconduct and has to prove himself in a second series of adventures (_âventiure_) until he becomes an exemplary knight.

- **Hartmann von Aue:** "Erec" and "Iwein" – the first German Arthurian romances
- **Wolfram von Eschenbach:** "Parzival" – a naive young man becomes king of the Grail
- **Gottfried von Straßburg:** "Tristan" – the tragic love between Tristan and Isolde

### Heroic epic

The **Nibelungenlied** (c. 1200) is the most important German heroic epic. It was probably written down in the Danube region between Passau and Vienna; its author is unknown. It tells of Siegfried, who marries Kriemhild and is murdered by Hagen, and of Kriemhild's revenge, which leads to the downfall of the Burgundians at the court of Etzel, King of the Huns. The epic is written in four-line stanzas (the **Nibelungen stanza**).

## Late Middle Ages

In the Late Middle Ages, courtly culture lost importance, and **towns** and the middle classes gained influence. The **Meistersang** of craftsmen in the towns (best-known representative: Hans Sachs), religious plays and carnival plays emerged.

"**Der Ackermann aus Böhmen**" (The Ploughman from Bohemia, c. 1400) by Johannes von Tepl is considered an important work at the transition to the modern age: a dispute between a widower and Death about the meaning of life.

Johannes Gutenberg's invention of **printing** with movable type (c. 1450) fundamentally changed literature: books could now be produced in large numbers.

## Genres

- **Poetry:** Minnesang, Sangspruch poetry, religious songs
- **Epic:** heroic epic, courtly romance, legends – almost always in **verse**
- **Drama:** religious plays (Easter and Passion plays), carnival plays
