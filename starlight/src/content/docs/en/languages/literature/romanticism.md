**Romanticism** turns away from the reason of the [Enlightenment](/en/languages/literature/enlightenment/) and the moderation of [Classicism](/en/languages/literature/weimar_classicism/). It seeks the mysterious, the unconscious and the miraculous – in dreams, in the night, in nature, in the Middle Ages and in distant lands. Its central feeling is **longing** (Sehnsucht) for something unattainable and infinite.

## Key facts

- c. 1795 – 1835
- Early Romanticism (Jena), High Romanticism (Heidelberg), Late Romanticism (Berlin)
- longing, wanderlust, escapism
- symbol: the **blue flower**
- night, dreams, nature, the Middle Ages
- interest in folk songs and fairy tales

## Historical background

The hopes of the French Revolution had been disappointed. **Napoleon** conquered large parts of Europe, and in 1806 the Holy Roman Empire collapsed. The Wars of Liberation (1813 – 1815) awakened a **national consciousness**. After the **Congress of Vienna** (1814/15), Prince Metternich restored the old order. At the same time, industrialisation began, and many people felt that the world was becoming increasingly sober and disenchanted.

## Features

- **Longing and wanderlust:** The goal of longing remains unattainable. Motifs include the wanderer, the post horn, the open window and the distance.
- **The blue flower:** a symbol of Romantic longing for the infinite, from Novalis' novel "Heinrich von Ofterdingen".
- **Night and dreams:** Night stands for the unconscious, the mysterious and the divine. The uncanny, doppelgängers and madness also become themes.
- **Nature:** Nature is animated and reflects people's moods.
- **Romanticising the world:** Novalis wanted to give "the common a higher meaning, the ordinary a mysterious appearance".
- **Romantic irony:** The author deliberately breaks the illusion of the work.
- **Universal poetry:** Friedrich Schlegel called for a "progressive universal poetry" that unites all genres, art and life.
- **Return to the Middle Ages** and folk culture: collections of folk songs and fairy tales.

## Authors and works

### Novalis (1772 – 1801)

Novalis (real name Friedrich von Hardenberg) is the most important poet of Early Romanticism. After the death of his young fiancée Sophie von Kühn, he wrote the "**Hymnen an die Nacht**" (Hymns to the Night, 1800). His unfinished novel "**Heinrich von Ofterdingen**" (1802) established the symbol of the blue flower.

### Joseph von Eichendorff (1788 – 1857)

Eichendorff's poems are among the best known in German poetry. Many were set to music and became folk songs. In the novella "**Aus dem Leben eines Taugenichts**" (Memoirs of a Good-for-Nothing, 1826), a carefree miller's son sets out into the world with his violin and travels as far as Italy.

<blockquote>

**Mondnacht** (Moonlit Night) – Joseph von Eichendorff

Es war, als hätt' der Himmel  
Die Erde still geküsst,  
Dass sie im Blütenschimmer  
Von ihm nur träumen müsst'.

Die Luft ging durch die Felder,  
Die Ähren wogten sacht,  
Es rauschten leis die Wälder,  
So sternklar war die Nacht.

Und meine Seele spannte  
Weit ihre Flügel aus,  
Flog durch die stillen Lande,  
Als flöge sie nach Haus.

</blockquote>

The poem unites heaven and earth in an image of longing. The **subjunctive** ("als hätt'" – as if, "als flöge" – as if it flew) shows that the union is only wished for and not real.

### E. T. A. Hoffmann (1776 – 1822)

E. T. A. Hoffmann was a lawyer, composer, illustrator and writer. He is the master of **Gothic Romanticism** and the uncanny. In "**Der Sandmann**" (The Sandman, 1816), the student Nathanael falls in love with the automaton doll Olimpia, whom he takes for a human being, and descends into madness. Other works include "Der goldne Topf" (The Golden Pot), "Das Fräulein von Scuderi" (one of the first German crime stories) and "Nussknacker und Mausekönig" (The Nutcracker and the Mouse King).

### Brothers Grimm

**Jacob** (1785 – 1863) and **Wilhelm Grimm** (1786 – 1859) collected fairy tales and published them as "**Kinder- und Hausmärchen**" (Children's and Household Tales, from 1812), including "Hansel and Gretel", "Little Red Riding Hood" and "Snow White". However, the tales did not come directly "from the people": they were mostly told to the brothers by educated middle-class women and heavily edited by Wilhelm Grimm. The brothers also founded German studies (Germanistik) and started the "Deutsches Wörterbuch" (German Dictionary).

### Other representatives

- **Ludwig Tieck** (1773 – 1853): literary fairy tale "Der blonde Eckbert"
- **Clemens Brentano** (1778 – 1842) and **Achim von Arnim** (1781 – 1831): folk song collection "Des Knaben Wunderhorn" (The Boy's Magic Horn, 1805 – 1808)
- **Friedrich Schlegel** (1772 – 1829) and **August Wilhelm Schlegel** (1767 – 1845): theorists of Early Romanticism; A. W. Schlegel translated Shakespeare
- **Adelbert von Chamisso** (1781 – 1838): "Peter Schlemihls wundersame Geschichte" about a man who sells his shadow
- **Heinrich von Kleist** (1777 – 1811): stands between Classicism and Romanticism; novella "Michael Kohlhaas", comedy "Der zerbrochne Krug" (The Broken Jug)

## Genres

- **Poetry:** mood and nature poems, folk songs
- **Epic:** literary fairy tale, novella, fragmentary novels
- **Drama:** less important, mainly closet dramas and Kleist's works
