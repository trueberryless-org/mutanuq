**Sturm und Drang** (Storm and Stress) was a short but intense movement of young authors who rebelled against the one-sided emphasis on reason of the [Enlightenment](/en/languages/literature/enlightenment/) and against the narrowness of the class-based society. In place of reason, they put **feeling**, passion and the creative **genius**. The epoch takes its name from a drama by Friedrich Maximilian Klinger (1776).

## Key facts

- c. 1765 – 1785
- also called the "age of genius"
- feeling instead of reason ("heart" instead of "head")
- cult of genius and freedom
- nature as a mirror of the soul
- rebellion against the nobility, tyranny and rigid rules
- the authors were mostly young men around 20

## Features

- **Genius:** The artist creates from within like a god and needs no rules. The model is **Shakespeare**, who did not follow the Aristotelian unities.
- **Feeling and passion:** One's own heart is the measure of truth.
- **Nature:** Nature is alive, divine and a mirror of one's own mood. The turn to nature goes back to **Jean-Jacques Rousseau** ("back to nature").
- **Criticism of the class-based society:** The heroes are outsiders and rebels who fail because of social barriers.
- **Language:** expressive, full of exclamations, ellipses and dashes, often colloquial; free rhythms instead of strict verse forms.

## Authors and works

### Johann Wolfgang von Goethe (1749 – 1832)

The young Goethe became the central figure of Sturm und Drang.

- **"Die Leiden des jungen Werthers"** (The Sorrows of Young Werther, 1774) is an **epistolary novel**: the sensitive Werther falls in love with Lotte, who is engaged to Albert. His unrequited love and his failure in aristocratic society drive him to despair until he shoots himself. The novel was a Europe-wide success and triggered a real "Werther fashion"; some readers even imitated his suicide (today this is called the **Werther effect**).
- **"Prometheus"** (1774) is a hymn in free rhythms in which the Titan Prometheus rebels against Zeus, the father of the gods. It expresses the self-confidence of the genius.
- **"Götz von Berlichingen"** (1773) is a drama about an upright knight fighting against arbitrary rule, deliberately written against the classical rules.
- Poems such as "Willkommen und Abschied" (Welcome and Farewell) and "Mailied" (May Song) belong to so-called **experiential poetry** (Erlebnislyrik), in which personal feelings are expressed directly.

<blockquote>

**Prometheus** (ending) – Johann Wolfgang von Goethe

Hier sitz' ich, forme Menschen  
Nach meinem Bilde,  
Ein Geschlecht, das mir gleich sei,  
Zu leiden, zu weinen,  
Zu genießen und zu freuen sich,  
Und dein nicht zu achten,  
Wie ich!

</blockquote>

### Friedrich Schiller (1759 – 1805)

- **"Die Räuber"** (The Robbers, 1781, premiered in Mannheim in 1782): the unequal brothers Karl and Franz Moor fight for their father's love and the inheritance. The scheming Franz pushes Karl aside, who then becomes the leader of a band of robbers to fight against the injustice of the world. The premiere was a scandal – the audience reacted with tumult.
- **"Kabale und Liebe"** (Intrigue and Love, 1784) is a **bourgeois tragedy**: the love between the nobleman Ferdinand and the middle-class girl Luise fails because of class barriers and an intrigue (Kabale) at court. In the end, both die.

Because of "The Robbers", Schiller came into conflict with his sovereign, the Duke of Württemberg, who forbade him to write. Schiller fled Stuttgart in 1782.

### Other representatives

- **Johann Gottfried Herder** (1744 – 1803): theorist of the movement, collected folk songs and emphasised the importance of folk poetry
- **Jakob Michael Reinhold Lenz** (1751 – 1792): dramas "Der Hofmeister" (The Tutor) and "Die Soldaten" (The Soldiers)
- **Gottfried August Bürger** (1747 – 1794): ballad "Lenore"
- **Friedrich Maximilian Klinger** (1752 – 1831): drama "Sturm und Drang" (1776), which gave the epoch its name

## Genres

- **Poetry:** experiential poetry, hymns in free rhythms, ballads
- **Epic:** epistolary novel
- **Drama:** open dramas modelled on Shakespeare, bourgeois tragedy
