In the epoch of Impressionism, artists do not focus on a specific world view but deal with a variety of literary styles. These are influenced by Realism, Naturalism, "workers' poetry" and Arthur Schnitzler. Geographically, Vienna, Berlin and Munich are the major centres. Impressionism not only distances itself from politics and society, but also refuses to depict reality realistically or in a critical, naturalistic way. In the context of Impressionism, this means that artists consciously decided not to represent reality in the traditional way that had been common in earlier art. Instead of sharp contours, detailed realism and meticulous precision, the Impressionists preferred loose brushwork, bright colours and the rendering of fleeting impressions.

## Key facts

-   end of the 19th century, beginning of the 20th century
-   variety of world views and literary styles
-   centres: Berlin, Vienna, Munich

## Impressionism and co.

Originally, the term **Impressionism** described a style of painting. In the context of the epoch, Impressionism describes works that convey subjective impressions, feelings, moods, light, shadow etc., but have no concrete plot.

The term **Symbolism**, on the other hand, describes poetry that has no "purpose" but a magical language. Symbolism is the art of "hinting".

Other terms related to Impressionism are **fin de siècle** and **Viennese Modernism**. The former literally means "end of the century" in French and expresses that art should stand on its own. In Viennese Modernism, as the name suggests, Vienna is at the centre of literature, architecture, painting etc.

## Artists

### Friedrich Nietzsche (1844-1900)

The philosopher Friedrich Nietzsche criticises the "average person" who merely vegetates. To become an "Übermensch" (superman), art is needed. This art should be free of moral and political constraints.

> Die Kunst und nichts als die Kunst!  
> _Sie ist die große Ermöglicherin des Lebens, die große Verführerin zum Leben, das große Stimulans des Lebens!_

In [Also sprach Zarathustra](#also-sprach-zarathustra-1883), this criticism is dealt with specifically.

### Charles Baudelaire (1821-1867)

According to the poet Charles Baudelaire, culture is the "victory" over nature. In addition, language creates its own reality. Charles created poetry freed from all purposes (_pure poetry_). He found his inspiration in alcohol and drug intoxication.

### Ernst Mach (1838-1916)

Ernst Mach believed that reality and personality are a succession of "colours, sounds, warmths, pressures and spaces". He was one of the central philosophers of Impressionism.

### Sigmund Freud (1856-1939)

Sigmund Freud was a psychoanalyst who declared the **three-part human personality**. It divides every person's way of thinking and acting into three areas: **ego**, **superego** and **id**. While the latter works completely unconsciously and drives self-realisation, sexuality and aggression, the superego is the counterpart to this id. The superego contains moral conscience, authorities, values and norms. The ego finds the middle ground between the superego and the id and lies largely in conscious thinking. Freud saw culture, for example, as the superego. Conflicts with these three layers shape us.

![Three-part human personality](/images/languages/dreischichtige_menschliche_persoenlichkeit.png)

Freud assumed that a large part of our decisions are made unconsciously. In addition, Freud held [Schnitzler](#arthur-schnitzler-1862-1931) in high esteem and called him his "doppelgänger".

### Arthur Schnitzler (1862-1931)

Arthur Schnitzler was the first author in German-language literature to use an interior monologue, in his work [Leutnant Gustl (1900)](#leutnant-gustl-1900).

### Hugo von Hofmannsthal (1874-1929)

Hugo von Hofmannsthal's poems are among the most famous of Viennese Modernism, but also among the most difficult to interpret. One of the most difficult is [Terzinen über Vergänglichkeit (1894)](#terzinen-über-vergänglichkeit-1894), alongside many others, such as the [Chandos-Brief (1902)](#chandos-brief-1902), Jedermann (1911) and Der Rosenkavalier (1911).

### Rainer Maria Rilke (1875-1926)

Rilke's first great success was the lyrical narrative "Die Weise von Liebe und Tod des Cornets Christoph Rilke" (1899). Rilke also wrote many "New Poems" (Neue Gedichte), which do not focus on feelings or sensations but on precise observations of people, animals and everyday objects in and around Paris. That is why they are also called "thing poems" (Dinggedichte). These include, for example, poems such as [Der Panther (1903)](#der-panther-1903) and [Die Erblindende (1906)](#die-erblindende-1906). With great sensitivity, Rilke filters out what is essential for these "things" in order to fathom their "true nature".

### Karl Kraus (1874-1936)

Karl Kraus was an Austrian writer, critic and satirist who lived from 1874 to 1936. He was a sharp-tongued critic of Viennese Modernism, the press and society. His texts were characterised by his rejection of empty phrases and of manipulation through language. Kraus is considered one of the most important German-language authors of the 20th century.

## Works

### Also sprach Zarathustra (1883)

With this and a few other works, [Friedrich Nietzsche](#friedrich-nietzsche-1844-1900) lays the philosophical foundation for the criticism of the time in [Expressionism](/en/languages/literature/expressionism/). In this work, Nietzsche contrasts the "last man", who only cares about his petty profit and actually denies and destroys life, with the "Übermensch". The latter despises the prevailing morality, thinking and society and grows beyond his personal pettiness.

> _„Ich lehre euch den Übermenschen.“_

### Die Fackel (1899-1936)

Die Fackel (The Torch) is a magazine that was published by Karl Kraus in Vienna from 1899 to 1936. The magazine had a sharp, polemical tone and addressed a wide range of social, cultural and political issues. Die Fackel became a central organ of criticism of Austrian society and especially of the press. [Kraus](#karl-kraus-1874-1936)' style was characterised by pointed analyses, paradoxical formulations and a deep mistrust of the powers of his time.

In terms of content, Die Fackel dealt with various topics, including political corruption and social injustice, but also cultural developments and literary works. Kraus did not mince his words and sharply attacked both individuals and institutions when he identified grievances and hypocrisy.

Die Fackel can be seen as a critical voice in a time of upheaval and political change. Kraus' writing style and his analysis of society have had a lasting influence on German-language literature and cultural history.

### Terzinen über Vergänglichkeit (1894)

Overall, this work consists of three parts. The first of these three is written in iambic pentameter with a chain rhyme scheme (`ABA CBC`). In this order, [Hofmannsthal](#hugo-von-hofmannsthal-1874-1929) describes memory, shock, insight and alienation in the five stanzas (the last one an unrhymed single line, a so-called orphan).

<blockquote>

Noch spür ich ihren Atem auf den Wangen:  
Wie kann das sein, daß diese nahen Tage  
Fort sind, für immer fort, und ganz vergangen?

Dies ist ein Ding, das keiner voll aussinnt,  
Und viel zu grauenvoll, als daß man klage:  
Daß alles gleitet und vorüberrinnt.

Und daß mein eignes Ich, durch nichts gehemmt,  
Herüberglitt aus einem kleinen Kind  
Mir wie ein Hund unheimlich stumm und fremd.

Dann: daß ich auch vor hundert Jahren war  
Und meine Ahnen, die im Totenhemd,  
Mit mir verwandt sind wie mein eignes Haar,

So eins mit mir als wie mein eignes Haar.

</blockquote>

### Leutnant Gustl (1900)

At the end of 1900, Leutnant Gustl appeared in the Viennese "Neue Freie Presse". [Arthur Schnitzler](#arthur-schnitzler-1862-1931) wrote this work in just five days. Subsequently, court-of-honour proceedings were initiated against Schnitzler, after which he was stripped of his officer's rank. The work is the first example of an [interior monologue](#the-interior-monologue). Because of its novelty, this form was seen as a provocation and a disgrace to the army.

#### Plot

The lieutenant is bored during the concert for which he was given the tickets. When leaving, there is a crush at the cloakroom, which is why Gustl gets into an argument with a master baker. The baker grabs Gustl's sabre, a disgrace for any officer. But nobody notices. Nevertheless, Gustl is afraid that the baker will tell others and thinks up ways out (e.g. moving to America) and the reactions of his relatives; he almost wants to shoot himself, until he finally falls asleep on a park bench. The next morning in the coffee house, he learns that the baker died at midnight.

### Chandos-Brief (1902)

[Hofmannsthal](#hugo-von-hofmannsthal-1874-1929) is sensitive to language and can feel its failure in communication more easily than others. To express this misuse of language, he published a short text in 1902, the Chandos letter.

#### Plot

Lord Chandos, a fictional English author, tries to explain why he has fallen silent as a writer.

> _„[...] [abstrakte Worte] zerfielen mir im Munde wie modrige Pilze.“_

### Der Panther (1903)

Der Panther (The Panther) is about a caged panther that would like to be free. The paradox of the text is the panther's natural strength, but also its weakness due to the thousands of bars surrounding it. In the very last verse, [Rilke](#rainer-maria-rilke-1875-1926) once again emphasises the panther's restricted habitat by shortening the metrical foot.

#### Plot

The panther lies in its cage and slowly vegetates. It is tired and closes its eyelids. Metaphor: the curtain of the pupil.

<blockquote>

DER PANTHER

IM JARDIN DES PLANTES, PARIS

Sein Blick ist vom Vorübergehn der Stäbe  
so müd geworden, daß er nichts mehr hält.  
Ihm ist, als ob es tausend Stäbe gäbe  
und hinter tausend Stäben keine Welt.

Der weiche Gang geschmeidig starker Schritte,  
der sich im allerkleinsten Kreise dreht,  
ist wie ein Tanz von Kraft um eine Mitte,  
in der betäubt ein großer Wille steht.

Nur manchmal schiebt der Vorhang der Pupille  
sich lautlos auf –. Dann geht ein Bild hinein,  
geht durch der Glieder angespannte Stille –  
und hört im Herzen auf zu sein.

</blockquote>

### Die Erblindende (1906)

In this thing poem, the main focus is on the eyes of the blind woman. The eyes are the thing. And for this reason, the blind woman behaves differently from other people: she holds her tea differently, walks slowly, etc.

<blockquote>

Sie saß so wie die anderen beim Tee.  
Mir war zuerst, als ob sie ihre Tasse  
ein wenig anders als die andern fasse.  
Sie lächelte einmal. Es tat fast weh.

Und als man schließlich sich erhob und sprach  
und langsam und wie es der Zufall brachte  
durch viele Zimmer ging (man sprach und lachte),  
da sah ich sie. Sie ging den andern nach,

verhalten, so wie eine, welche gleich  
wird singen müssen und vor vielen Leuten;  
auf ihren hellen Augen die sich freuten  
war Licht von außen wie auf einem Teich.

Sie folgte langsam und sie brauchte lang  
als wäre etwas noch nicht überstiegen;  
und doch: als ob, nach einem Übergang,  
sie nicht mehr gehen würde, sondern fliegen.

</blockquote>

## Genres

In Impressionism, short forms that capture moods and fleeting impressions dominate:

-   **Poetry**: mood poems and [thing poems](#rainer-maria-rilke-1875-1926), which are characterised by musical language and precise observation.
-   **Prose**: novellas, sketches and stories, often with an [interior monologue](#the-interior-monologue).
-   **Drama**: one-act plays and lyrical dramas, for example by Schnitzler and Hofmannsthal, in which mood and the inner life of the characters are more important than the plot.

## Vienna

During Impressionism, Vienna grew rapidly. From 400,000 inhabitants, there were suddenly two million, many of them Jewish citizens. At the same time, political antisemitism began. Hermann Bahr called antisemitism the "morphine of the little people", since demagogues used it as a means to channel national and social tensions onto Jewish people as scapegoats.

## Literature

Writers and authors of Impressionism came from cultivated, wealthy middle-class backgrounds (secure existence), independent of sales. For only with this wealth can one train a sense of beauty. However, literature was naive about social and political questions.

### The interior monologue

[Arthur Schnitzler](#arthur-schnitzler-1862-1931) introduces the interior monologue into German-language literature, in which thoughts, associations and impressions are reproduced. There is no narrator. The prime example of this technique is Arthur Schnitzler's novella [Leutnant Gustl](#leutnant-gustl-1900). In such interior monologues, a person's inner life is often also portrayed in a sexual or erotic way, for example in "Reigen". Poetry is perfectly suited to creating an interior monologue, but the meaning of these texts often remains unclear. It is up to the reader to give the poem a meaning.

> [Rainer Maria Rilke](#rainer-maria-rilke-1875-1926), [Hugo von Hofmannsthal](#hugo-von-hofmannsthal-1874-1929)

### Coffee house

The coffee house became a tradition as a literary meeting place. This tradition has been preserved to this day, though not necessarily with the literary aspect. Peter Altenberg writes a poem, "Kaffeehaus", which symbolises this short literary form very exquisitely.

> Café Griensteidl, Café Central
