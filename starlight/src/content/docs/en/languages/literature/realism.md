After the failure of the 1848 revolution, many authors turned away from political utopias. They wanted to depict **reality** as it is – but not as a mere copy, rather artistically shaped and "transfigured". This is why it is called **poetic** or **bourgeois realism**.

## Key facts

- c. 1848 – 1890
- depiction of reality, but with **transfiguration** (Verklärung)
- the middle classes as readers and subject
- humour as a reconciling attitude
- most important genres: the **novella** and the novel
- politics and social misery are left out

## Historical background

After 1848, a period of restoration began again. **Industrialisation** progressed rapidly, cities grew and the railway changed life. The natural sciences (for example Darwin's theory of evolution, 1859) shaped a new, worldly view of life. In 1871, the German Empire was founded; in 1867, Austria became the **Dual Monarchy of Austria-Hungary**. The wealthy middle classes became the leading class of society but were mostly politically conservative.

## Features

- **Objectivity:** The narrator often steps back, and the characters and their surroundings are described precisely and credibly.
- **Transfiguration:** The ugly and miserable is not left out, but softened. Art should show what is essential and universally valid in reality.
- **Humour:** It reconciles with the contradictions of the world.
- **Themes:** middle-class life, marriage and family, social conventions, conflicts between the individual and society, home and region.
- **Novella:** According to Goethe, it is "an unheard-of event that has occurred". It is usually tightly structured, has a turning point and often a **central symbol** and a **frame narrative**.

## Authors and works

### Theodor Fontane (1819 – 1898)

Fontane was a pharmacist, journalist and theatre critic before becoming a great novelist at almost 60. His best-known work "**Effi Briest**" (1895) tells of a young woman who marries the much older Baron von Innstetten at 17. Out of boredom, she has a short affair with Major Crampas. When Innstetten finds letters years later, he kills Crampas in a duel and casts Effi out; she is excluded from society and dies young. The novel criticises the rigid notions of honour in Prussian society. Other works include "Irrungen, Wirrungen" (Trials and Tribulations), "Frau Jenny Treibel" and the ballad "Herr von Ribbeck auf Ribbeck im Havelland".

### Theodor Storm (1817 – 1888)

The North German Storm mainly wrote novellas. "**Der Schimmelreiter**" (The Rider on the White Horse, 1888) tells of the dike reeve Hauke Haien, who builds a new dike against the resistance and superstition of the villagers and dies with his family in a storm surge. The novella has an elaborate multiple frame. "Immensee" is also well known.

### Gottfried Keller (1819 – 1890)

The Swiss author Keller wrote the Bildungsroman "**Der grüne Heinrich**" (Green Henry) and the collection of novellas "Die Leute von Seldwyla" (The People of Seldwyla) with "**Romeo und Julia auf dem Dorfe**" (A Village Romeo and Juliet, 1856).

### Marie von Ebner-Eschenbach (1830 – 1916)

The Austrian author, born in Moravia, dealt with social issues and the role of women in her works. In the story "**Krambambuli**" (1883), a dog is caught in a conflict of loyalty between his former master, a poacher, and his new master, a forester. Her aphorisms are also well known, such as "Wer nichts weiß, muss alles glauben." (Whoever knows nothing must believe everything.)

### Ludwig Anzengruber (1839 – 1889)

The Viennese Anzengruber wrote realistic **folk plays** about rural life, such as "Der Pfarrer von Kirchfeld" (The Priest of Kirchfeld, 1870) and "Der G'wissenswurm" (The Worm of Conscience), criticising hypocrisy and religious narrow-mindedness.

### Other representatives

- **Peter Rosegger** (1843 – 1918): Styrian regional writer, "Als ich noch der Waldbauernbub war" (When I Was a Forest Farmer's Boy)
- **Ferdinand von Saar** (1833 – 1906): novellas from Austria
- **Conrad Ferdinand Meyer** (1825 – 1898): novellas and poems such as "Zwei Segel" (Two Sails) and "Der römische Brunnen" (The Roman Fountain)
- **Wilhelm Raabe** (1831 – 1910): novels such as "Der Hungerpastor" (The Hunger Pastor)
- **Wilhelm Busch** (1832 – 1908): picture stories such as "Max und Moritz" (1865)

## Genres

- **Epic:** novella, novel (social novel and Bildungsroman), story
- **Poetry:** thing poem, ballad
- **Drama:** folk play (in Austria), otherwise rather unimportant
