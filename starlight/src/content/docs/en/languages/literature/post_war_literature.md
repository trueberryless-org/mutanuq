After the Second World War, cities lay in ruins, and millions of people were dead, displaced or in captivity. Even the language seemed damaged by Nazi propaganda. Young authors therefore wanted to **start anew**: with a simple, honest language that shows reality without embellishment. Later, the question of **guilt** and **responsibility** for the Nazi crimes became the central theme.

## Key facts

- c. 1945 – 1970
- **rubble literature** (Trümmerliteratur, literature of war and homecoming)
- **Kahlschlag** ("clear-cutting") and "zero hour": a radical new beginning in language and literature
- **Group 47** (Gruppe 47)
- the **short story** based on the American model
- coming to terms with the past, criticism of the economic miracle
- division of Germany: literature in the FRG and the GDR

## Historical background

In 1945, Germany and Austria were occupied by the Allies. In 1949, the Federal Republic of Germany (FRG) and the German Democratic Republic (GDR) were founded. Austria regained full independence in 1955 with the **State Treaty**. In the West, the **economic miracle** (Wirtschaftswunder) quickly brought prosperity, and many people preferred to forget the past. However, the Auschwitz trials (1963 – 1965) and the student movement of **1968** forced society to confront the crimes of the Nazi era.

## Rubble literature and Kahlschlag

The first post-war years depict the destroyed cities, hunger, the soldiers' homecoming and psychological wounds. The authors wanted to free the language from the "bombast" of the Nazi era. The term **Kahlschlag** (Wolfgang Weyrauch) describes this radical new beginning: short sentences, simple words, no metaphors and no pathos.

### Wolfgang Borchert (1921 – 1947)

Borchert returned from the war seriously ill and died at the age of only 26. His drama "**Draußen vor der Tür**" (The Man Outside, 1947) tells of the returning soldier Beckmann, who can no longer find a place in post-war society – he always remains "outside the door". His short stories such as "**Das Brot**" (The Bread) and "**Die Küchenuhr**" (The Kitchen Clock) are considered model examples of the genre.

### Heinrich Böll (1917 – 1985)

Böll began with short stories about the war ("Wanderer, kommst du nach Spa…") and later wrote socially critical novels about the Federal Republic, such as "**Ansichten eines Clowns**" (The Clown, 1963) and "**Die verlorene Ehre der Katharina Blum**" (The Lost Honour of Katharina Blum, 1974) about the power of the tabloid press. In 1972, he received the Nobel Prize in Literature. In his essay "Bekenntnis zur Trümmerliteratur" (In Defence of Rubble Literature, 1952), he defended this literature against the accusation of bleakness.

### Günter Eich (1907 – 1972)

His poem "**Inventur**" (Inventory, 1945/47) is considered the prime example of Kahlschlag literature: a prisoner of war soberly lists his few possessions. Eich also wrote many radio plays.

## The short story

The **short story** (Kurzgeschichte, based on the American _short story_) became the most important prose form of the post-war period. Typical features:

- short length
- **abrupt beginning** without an introduction
- **open ending**
- an excerpt from everyday life, often a turning point in a character's life
- few, mostly average characters
- simple, everyday language, often a lot of direct speech
- leitmotifs and symbols

## Group 47

**Group 47** (Gruppe 47) was a loose circle of authors founded by Hans Werner Richter in 1947. At its meetings, members read from unpublished texts and faced direct criticism from the others (the "electric chair"). The group decisively shaped West German literature until 1967. Its members or prize winners included Heinrich Böll, Günter Grass, **Ingeborg Bachmann**, **Ilse Aichinger**, Paul Celan, Martin Walser and Hans Magnus Enzensberger.

## Coming to terms with the past

### Günter Grass (1927 – 2015)

"**Die Blechtrommel**" (The Tin Drum, 1959) tells the story of Oskar Matzerath, who decides on his third birthday to stop growing. From his perspective, the reader experiences the rise of National Socialism, the war and the post-war period in Danzig and Düsseldorf in grotesque form. In 1999, Grass received the Nobel Prize in Literature.

### Friedrich Dürrenmatt (1921 – 1990) and Max Frisch (1911 – 1991)

The two Swiss authors shaped post-war theatre.

- **Dürrenmatt:** In the tragic comedy "**Der Besuch der alten Dame**" (The Visit, 1956), the billionaire Claire Zachanassian returns to her impoverished hometown of Güllen and offers one billion for the death of her former lover Alfred Ill, who once abandoned her. The citizens indignantly refuse – and yet start buying on credit until they finally kill Ill. "**Die Physiker**" (The Physicists, 1962) is about the responsibility of science. Dürrenmatt also wrote crime novels such as "Der Richter und sein Henker" (The Judge and His Hangman).
- **Frisch:** In "**Andorra**" (1961), the young Andri is believed by everyone to be a Jew and is driven to his death by the prejudices of the Andorrans – a play about antisemitism and guilt. In "Biedermann und die Brandstifter" (The Fire Raisers), a citizen lets arsonists into his house out of cowardice. The novel "Homo faber" (1957) is also well known.

### Documentary theatre

In the 1960s, **documentary theatre** brought historical documents onto the stage. In "Die Ermittlung" (The Investigation, 1965), Peter Weiss used transcripts of the Frankfurt Auschwitz trial; in "Der Stellvertreter" (The Deputy, 1963), Rolf Hochhuth criticised the Pope's silence on the Holocaust.

## Literature in the GDR

In the GDR, literature was supposed to serve the building of socialism (**socialist realism**). Critical authors were censored or expatriated, like the singer-songwriter Wolf Biermann in 1976. Important works are Christa Wolf's "Der geteilte Himmel" (Divided Heaven, 1963) about the division of Germany and Ulrich Plenzdorf's "Die neuen Leiden des jungen W." (The New Sufferings of Young W., 1972), which transfers Goethe's "Werther" to the GDR of the 1970s.

## Genres

- **Epic:** short story, novel, radio play
- **Poetry:** Kahlschlag poetry, hermetic poetry, political poetry, concrete poetry
- **Drama:** homecoming drama, parable play, documentary theatre
