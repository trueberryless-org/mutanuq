## Impressionism

In the epoch of Impressionism, artists do not focus on a specific world view but deal with a variety of literary styles. These are influenced by Realism, Naturalism, "workers' poetry" and Arthur Schnitzler. Geographically, Vienna, Berlin and Munich are the major centres. Impressionism not only distances itself from politics and society, but also refuses to depict reality realistically or in a critical, naturalistic way. In the context of Impressionism, this means that artists consciously decided not to represent reality in the traditional way that had been common in earlier art. In painting, where the term comes from, the Impressionists preferred loose brushwork, bright colours and the rendering of fleeting impressions to sharp contours, detailed realism and meticulous precision.

### Key facts

-   end of the 19th century, beginning of the 20th century
-   variety of world views and literary styles
-   centres: Berlin, Vienna, Munich

### Impressionism and co.

Originally, the term **Impressionism** described a style of painting. In the context of the epoch, Impressionism describes works that convey subjective impressions, feelings, moods, light, shadow etc., but have no concrete plot.

The term **Symbolism**, on the other hand, describes poetry that has no "purpose" but a magical language. Symbolism is the art of "hinting".

Other terms related to Impressionism are **fin de siècle** and **Viennese Modernism** (Wiener Moderne). The former literally means "end of the century" in French and describes the mood of this time between a sense of doom, decadence and new beginnings. Associated with it is the principle of _l’art pour l’art_: art should exist for its own sake and stand on its own. In Viennese Modernism – as the name suggests – Vienna is at the centre of literature, architecture, painting etc.

### Artists

#### Friedrich Nietzsche (1844-1900)

The philosopher Friedrich Nietzsche criticises the "average person" who merely vegetates. To become an "Übermensch" (superman), art is needed. This art should be free of moral and political constraints.

> Die Kunst und nichts als die Kunst!  
> _Sie ist die große Ermöglicherin des Lebens, die große Verführerin zum Leben, das große Stimulans des Lebens!_

In [Also sprach Zarathustra](#also-sprach-zarathustra-1883), this criticism is dealt with specifically.

#### Charles Baudelaire (1821-1867)

According to the French poet Charles Baudelaire, culture is the "victory" over nature. In addition, language creates its own reality. With his collection of poems "Les Fleurs du mal" (The Flowers of Evil, 1857), Baudelaire is considered a pioneer of poetry freed from all purposes (_pure poetry_). In "Les Paradis artificiels" (Artificial Paradises, 1860), he also dealt with states of intoxication caused by alcohol and drugs.

#### Ernst Mach (1838-1916)

The physicist and philosopher Ernst Mach believed that reality and personality are a succession of "colours, sounds, warmths, pressures and spaces". His sentence "The ego cannot be saved" (Das Ich ist unrettbar) became famous. He was one of the central thinkers of Impressionism.

#### Sigmund Freud (1856-1939)

Sigmund Freud was a neurologist and the founder of psychoanalysis. He described the **three-part human personality** (structural model). It divides every person's way of thinking and acting into three areas: **ego** (ICH), **superego** (ÜBER-ICH) and **id** (ES). While the id works completely unconsciously and drives self-realisation, sexuality and aggression, the superego is its counterpart. The superego contains moral conscience, authorities, values and norms. The ego finds the middle ground between the superego and the id and lies largely in conscious thinking. Conflicts between these three layers shape us.

![Three-part human personality](/images/languages/dreischichtige_menschliche_persoenlichkeit.png)

Freud assumed that a large part of our thinking and acting is controlled unconsciously – similar to an iceberg, of which only the tip is visible. In addition, Freud held [Schnitzler](#arthur-schnitzler-1862-1931) in high esteem and even called him his "doppelgänger" in a letter, because Schnitzler described similar insights into the human psyche in his works.

#### Arthur Schnitzler (1862-1931)

Arthur Schnitzler was a Viennese doctor and writer. He was the first author in German-language literature to use an interior monologue throughout a work, in [Leutnant Gustl (1900)](#leutnant-gustl-1900). His model was the novel "Les Lauriers sont coupés" (1887) by the Frenchman Édouard Dujardin.

#### Hugo von Hofmannsthal (1874-1929)

Hugo von Hofmannsthal's poems are among the most famous of Viennese Modernism, but also among the most difficult to interpret. One of the most difficult is [Terzinen über Vergänglichkeit (1894)](#terzinen-über-vergänglichkeit-1894), alongside many other works such as the [Chandos-Brief (1902)](#chandos-brief-1902), the libretto for "Der Rosenkavalier" (1911) and the mystery play "Jedermann" (1911), which has been performed at the Salzburg Festival since 1920.

#### Rainer Maria Rilke (1875-1926)

Rilke's first great success was the lyrical narrative "Die Weise von Liebe und Tod des Cornets Christoph Rilke" (written in 1899, published in 1906). Rilke also wrote many "New Poems" (Neue Gedichte), which do not focus on feelings or sensations but on precise observations of people, animals and everyday objects in and around Paris. That is why they are also called "thing poems" (Dinggedichte). These include, for example, poems such as [Der Panther (1903)](#der-panther-1903) and [Die Erblindende (1906)](#die-erblindende-1906). With great sensitivity, Rilke filters out what is essential for these "things" in order to fathom their "true nature".

#### Karl Kraus (1874-1936)

Karl Kraus was an Austrian writer, critic and satirist who lived from 1874 to 1936. He was a sharp-tongued critic of Viennese Modernism, the press and society. His texts were characterised by his rejection of empty phrases and of manipulation through language. Kraus is considered one of the most important German-language authors of the 20th century.

### Works

#### Also sprach Zarathustra (1883)

With this and a few other works, [Friedrich Nietzsche](#friedrich-nietzsche-1844-1900) lays the philosophical foundation for the criticism of the time in [Expressionism](#expressionism). In this work, Nietzsche contrasts the "last man", who only cares about his petty profit and actually denies and destroys life, with the "Übermensch". The latter despises the prevailing morality, thinking and society and grows beyond his personal pettiness.

> _„Ich lehre euch den Übermenschen.“_

#### Die Fackel (1899-1936)

Die Fackel (The Torch) is a magazine that was published by Karl Kraus in Vienna from 1899 to 1936. The magazine had a sharp, polemical tone and addressed a wide range of social, cultural and political issues. Die Fackel became a central organ of criticism of Austrian society and especially of the press. [Kraus](#karl-kraus-1874-1936)' style was characterised by pointed analyses, paradoxical formulations and a deep mistrust of the powers of his time.

In terms of content, Die Fackel dealt with various topics, including political corruption and social injustice, but also cultural developments and literary works. Kraus did not mince his words and sharply attacked both individuals and institutions when he identified grievances and hypocrisy.

Die Fackel can be seen as a critical voice in a time of upheaval and political change. Kraus' writing style and his analysis of society have had a lasting influence on German-language literature and cultural history.

#### Terzinen über Vergänglichkeit (1894)

Overall, this work consists of three parts. The first of these three is written in iambic pentameter and, as is typical for tercets, follows the scheme of the chain rhyme (`ABA CBC DCD ...`). In this order, [Hofmannsthal](#hugo-von-hofmannsthal-1874-1929) describes memory, shock, insight and alienation in the five stanzas (the last one consists of only a single verse).

<blockquote>

Noch spür ich ihren Atem auf den Wangen:  
Wie kann das sein, daß diese nahen Tage  
Fort sind, für immer fort, und ganz vergangen?

Dies ist ein Ding, das keiner voll aussinnt,  
Und viel zu grauenvoll, als daß man klage:  
Daß alles gleitet und vorüberrinnt.

Und daß mein eignes Ich, durch nichts gehemmt,  
Herüberglitt aus einem kleinen Kind  
Mir wie ein Hund unheimlich stumm und fremd.

Dann: daß ich auch vor hundert Jahren war  
Und meine Ahnen, die im Totenhemd,  
Mit mir verwandt sind wie mein eignes Haar,

So eins mit mir als wie mein eignes Haar.

</blockquote>

#### Leutnant Gustl (1900)

At the end of 1900, Leutnant Gustl appeared in the Viennese "Neue Freie Presse". [Arthur Schnitzler](#arthur-schnitzler-1862-1931) wrote this work in just five days. Subsequently, military court-of-honour proceedings were initiated against Schnitzler, who, as a reserve senior medical officer, was himself an officer. In 1901, he was stripped of his officer's rank because he had allegedly violated the honour of the army with the work. The work is the first example of a continuous [interior monologue](#the-interior-monologue) in German-language literature. The unsparing portrayal of a cowardly, superficial officer was seen as a provocation and a disgrace to the army.

##### Plot

The lieutenant is bored during the concert for which he was given the tickets. When leaving, there is a crush at the cloakroom, which is why Gustl gets into an argument with a master baker. The baker grabs Gustl's sabre, a disgrace for any officer. But nobody notices. Nevertheless, Gustl is afraid that the baker will tell others and thinks up ways out (e.g. moving to America) and the reactions of his relatives; he almost wants to shoot himself, until he finally falls asleep on a park bench. The next morning in the coffee house, he learns that the baker died of a stroke during the night – and Gustl is relieved.

#### Chandos-Brief (1902)

[Hofmannsthal](#hugo-von-hofmannsthal-1874-1929) is sensitive to language and can feel its failure in communication more easily than others. To express this crisis of language, he published a short text in 1902, the Chandos letter.

##### Plot

Lord Chandos – a fictional English author – tries to explain why he has fallen silent as a writer.

> _„[...] [abstrakte Worte] zerfielen mir im Munde wie modrige Pilze.“_

#### Der Panther (1903)

Der Panther (The Panther) is about a caged panther that would like to be free. The paradox of the text is the panther's natural strength, but also its weakness due to the thousands of bars surrounding it. In the very last verse, [Rilke](#rainer-maria-rilke-1875-1926) once again emphasises the panther's restricted habitat by shortening the verse by one stress (four instead of five stresses).

##### Plot

The panther lies in its cage and slowly vegetates. It is tired and closes its eyelids – metaphor: the curtain of the pupil.

<blockquote>

DER PANTHER

IM JARDIN DES PLANTES, PARIS

Sein Blick ist vom Vorübergehn der Stäbe  
so müd geworden, daß er nichts mehr hält.  
Ihm ist, als ob es tausend Stäbe gäbe  
und hinter tausend Stäben keine Welt.

Der weiche Gang geschmeidig starker Schritte,  
der sich im allerkleinsten Kreise dreht,  
ist wie ein Tanz von Kraft um eine Mitte,  
in der betäubt ein großer Wille steht.

Nur manchmal schiebt der Vorhang der Pupille  
sich lautlos auf –. Dann geht ein Bild hinein,  
geht durch der Glieder angespannte Stille –  
und hört im Herzen auf zu sein.

</blockquote>

#### Die Erblindende (1906)

In this thing poem, the main focus is on the eyes of the blind woman. The eyes are the thing. And for this reason, the blind woman behaves differently from other people – she holds her tea differently, walks slowly, etc.

<blockquote>

Sie saß so wie die anderen beim Tee.  
Mir war zuerst, als ob sie ihre Tasse  
ein wenig anders als die andern fasse.  
Sie lächelte einmal. Es tat fast weh.

Und als man schließlich sich erhob und sprach  
und langsam und wie es der Zufall brachte  
durch viele Zimmer ging (man sprach und lachte),  
da sah ich sie. Sie ging den andern nach,

verhalten, so wie eine, welche gleich  
wird singen müssen und vor vielen Leuten;  
auf ihren hellen Augen die sich freuten  
war Licht von außen wie auf einem Teich.

Sie folgte langsam und sie brauchte lang  
als wäre etwas noch nicht überstiegen;  
und doch: als ob, nach einem Übergang,  
sie nicht mehr gehen würde, sondern fliegen.

</blockquote>

### Genres

In Impressionism, short forms that capture moods and fleeting impressions dominate:

-   **Poetry**: mood poems and [thing poems](#rainer-maria-rilke-1875-1926), which are characterised by musical language and precise observation.
-   **Prose**: novellas, sketches and stories, often with an [interior monologue](#the-interior-monologue).
-   **Drama**: one-act plays and lyrical dramas, for example by Schnitzler and Hofmannsthal, in which mood and the inner life of the characters are more important than the plot.

### Vienna

Around the turn of the century, Vienna grew rapidly: between 1850 and 1910, its population rose from around 400,000 to over two million, including many Jewish citizens. At the same time, political antisemitism increased, for example under the Christian Social mayor Karl Lueger (from 1897). Hermann Bahr called antisemitism the "morphine of the little people", since demagogues used it as a means to channel national and social tensions onto Jewish people as scapegoats.

### Literature

Writers and authors of Impressionism mostly came from cultivated, wealthy middle-class backgrounds (secure existence), independent of sales. Only with this wealth – according to the attitude of the time – could one develop a sense of beauty. However, literature was naive about social and political questions.

#### The interior monologue

[Arthur Schnitzler](#arthur-schnitzler-1862-1931) introduces the interior monologue into German-language literature, in which thoughts, associations and impressions are reproduced. There is no narrator. Prime examples of this technique are Arthur Schnitzler's novellas [Leutnant Gustl](#leutnant-gustl-1900) and "Fräulein Else" (1924). Schnitzler often also portrays the inner life of his characters in a sexual or erotic way – most clearly in the drama "Reigen", which caused a scandal when it was performed in 1921. The poetry of this time also focuses on subjective impressions and moods, but the meaning of these texts often remains unclear. It is up to the reader to give the poem a meaning.

> [Rainer Maria Rilke](#rainer-maria-rilke-1875-1926), [Hugo von Hofmannsthal](#hugo-von-hofmannsthal-1874-1929)

#### Coffee house

The coffee house became a tradition as a literary meeting place. This tradition has been preserved to this day, though not necessarily with the literary aspect. The poet Peter Altenberg, who practically lived in the Café Central and gave it as his postal address, shaped so-called coffee house literature with his short prose sketches.

> Café Griensteidl, Café Central

## Expressionism

In the epoch of Expressionism, artists strongly criticise several topics, such as the big city, mass society, industrialisation, militarisation, manipulation and the First World War. This epoch is opposed to Realism and Naturalism, which only depicted reality. In addition, it turns away from the fin de siècle (escape into aesthetics) and from Goethe's classicism, which was perceived as bourgeois.

> _[...] Weg überhaupt mit den sogenannten Dichtern! Schluss! Unsere Kultur ist Gerümpel. [...] Der junge Dichter muss demolieren. [...]_

Role models of Expressionism are the Sturm und Drang, the Baroque, the art of children and of indigenous peoples as well as literary outsiders (Hölderlin, Kleist, Büchner and E.T.A. Hoffmann). Many authors and artists in general call themselves activists, "Sturm" artists and abstract artists.

### Key facts

-   beginning of the 20th century (approx. 1910 to 1925)
-   shattering of language
-   poems that cannot be interpreted

### Expressionism and co.

The term Expressionism (Latin _expressio_ = expression) originally comes from the visual arts, as practised for example by Egon Schiele, Oskar Kokoschka or the artists' groups "Die Brücke" and "Der Blaue Reiter". The pictures are meant to express how the artist experiences the world.

The term Dadaism emerged during the epoch of Expressionism and describes a destruction of language. It includes poems that cannot be interpreted and have no meaning.

### Artists

The foundation of this epoch is [Friedrich Nietzsche](#friedrich-nietzsche-1844-1900)'s [Also sprach Zarathustra](#also-sprach-zarathustra-1883) and the philosophy of Søren Kierkegaard, who puts freedom at the centre of thinking. Unlike animals, humans can decide on a particular way of life. They can remain in a superficial life of pleasure, the existence Kierkegaard called "aesthetic". But they can also work in solidarity on building a humane society in an "ethical" existence.

The Expressionists also contrast the superficial old person with a coming new person who is fundamentally different from the old one and shows solidarity with fellow human beings. The "old" person is hard-hearted, harsh, evil, alien to fellow human beings and separated by boundary posts. In addition, the terms **society** (Gesellschaft) and **community** (Gemeinschaft) are contrasted: the former defines a construct with rules in which you do not know your fellow human beings, the latter describes a more harmonious, smaller group of people who consciously know each other.

#### Hermann Bahr (1863-1934)

Hermann Bahr was an Austrian writer and critic and is considered the spokesman of Viennese Modernism. In 1916, he published the essay "Expressionismus", in which he interpreted the new art movement as art crying out against a time that turned people into mere machines.

#### August Stramm (1874-1915)

August Stramm was a German Expressionist poet and playwright. He died in the First World War. Known for his innovative language and experimental style, he left behind collections of poems such as "Du" (1915) and "Tropfblut" (1919, published after his death). Stramm's radical approach to literature had a decisive influence on Expressionism. Some of his well-known works are ["Zwist" (1914)](#zwist-1914) and ["Patrouille" (1915)](#patrouille-1915).

#### Franz Kafka (1883-1924)

Franz Kafka was an influential German-language writer of the 20th century. Known for his unique literary world, he created works such as ["Die Verwandlung" (The Metamorphosis, 1915)](#die-verwandlung-1915), in which the protagonist turns into a "monstrous vermin", and ["Der Prozess" (The Trial, 1914)](#der-prozess-1914) (written in 1914/15, only published in 1925 after his death), which deals with the absurdities of an opaque judicial system. In his ["Brief an den Vater" (Letter to His Father, 1919)](#brief-an-den-vater-1919), Kafka reflects on his relationship with his authoritarian father.

Although he lived at the time of Expressionism, Kafka's works defy easy classification in this epoch. His works are characterised by existential themes, isolation and the breaking out of reality. In almost all of his works, an incident occurs that interrupts everyday life.

Kafka died in 1924 and left behind a literary legacy of great depth and complexity that continues to fascinate readers worldwide. Because of his unique writing style and content, Kafka even has his own adjective: "Kafkaesque" (_in the manner of Kafka's depictions; mysteriously uncanny, threatening_).

##### Kafka's life

In many works, Kafka builds his own name into the main character with similarities. For example, `Samsa` has the same vowel sequence and number of letters as `Kafka`. There are also similarities between Gregor's flat in ["Die Verwandlung"](#die-verwandlung-1915) and Kafka's own flat. This suggests that the hopelessness of Kafka's characters in the face of powers that exceed their own strength has personal roots. Some of Kafka's traumas can be read from the [letter to his father](#brief-an-den-vater-1919).

#### Gottfried Benn (1886-1956)

Gottfried Benn was a doctor and poet. In his first collection of poems, "Morgue" (1912), he processed his experiences from the mortuary and shocked readers with a sober, cold portrayal of death and decay. He also criticised a belief in progress that also drove militarisation: he saw no progress if the murderous rifle bullet became an even more murderous grenade. His best-known works include [Kleine Aster](#kleine-aster-1912) and [Schöne Jugend](#schöne-jugend-1912).

#### Georg Heym (1887-1912)

Georg Heym was a German poet and writer of Expressionism. Born in Hirschberg in Silesia, he studied law, among other places in Berlin. There he joined the Expressionist group of poets "Der Neue Club".

Heym's literary work is characterised by a dark, often apocalyptic atmosphere. Well-known works are the collection of poems "Der ewige Tag" (1911), the only one published during his lifetime, and the poem it contains, ["Der Gott der Stadt" (1910)](#der-gott-der-stadt-1910). His writing style reflects the unrest and dissatisfaction of the time before the First World War.

The poet tragically died young: in January 1912, at the age of only 24, he drowned in the river Havel while ice skating when he tried to help a friend who had fallen through the ice. Despite his short life, Georg Heym had a lasting influence on German Expressionist literature.

#### Jakob van Hoddis (1887-1942)

Jakob van Hoddis was a German Expressionist poet. Born Hans Davidsohn in Berlin, he later changed his name to Jakob van Hoddis, an anagram of his surname. His life was marked by mental instability, and he spent some time in psychiatric institutions.

Van Hoddis was a member of the Expressionist movement and part of the group of poets "Der Neue Club". His best-known work is the poem ["Weltende" (1911)](#weltende-1911), which is considered one of the most important Expressionist poems. This work reflects the unrest and uncertainty of the time before the First World War.

Jakob van Hoddis' literary career was short, and because of his illness he was forgotten from the First World War onwards. As a mentally ill Jew, he became a victim of the National Socialist regime: in 1942, he was deported from a sanatorium in Bendorf-Sayn and murdered in the Sobibor extermination camp.

Despite his short creative period, van Hoddis had a lasting influence on Expressionist poetry and is regarded as an important representative of this literary movement.

#### Alfred Lichtenstein (1889-1914)

Alfred Lichtenstein was a German Expressionist poet. Born in Berlin, he became known for his sarcastic and socially critical poems. Lichtenstein was part of the Expressionist literary movement and published mainly in the magazine "Die Aktion". His work reflects the unrest and tensions of the time before the First World War. He was killed in the war in 1914 at the age of 25. Lichtenstein's poems, including ["Abschied" (1914)](#abschied-1914) and "Die Dämmerung", show a unique style characterised by a dark world view and a pronounced linguistic artistry.

### Works

#### Der Gott der Stadt (1910)

In this poem, [Georg Heym](#georg-heym-1887-1912) describes God (Baal) with dark colours, such as "schwarzen Winden" (black winds), "roter Bauch" (red belly), "schwarzen Türmen" (black towers) and "dunkler Abend" (dark evening). God is angry, sour, gloomy, red. In addition, the city is described with rather negative aspects: factories, exhaust fumes, fire, noise, industrialisation, mass society, flames, smouldering smoke.

<blockquote>

Auf einem Häuserblocke sitzt er breit.  
Die Winde lagern schwarz um seine Stirn.  
Er schaut voll Wut, wo fern in Einsamkeit  
Die letzten Häuser in das Land verirrn.

Vom Abend glänzt der rote Bauch dem Baal,  
die großen Städte knien um ihn her.  
Der Kirchenglocken ungeheure Zahl  
Wogt auf zu ihm aus schwarzer Türme Meer.

Wie Korybanten-Tanz dröhnt die Musik  
Der Millionen durch die Straßen laut.  
Der Schlote Rauch, die Wolken der Fabrik  
Ziehn auf zu ihm, wie Duft von Weihrauch blaut.

Das Wetter schwelt in seinen Augenbrauen.  
Der dunkle Abend wird in Nacht betäubt.  
Die Stürme flattern, die wie Geier schauen  
Von seinem Haupthaar, das im Zorne sträubt.

Er streckt ins Dunkel seine Fleischerfaust.  
Er schüttelt sie. Ein Meer von Feuer jagt  
Durch eine Straße. Und der Glutqualm braust  
Und frißt sie auf, bis spät der Morgen tagt.

</blockquote>

#### Weltende (1911)

In the poem "Weltende" (End of the World), the storm describes destruction, as the waves become tsunamis, houses collapse and the world falls apart. The penultimate verse ("Die meisten Menschen haben einen Schnupfen" – most people have a cold) stands out in particular, since it describes a comparatively small problem in contrast to the end of the world. [Jakob van Hoddis](#jakob-van-hoddis-1887-1942) wants to express that people do not care about anything they only read about in the newspaper.

<blockquote>

Dem Bürger fliegt vom spitzen Kopf der Hut,  
In allen Lüften hallt es wie Geschrei,  
Dachdecker stürzen ab und gehn entzwei  
Und an den Küsten – liest man – steigt die Flut.

Der Sturm ist da, die wilden Meere hupfen  
An Land, um dicke Dämme zu zerdrücken.  
Die meisten Menschen haben einen Schnupfen.  
Die Eisenbahnen fallen von den Brücken.

</blockquote>

#### Schöne Jugend (1912)

The title may be misleading, since this poem from the "Morgue" cycle is not about young people but about **rats** that have spent a "beautiful youth" in the body of a drowned girl.

<blockquote>

Der Mund eines Mädchens, das lange im Schilf gelegen hatte,  
sah so angeknabbert aus.  
Als man die Brust aufbrach, war die Speiseröhre so löcherig.  
Schließlich in einer Laube unter dem Zwerchfell  
fand man ein Nest von jungen Ratten.  
Ein kleines Schwesterchen lag tot.  
Die andern lebten von Leber und Niere,  
tranken das kalte Blut und hatten  
hier eine schöne Jugend verlebt.  
Und schön und schnell kam auch ihr Tod:  
Man warf sie allesamt ins Wasser.  
Ach, wie die kleinen Schnauzen quietschten!

</blockquote>

#### Kleine Aster (1912)

The title suggests an idyllic flower poem. In fact, however, it is about the autopsy of a drowned beer-delivery driver in whose chest cavity an aster is placed, so that, just like with [Schöne Jugend](#schöne-jugend-1912), the reader is deceived.

<blockquote>

Ein ersoffener Bierfahrer wurde auf den Tisch gestemmt.  
Irgendeiner hatte ihm eine dunkelhelllila Aster zwischen die Zähne geklemmt.  
Als ich von der Brust aus  
unter der Haut  
mit einem langen Messer  
Zunge und Gaumen herausschnitt,  
Muss ich sie angestoßen haben, denn sie glitt in das nebenliegende Gehirn.  
Ich packte sie ihm in die Brusthöhle zwischen die Holzwolle,  
als man zunähte.  
Trinke dich satt in deiner Vase!  
Ruhe sanft,  
Kleine Aster!

</blockquote>

#### Der Prozess (1914)

A novel in which Josef K. is arrested although he has done nothing wrong. Throughout the entire plot, no guilt on Josef K.'s part becomes apparent. For this reason, "Der Prozess" does not end with a trial either. Just as at the beginning of the novel two men appear in K.'s room and throw him out of his usual life, two men appear in the final chapter to end the "trial". Josef K. knows that it would be his duty to kill himself with the knife, but he does not do it. Instead, the two men lead him to a small quarry on the outskirts of town, where they stab him – "Like a dog!" are his last words.

#### Zwist (1914)

Not everything in the poem can be interpreted, but some stylistic devices can be identified, e.g. antitheses (Quälen küsst, Kosen schelten, ...). Another (possibly noteworthy) feature is the excessive use of verbs.

<blockquote>

Gallen foltern bäumen lösen  
Knirschen zürnen meiden Hass  
Verben  
Zittern stampfen schäumen grämen  
Suchen beben forschen bang  
Wenden zagen schauen langen  
Stehen rühren seufzen gehn  
Streicheln klagen  
Kosen schelten  
Schämen schmäht Und  
Fliehen wirbt  
Schmiegen wehret  
Armen sträubet  
Quälen küsst

</blockquote>

#### Abschied (1914)

[Alfred Lichtenstein](#alfred-lichtenstein-1889-1914) wrote this linguistically excellent poem shortly before his death in the First World War.

<blockquote>

Vorm Sterben mache ich noch mein Gedicht.  
Still, Kameraden, stört mich nicht.

Wir ziehn zum Krieg. Der Tod ist unser Kitt.  
Oh; heulte mir doch die Geliebte nit. -

Was liegt an mir. Ich gehe gerne ein.  
Die Mutter weint. Man muß aus Eisen sein.

#### Die Verwandlung (1915)

Kafka's stories and novels often begin with situations that are inexplicable and threatening for those affected. A completely realistic situation suddenly bursts the boundaries of reality. There are many parodies of Die Verwandlung, for example [Jan Böhmermann's satire](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8F2IXNErOas). "Die Verwandlung" begins with the following sentences:

<blockquote>

Als Gregor Samsa eines Morgens aus unruhigen Träumen erwachte, fand er sich in seinem Bett zu einem ungeheueren Ungeziefer verwandelt. Er lag auf seinem panzerartig harten Rücken und sah, wenn er den Kopf ein wenig hob, seinen gewölbten, braunen, von bogenförmigen Versteifungen geteilten Bauch, auf dessen Höhe sich die Bettdecke, zum gänzlichen Niedergleiten bereit, kaum noch erhalten konnte. Seine vielen, im Vergleich zu seinem sonstigen Umfang kläglich dünnen Beine flimmerten ihm hilflos vor den Augen. »Was ist mit mir geschehen?« dachte er. Es war kein Traum.

</blockquote>

The story is divided into three parts, the first of which deals with the first hours after the transformation. The second part begins on the evening of the day of the transformation and describes the following weeks. The last part tells of long-term consequences that only happen months after the transformation.

##### Part 1

Gregor Samsa wakes up in the morning and realises that he has turned into a giant vermin, which is usually imagined as a beetle. He thinks about his job as a travelling cloth salesman. He hates it, but it is necessary to support the family. Mother, father and sister Grete knock on the door because they want to ask anxiously how he is. It takes Gregor so long to get out of bed that in the meantime a chief clerk from Gregor's employer has turned up to ask about him. In addition, his professional performance is criticised. Gregor then wants to assure them that he will take the next train, but those present only hear animal sounds. A doctor and a locksmith are called to open the door. But Gregor opens the door with his jaws so that his family can help him. Father, mother and the chief clerk react in horror to the sight of him. Gregor is pushed back with a stick and injured on his left leg.

##### Part 2

In the evening, Gregor wakes up because of the smell of delicious food. His sister has put his favourite drink at the door, but he feels an aversion to the milk. He crawls under the sofa and finally feels comfortable. Early in the morning, Grete brings him a large selection of food, of which he only eats the half-rotten items with pleasure.

In the following days, the family worries about money. Grete takes over looking after Gregor and notices that he likes to crawl up the walls. Mother and sister then want to clear the furniture out of his room, but Gregor clings to a picture of a lady in furs. The mother faints and Gregor wants to help her. The sister says that the vermin has broken out, and the father pelts him with apples.

##### Part 3

One of the apples remains stuck in his body for months, so that the spot becomes inflamed. Gregor is increasingly neglected and his room becomes a storage room. As an additional source of income, the family rents out a room of the flat to three lodgers, which is why Gregor's door remains closed during dinner. But when his sister plays the violin, Gregor opens the door and the gentlemen threaten to give notice. Grete demands that they must try to get rid of **it** (Gregor). That night, Gregor dies, weakened by the injury from the apple and by hunger. The new charwoman – the only one who does not detest Gregor – informs the family and disposes of him.

The family is relieved: they throw out the lodgers, dismiss the charwoman and go on an excursion to the countryside. On the way, the parents notice that Grete has grown into a beautiful girl and that it will soon be time for her to find a husband.

#### Brief an den Vater (1919)

In the night of 22 to 23 September 1912, Kafka writes the story "Das Urteil" (The Judgement). The two acting characters are father and son. At the end of a long conversation, the father says to the son: _"Ich verurteile dich jetzt zum Tode des Ertrinkens!"_ (I now sentence you to death by drowning!) Without resistance, the son accepts the verdict and lets himself fall from the bridge into the river. At the age of 36, in 1919, Kafka writes the "Brief an den Vater" (Letter to His Father). It comprises more than 100 pages and was actually intended to be handed to his father. Kafka probably asked his mother to hand it over, but this never happened.

<blockquote>

Deine äußerst wirkungsvollen, wenigstens mir gegenüber niemals versagenden rednerischen Mittel bei der Erziehung waren: Schimpfen, Drohen, Ironie, böses Lachen und merkwürdigerweise - Selbstbeklagung. Das Schimpfen verstärktest Du mit Drohen [...]. Schrecklich war mir zum Beispiel dieses: ich zerreiße Dich wie einen Fisch, trotzdem ich ja wusste, dass dem nichts Schlimmeres nachfolgte (als kleines Kind wusste ich das allerdings nicht), aber es entsprach fast meinen Vorstellungen von Deiner Macht, dass Du auch das imstande gewesen wärest. Schrecklich war es auch, wenn Du schreiend um den Tisch herumliefst, um einen zu fassen, offenbar gar nicht fassen wolltest, aber doch so tatest und die Mutter einen schließlich scheinbar rettete. Wieder hatte man einmal, so schien es dem Kind, das Leben durch Deine Gnade behalten und trug es als Dein unverdientes Geschenk weiter. [...]

Deine Erziehungsmittel in den allerersten Jahren kann ich heute natürlich nicht unmittelbar beschreiben, aber ich kann sie mir etwa vorstellen durch Rückschluss aus den später erfahrenen [...]. Direkt erinnere ich mich nur an einen Vorfall aus den ersten Jahren. Du erinnerst Dich vielleicht auch daran. Ich winselte einmal in der Nacht immerfort um Wasser, gewiss nicht aus Durst, sondern wahrscheinlich teils um zu ärgern, teils um mich zu unterhalten. Nachdem einige starke Drohungen nicht geholfen hatten, nahmst Du mich aus dem Bett, trugst mich auf die Pawlatsche[^2] und ließest mich dort allein vor der geschlossenen Tür ein Weilchen im Hemd stehn. Ich will nicht sagen, dass das unrichtig war, vielleicht war damals die Nachtruhe auf andere Weise wirklich nicht zu verschaffen, ich will aber damit Deine Erziehungsmittel und ihre Wirkung auf mich charakterisieren. Ich war damals nachher wohl schon folgsam, aber ich hatte einen inneren Schaden davon. [...] Noch nach Jahren litt ich unter der quälenden Vorstellung, dass der riesige Mann, mein Vater, die letzte Instanz, fast ohne Grund kommen und mich in der Nacht aus dem Bett auf die Pawlatsche tragen konnte und dass ich also ein solches Nichts für ihn war.

</blockquote>

### Genres

#### Poetry

The poems should not "move" (rühren) but "stir up" (aufrühren) and create a belief in the "new" person. The confrontation with the threatening, repulsive and grotesque forms the second facet of poetry, as in the poems ["Weltende" (1911)](#weltende-1911) by [Jakob van Hoddis](#jakob-van-hoddis-1887-1942) and ["Schöne Jugend" (1912)](#schöne-jugend-1912) by [Gottfried Benn](#gottfried-benn-1886-1956).

The form of the poems varies greatly: some show metaphor-rich and emotional language, while others shatter sentence structure and negate logic.

In 1919, the most representative collection of Expressionist poems appeared, edited by Kurt Pinthus. It is called "Menschheitsdämmerung" (Twilight of Humanity). Its four sections show the themes of Expressionist poetry: "Sturz und Schrei" (fall and scream), "Erweckung des Herzens" (awakening of the heart), "Aufruf und Empörung" (call and outrage) and "Liebe den Menschen" (love to people).

#### Drama

In drama, there is a struggle between the "old" and the "new" person, for example the generational conflict and the struggle of sons against fathers. Parricide is the symbol of the downfall of authorities. There is also a conflict between the sexes. Here, the drama "Frühlings Erwachen" (Spring Awakening, 1891) by Frank Wedekind is regarded as a model because of its criticism of sexual morality and school.

#### Prose

In Expressionism, only a few works are devoted to prose. The shattering of language, enthusiasm and pathos can be applied much better in poetry and drama. However, one prose writer from this time is very famous: Franz Kafka – although he cannot be clearly assigned to Expressionism. He writes about the alienation of people in a world they do not understand. Some of his most important works are "Die Verwandlung", "Der Prozess" and "Eine alltägliche Verwirrung". He also wrote a "Brief an den Vater", which comprises more than 100 pages and reveals a lot about Franz Kafka's character.

### Magazines

In this epoch, many literary magazines were published, such as "Die Aktion" (from 1911) or "Der Sturm" (from 1910). They offered young, still unknown authors a platform.

### Dadaism

Dadaism was born when Hugo Ball, Richard Huelsenbeck, Hans Arp and Tristan Tzara met in the "Cabaret Voltaire" in Zurich in 1916 and discovered the word "Dada" for their art in a dictionary. This "anti-art" was directed against everything that had come before. The "Dadaist Manifesto" of 1918 states that language should express the "explosion" of the time.

To demonstrate this destruction of language, the four founders used various methods, such as "unreason" (no logic in texts), sound poems (no words, but individual sounds), letter poems (focus on one word) and chance. Particularly striking poems are, firstly, the **bruitist** poem – also noise poem – which is supplemented by sounds and acting, and secondly, the **simultaneous** poem, in which several poems are read at the same time.

#### The Vienna Group

At the end of the 1950s, a group of poets appeared in Austrian literature who initially met with massive public resistance. Friedrich Achleitner, H. C. Artmann, Konrad Bayer, Gerhard Rühm, Oswald Wiener as well as Ernst Jandl and Friederike Mayröcker, who were close to the group, provoked. Words and sentences are broken apart and swapped, and the grammatical, orthographic and syntactic norms of language are destroyed. Absolutely "unpoetic" vocabulary from everyday up to vulgar language finds its way into poetry; they write radically in lower case so as not to favour nouns (which are capitalised in German). The inclusion of acoustic and graphic elements of language in poetry is just as astonishing as the discovery of dialect for poetry, which can be seen, among other things, in H. C. Artmann's work "med ana schwoazzn dintn", which later became a poetry bestseller.

##### Fisches Nachtgesang

"Fisches Nachtgesang" (Fish's Night Song) from the "Galgenlieder" (Gallows Songs, 1905) by Christian Morgenstern consists only of metrical symbols for stressed and unstressed syllables. It is considered a forerunner of visual poetry, which the Vienna Group built upon.
