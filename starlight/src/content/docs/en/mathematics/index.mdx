At technical college, mathematics is the tool used to describe and solve technical problems, from converting between number systems and calculating AC circuits with complex numbers to optimising with differential calculus. The subject **Applied Mathematics** therefore focuses less on proofs and more on modelling, calculating and interpreting.

The pages mainly follow the curriculum of the first three years: the basics of algebra and geometry are laid in the first year, the most important types of functions, complex numbers and vectors follow in the second year, and calculus with differentiation and integration in the third year.

## Notation

These pages use the notation common in Austria and in engineering:

- The imaginary unit of complex numbers is written as $j$ instead of $i$, as is usual in electrical engineering, because $i$ already stands for electric current.
- Intervals are written with brackets that face outwards for excluded endpoints: $]a; b[$ is an open interval, which is often written as $(a, b)$ in English-speaking countries.
- In the German version, a decimal comma is used ($3{,}14$). The English pages use a decimal point ($3.14$).

:::tip[Using technology]
Calculators and programs such as GeoGebra may be used in the final exams. Still, it pays to understand the methods: only if you know what the calculator does can you interpret the results and spot mistakes.
:::
