Not every task is a project. Backing up the server every Friday is routine. Introducing a new backup system, on the other hand, is a project. To decide whether the effort of project management is worthwhile, you need to know what characterises a project.

## What is a project?

A **project** is a temporary, one-time undertaking that is meant to reach a specific goal with limited resources and needs its own organisation to do so. Typical characteristics are:

- **Uniqueness:** The undertaking is carried out in this form only once.
- **Limited duration:** There is a defined start and end date.
- **Goal orientation:** The project is supposed to deliver a clearly defined result.
- **Limited resources:** Staff, budget and equipment are not available without limit.
- **Complexity and novelty:** The task is extensive, and there is usually no ready-made solution.
- **Risk:** Because the undertaking is new, a lot can go wrong.
- **Own organisation:** A team with its own roles is formed for the project and works across the normal company structure.
- **Interdisciplinary cooperation:** Several departments or fields work together.

:::tip[Examples from school]
The diploma thesis, organising the open day or developing an app for a client are projects. Installing updates every week or creating new user accounts at the start of the school year are routine tasks.
:::

## Distinctions

| Term               | Description                                                                                         | Example                                          |
| ------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Routine task       | recurring task within the normal organisation, usually with fixed procedures                        | monthly payroll, daily backup                    |
| Small project      | one-time undertaking of limited scope for which simplified project management is enough             | new printer for a department                     |
| Project            | one-time, complex undertaking with its own project organisation                                     | introduction of a new ERP system                 |
| Programme          | several related projects that together pursue a higher-level goal                                  | digitalisation of all administrative processes   |
| Project portfolio  | all projects and programmes of a company, which are steered and prioritised together               | all running IT projects of a company             |

Ultimately, the company decides whether an undertaking is treated as a project. Many companies define thresholds for this, for example a certain duration, a certain budget or a minimum number of departments involved.

## Types of projects

Projects can be classified by different criteria:

- **By content:** investment projects (such as a new data centre), research and development projects, organisational projects (new processes, restructuring) and IT projects (software development, system rollout).
- **By client:** internal projects, where the company itself is the client, and external projects for customers.
- **By size:** small, medium and large projects, depending on duration, budget and the number of people involved.
- **By novelty:** pioneering projects that create something completely new, and repeat projects that can draw on experience from similar projects.

## What is project management?

**Project management** covers all the leadership tasks, forms of organisation, methods and tools needed to start, plan, control and close a project. It is not only about plans and spreadsheets, but also about people: a project only succeeds if the team works well together and the people involved are informed and included.

### The project management process

In Austria, project management is often described as a process with several sub-processes, for example in the approach of Projekt Management Austria (pma):

1. **Project assignment:** The undertaking is assessed, and the client issues the project order.
2. **Project start:** Objectives, context, organisation and plans are worked out, and the team comes together.
3. **Project coordination:** Ongoing coordination within the team, allocation of tasks and information throughout the whole project.
4. **Project controlling:** Regular comparison of plan and actual values, corrective measures and adjustment of the plans.
5. **Project closing:** Handover of the results, dissolution of the project organisation, lessons learned.

If a crisis or an unexpected opportunity arises during the project, this is called a **discontinuity**. It is handled in a separate process (see [crises and opportunities](/en/project_management/controlling/discontinuities/)).

### Phases and process

The **project phases** describe the content-related sequence of the project, for example analysis, design, implementation, testing and rollout in a software project. The **project management process**, by contrast, describes the management tasks that run in parallel. Project controlling, for example, takes place in every phase.

## Project management standards

Because project management is needed in every industry, there are several recognised standards and certifications:

| Standard           | Publisher                                        | Focus                                                         |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| IPMA ICB           | International Project Management Association     | competences of project managers                               |
| PMBOK Guide        | Project Management Institute (PMI)               | principles and performance domains of project management      |
| PRINCE2            | PeopleCert (originally the UK government)        | process-oriented method with clear roles and stages           |
| DIN 69901          | German Institute for Standardization             | terms, processes and data in project management               |
| ISO 21502          | International Organization for Standardization   | international guidance on project management                  |
| Scrum Guide        | Ken Schwaber and Jeff Sutherland                 | agile framework for product development                       |

In Austria, certification according to IPMA, offered by Projekt Management Austria (pma), is particularly widespread.

## Why projects fail

Studies repeatedly show that many projects miss their goals. Common causes are:

- unclear or constantly changing goals and requirements
- overly optimistic estimates of effort and duration
- lack of support from management
- poor communication between client, team and users
- too few or unsuitable staff
- missing risk management and reacting to problems too late
