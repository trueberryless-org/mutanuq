Without clear objectives, nobody can say whether a project was successful. Objectives give the team a direction, are the basis for planning and are used for acceptance at the end.

## SMART objectives

A well-formulated objective meets the **SMART** criteria:

| Letter | Meaning                      | Question                                                   |
| ------ | ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| S      | specific                     | Is the objective described clearly and concretely?         |
| M      | measurable                   | How can you tell that the objective has been reached?      |
| A      | accepted or achievable       | Do the client and the team stand behind the objective?     |
| R      | realistic                    | Can the objective be reached with the available means?     |
| T      | time-bound                   | By when should the objective be reached?                   |

Objectives describe a **state** to be reached, not the way there. "We program an app" is an activity. "By 31 March, customers can book appointments via an app" is an objective.

### Examples

| poorly formulated                | SMART                                                                                                          |
| -------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The website should become faster. | By 30 June, the home page loads in under 2 seconds on a smartphone with 4G (measured with Lighthouse).         |
| Fewer support requests.          | By the end of the year, the number of password reset tickets falls by 50 % compared to the previous year.        |
| A secure network.                | By the end of the semester, all servers can only be reached via VPN, and the penetration test shows no critical vulnerabilities. |

## Types of objectives

- **Main objectives** are the central objectives for which the project is carried out.
- **Additional objectives** are desirable but not decisive for the success of the project, for example an additional language version.
- **Non-objectives** explicitly state what is not to be achieved. They are just as important as the objectives because they avoid misunderstandings and limit the scope.

A further distinction:

- **Result objectives** describe the project result itself: scope and quality, deadlines and costs (see [magic triangle](/en/project_management/basics/magic_triangle/)).
- **Process objectives** relate to the way the project is carried out, for example "The team learns to work with Docker" or "Cooperation with the new supplier is tried out".

## Relationships between objectives

Objectives can influence each other:

- **Complementary objectives** support each other. Automated tests improve quality and reduce maintenance costs later.
- **Competing objectives** hinder each other. More features increase the benefit, but also the cost and duration.
- **Neutral objectives** have no influence on each other.

Competing objectives must be prioritised. The decision is made by the client, not by the project team.

## Objective hierarchy

Project objectives are derived from higher-level objectives and are themselves broken down into sub-objectives:

1. **Company objective:** Customer satisfaction increases.
2. **Project objective:** Customers can submit requests around the clock via an online portal.
3. **Sub-objectives:** The portal is connected to the ticket system, requests are answered within 24 hours, and the portal is accessible.

:::tip
Formulate objectives together with the client and record them in writing in the project order. At the end of the project, exactly these objectives are checked during acceptance.
:::
