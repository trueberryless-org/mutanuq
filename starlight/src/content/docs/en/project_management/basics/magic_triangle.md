The magic triangle describes three factors in project management that always depend on each other. If one factor changes, one or both of the other factors must change as well.

[![Magic triangle](/images/project_management/magic_triangle.jpg)](https://vollcomdigital.medium.com/what-is-the-magic-triangle-in-project-management-db1486b05f85)

## The three factors

- **Scope** (performance and quality): _What_ should be delivered and in what quality? This includes all functions and requirements defined in the requirements specification.
- **Time** (deadlines): _When_ must the project or individual milestones be finished?
- **Cost** (budget and resources): _How much_ may the project cost? Besides money, this also includes staff and material.

The triangle is called "magic" because the three goals influence each other and can never all be fulfilled to the maximum at the same time. A project that is supposed to be implemented very quickly, very cheaply and in the highest quality is hardly possible in practice. This is often summed up with the saying "fast, cheap, good: pick two".

## Examples

> If the quality of the product is to be increased, more time must be spent or additional employees must be hired, which in turn costs more.

> If too many employees quit, the customer has to be told that either the required quality cannot be achieved or the development of the product will take more time.

In most cases, the best solution is probably a roughly equal change of the other two aspects. For the second example, this would mean the following:

> Due to the missing capacity, the product is developed both later and at a lower quality.

## Meaning of the different areas

[![Meaning](/images/project_management/magic_triangle_context.png)](https://qosmic.tech/rocketsurgery/project-management-triangle)

Depending on which factor is most important for a project, different priorities arise:

- **Time is fixed:** For a trade fair appearance or a legally required deadline, the date must not be postponed. If necessary, features have to be dropped or additional resources used.
- **Cost is fixed:** With a fixed budget, for example a funded project, you have to make compromises on scope or deadlines.
- **Scope is fixed:** With safety-critical software, for example in medical technology, quality must not be compromised. Here, delays or higher costs are more likely to be accepted.

In agile software development (for example with Scrum), time (fixed sprint length) and cost (fixed team) are usually deliberately fixed, while the scope remains flexible: what brings the greatest benefit is implemented first.

## Extension: devil's square

Harry Sneed extended the model to the **devil's square**, which contains four variables: **quality**, **quantity** (scope), **time** and **cost**. The area of the square represents the constant productivity of the team. If you pull on one corner, i.e. improve one factor, at least one other corner has to give way, as long as productivity does not change.

Monitoring whether these goals are met is a central task of [project controlling](/en/project_management/controlling/project_controlling/).
