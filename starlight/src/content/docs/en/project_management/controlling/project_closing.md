Many projects do not end with a clear closing but simply "fizzle out": the team turns to new tasks, remaining work is left undone and nobody feels responsible any more. A deliberate project closing prevents this and secures the experience for future projects.

## Closing the content

- **Acceptance:** The client checks whether the agreed objectives and requirements have been met. The basis is the project order and the functional specification. The result is recorded in the **acceptance certificate**, which also lists open defects and deadlines for fixing them.
- **Handover:** The result is handed over to the users or to operations, for example with operating documentation, access data and training.
- **Remaining work:** Open items are listed and assigned to responsible people, even beyond the end of the project.
- **Post-project phase:** Maintenance, support and further development are arranged.

With acceptance, responsibility usually passes to the client, and for external projects the warranty period begins (see [warranty and damages](/en/economy_and_law/law/warranty_and_damages/)).

## Closing with the people involved

- **Feedback:** Team members give each other and the project manager feedback on the collaboration.
- **Recognition:** The team's achievements are acknowledged, for example by the client.
- **Closing celebration:** A shared ending helps to close the project emotionally.
- **Return:** The next assignment of the team members is clarified, especially in a pure project organisation.

## Organisational closing

- complete and archive the project documentation
- settle costs and close project accounts
- end contracts with suppliers, revoke access and permissions
- dissolve the project organisation and formally discharge the project manager

## Lessons learned

In a **lessons learned workshop**, the team reflects on the project. Possible guiding questions:

- What went well and should be kept?
- What went badly, and what would we do differently next time?
- Which estimates were off, and why?
- Which risks occurred, and which were unnecessary?

The results are documented so that future projects can access them, for example in a knowledge base.

## Project closing report

The **project closing report** summarises the project for the client:

1. summary of the project and its results
2. achievement of objectives: scope, schedule and costs compared with the plan
3. significant deviations and their reasons
4. open items and recommendations for the post-project phase
5. lessons learned
6. assessment of the collaboration

## Project evaluation

The project evaluation assesses the success of the project. There are two questions:

- **Was the right result achieved?** Were the objectives met, is the client satisfied, is the result used, does the planned benefit materialise?
- **Was the project managed well?** Were the schedule and budget kept, did the collaboration work, were risks recognised in time?

| Criterion                       | Rating (1 to 5) | Reason                                                   |
| ------------------------------- | --------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Achievement of scope objectives | 4               | all main objectives met, one additional objective postponed |
| Keeping to the schedule         | 3               | go-live two weeks later than planned                     |
| Keeping to the budget           | 5               | 3 % under budget                                         |
| Client satisfaction             | 4               | according to the closing meeting                         |
| Collaboration in the team       | 4               | conflict in the storming stage resolved well             |

Whether a project has also paid off economically can often only be judged months or years later. The methods of [investment appraisal](/en/project_management/controlling/investment_analysis/) are used for this.
