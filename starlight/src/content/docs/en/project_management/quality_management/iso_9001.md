**ISO 9001** is the most widely used standard for quality management systems in the world. It defines which requirements a QMS must meet, but does not prescribe how a company implements them. This is why it can be applied in any industry, from a software company to a bakery.

## The ISO 9000 family

| Standard  | Content                                                                  |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| ISO 9000  | fundamentals and vocabulary of quality management                        |
| ISO 9001  | requirements for a QMS; certification is based on this standard          |
| ISO 9004  | guidance for achieving sustained success of an organisation              |
| ISO 19011 | guidelines for auditing management systems                               |

Related standards use the same basic structure, for example ISO/IEC 27001 for information security, ISO 14001 for environmental management and ISO/IEC 20000 for IT service management. This makes it possible to combine several management systems into one integrated system.

## The seven principles

ISO 9000 describes seven quality management principles:

1. **Customer focus:** Meet customers' requirements and strive to exceed their expectations.
2. **Leadership:** Leaders create unity of purpose and an environment in which everyone is committed to quality.
3. **Engagement of people:** Competent, empowered and engaged people at all levels.
4. **Process approach:** Activities are understood and managed as interrelated processes.
5. **Improvement:** Successful organisations improve continuously.
6. **Evidence-based decision making:** Decisions are based on the analysis of data.
7. **Relationship management:** Relationships with suppliers and partners are managed.

## Process approach

A company is viewed as a network of processes. The **process map** shows the most important processes and how they relate:

- **Management processes** steer the company, such as strategy, target planning and management review.
- **Core processes** create value directly for customers, such as sales, software development and support.
- **Support processes** enable the core processes, such as human resources, purchasing and IT operations.

For each process, the inputs, outputs, responsible people, required resources and key figures are defined.

## PDCA cycle

ISO 9001 is based on the **PDCA cycle** (Deming cycle), a model for continuous improvement:

![PDCA cycle](/images/project_management/pdca_en.svg)

- **Plan:** set objectives, analyse the current state, plan measures
- **Do:** implement the measures, often on a small scale at first
- **Check:** measure the results and compare them with the objectives
- **Act:** adopt successful measures as the standard or start a new cycle if there are deviations

## Structure of ISO 9001

The standard follows a uniform basic structure with ten clauses. The requirements are in clauses 4 to 10:

| Clause | Topic                                   | Examples of requirements                                         |
| ------ | --------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 4      | Context of the organisation             | internal and external issues, interested parties, scope          |
| 5      | Leadership                              | quality policy, roles and responsibilities                        |
| 6      | Planning                                | risks and opportunities, quality objectives                       |
| 7      | Support                                 | resources, competence, awareness, communication, documented information |
| 8      | Operation                               | product requirements, development, suppliers, production         |
| 9      | Performance evaluation                  | customer satisfaction, internal audits, management review        |
| 10     | Improvement                             | nonconformities, corrective action, continual improvement         |

Clauses 4 to 10 can be mapped to the PDCA cycle: planning (4 to 6), doing (7 and 8), checking (9) and acting (10). An important innovation of the 2015 edition is **risk-based thinking**: risks and opportunities must be considered systematically.

## Quality manual

In the past, ISO 9001 required a **quality manual** describing the QMS. Since the 2015 edition, the standard only speaks of "documented information". Many companies still keep a quality manual because it provides a good overview. It typically contains:

- quality policy and quality objectives
- scope of the QMS
- organisation chart and responsibilities
- process map and process descriptions
- references to work instructions, forms and checklists
- rules for document control, audits and improvements

Today, the quality manual is usually no longer a paper folder but kept on an intranet or wiki.

## Audits and certification

An **audit** is a systematic check of whether the defined requirements are met.

- **Internal audits** are carried out by the company itself to find weaknesses.
- **Supplier audits** check whether a supplier meets the requirements.
- **Certification audits** are carried out by an independent, accredited certification body.

The certification process:

1. The company introduces a QMS and operates it for a few months.
2. In the **initial certification audit**, the certification body checks the documentation and the actual practice.
3. If no major nonconformities are found, the certificate is issued for three years.
4. Annual **surveillance audits** check whether the QMS is still effective.
5. After three years, a **recertification audit** follows.

A certificate is often a prerequisite for receiving orders from large customers or public authorities.
