Quality does not happen by chance, and it cannot be "tested in" at the end. **Quality management** covers all activities with which a company ensures that its products and services reliably meet the requirements of its customers.

## Basic terms

- According to ISO 9000, **quality** is the degree to which the characteristics of a product or service fulfil the requirements. Quality therefore does not mean "as luxurious as possible", but "fit for the requirements".
- **Quality assurance** covers measures that create confidence that the requirements will be met, such as inspections, tests and reviews.
- **Quality management** is broader: it plans, controls, assures and improves quality throughout the whole company.
- A **quality management system** (QMS) is the entirety of rules, processes, responsibilities and documents with which a company practises quality management.

## Cost of quality

Quality costs money, but poor quality usually costs even more. A distinction is made between:

- **prevention costs:** training, planning, code reviews, quality management
- **appraisal costs:** tests, acceptance, measurements, audits
- **failure costs:** rework, recalls, warranty claims, outages, damage to reputation

The **rule of ten** states that the cost of fixing a defect increases roughly tenfold with each phase in which it remains undetected. A defect in the requirements that is found in a review costs little. The same defect, discovered only at the customer during operation, can cost many times as much.

## Total quality management

**Total quality management** (TQM) is a company philosophy in which quality is the task of every employee and not just of a quality department. Important principles are:

- focus on customers, including internal customers such as the next department
- involvement of all employees, with leaders as role models
- continuous improvement in small steps (_kaizen_, continuous improvement process)
- decisions based on data and facts

A well-known model for TQM is the **EFQM model** of the European Foundation for Quality Management. Companies can use it to assess themselves and to apply for quality awards, in Austria for example the national business excellence award.
